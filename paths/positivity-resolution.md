This chapter gives the positivity theory of line bundles that the volume branch of the proof assumes: ample, nef and big line bundles, metrics and their curvature, Chern-Weil forms, resolution of singularities and principalization, the vanishing theorems of Kodaira, Kawamata-Viehweg and Nadel with multiplier ideals, the $L^2$ estimates of Hörmander and Ohsawa-Takegoshi, and the Angehrn-Siu theorem. [The Weil bound](/proof/weil-bound/) takes `ResolutionCharZero`, `PrincipalisationCharZero` and `NefRR` as inputs. The bundle of [published arithmetic results](/step/published-results-arithmetic/) contains `KawamataViehweg`, `NadelVanishingAndNonKltDivisor`, `L2ExtensionLogCanonical`, `GoodChernWeil`, `ArithHilbertSamuel` and `Principalisation`, and [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/) uses the point separation theorem `point_separation_pair_of_literature`. The [geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/) gives schemes, Cartier divisors, Dolbeault cohomology and short statements of some of these theorems. Here we give the precise forms, the short proofs, and the methods. The [Arakelov chapter](/learn/arakelov/) gives Hermitian line bundles and the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula.

Conventions. A variety is integral and of finite type over a field. Unless we say otherwise, the field is $\mathbb{C}$, and $n = \dim X$. We write divisors and line bundles additively, so $K_X + L$ means $\omega_X \otimes L$. On a complex manifold, $d^c = \frac{1}{4\pi i}(\partial - \bar\partial)$, so $dd^c = \frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar\partial$, as in the Arakelov chapter.

## Ample, nef and big line bundles

Let $X$ be a projective variety. A *$\mathbb{Q}$-divisor* is a finite sum $D = \sum a_i D_i$ of prime divisors with $a_i \in \mathbb{Q}$; it is *$\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier* if $mD$ is a [[Cartier divisor|FHV05ZK99F]] for some integer $m > 0$. For a $k$-dimensional subvariety $V \subseteq X$ and $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisors $D_1, \dots, D_k$, the intersection number $(D_1 \cdots D_k \cdot V) \in \mathbb{Q}$ is multilinear and symmetric, and $(D^k \cdot V)$ is the degree of $V$ when $D$ is a hyperplane section.

**Definition.** Two $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisors $D, D'$ are *numerically equivalent*, $D \equiv D'$, if $(D \cdot C) = (D' \cdot C)$ for each irreducible curve $C \subseteq X$. The quotient $N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}$ of the real Cartier divisors by $\equiv$ is a finite-dimensional real vector space (the theorem of the base).

**Definition.** A line bundle $L$ is *[[ample|RRK8P6TXG2]]* if $L^{\otimes m}$ is very ample for some $m > 0$, that is, its sections give a closed immersion into a projective space. A $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D$ is ample if $mD$ is an ample Cartier divisor for some $m > 0$.

**Theorem (Cartan, Serre, Grothendieck).** Let $X$ be a projective scheme over a field and $L$ a line bundle on $X$. The following are equivalent.

1. $L$ is ample.
2. For each coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ there is $m_0$ with $H^i(X, \mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m}) = 0$ for all $i > 0$ and $m \geq m_0$.
3. For each coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$, the sheaf $\mathcal{F} \otimes L^{\otimes m}$ is generated by global sections for all large $m$.

See Hartshorne, *Algebraic Geometry*, II.7 and III.5, and Lazarsfeld, *Positivity I*, Section 1.2. Condition 2 is *Serre vanishing*. Below we use it to extend sections from a subvariety: if $Z \subseteq X$ is closed with ideal $\mathcal{I}_Z$, then $H^1(X, \mathcal{I}_Z \otimes L^{\otimes m}) = 0$ for large $m$, so $H^0(X, L^{\otimes m}) \to H^0(Z, L^{\otimes m}|_Z)$ is onto.

**Theorem (Nakai-Moishezon criterion).** A Cartier divisor $D$ on a projective scheme $X$ is ample if and only if $(D^{\dim V} \cdot V) > 0$ for each irreducible closed subvariety $V \subseteq X$ of positive dimension (Lazarsfeld, *Positivity I*, Section 1.2).

**Example.** On a smooth projective curve, $D$ is ample if and only if $\deg D > 0$. On $\mathbb{P}^n$, $\mathcal{O}(a)$ is ample if and only if $a > 0$.

**Definition.** A $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D$ is *[[nef|RZ66X0NAM5]]* if $(D \cdot C) \geq 0$ for each irreducible curve $C \subseteq X$. Nefness depends only on the class of $D$ in $N^1(X)_{\mathbb{R}}$. The nef classes form a closed convex cone $\mathrm{Nef}(X)$, and the ample classes form a convex cone $\mathrm{Amp}(X)$.

**Theorem (Kleiman).** Let $X$ be a projective variety. If $D$ is nef, then $(D^k \cdot V) \geq 0$ for each subvariety $V$ of dimension $k$. The nef cone is the closure of the ample cone, and the ample cone is the interior of the nef cone (Lazarsfeld, *Positivity I*, Section 1.4).

**Proposition.** Let $f : Y \to X$ be a morphism of projective varieties. If $D$ is nef on $X$, then $f^*D$ is nef on $Y$. If $A$ is ample and $N$ is nef on $X$, then $A + N$ is ample.

*Proof.* For a curve $C \subseteq Y$, the projection formula gives $(f^*D \cdot C) = (D \cdot f_*C)$, and $f_*C$ is $0$ or a positive multiple of a curve, so the number is $\geq 0$. For the second claim, let $V$ have dimension $d > 0$. Then $((A+N)^d \cdot V) = \sum_{i=0}^{d} \binom{d}{i} (A^{d-i} N^i \cdot V)$. Each term is $\geq 0$, since an intersection number of nef classes on a subvariety is $\geq 0$ (Lazarsfeld, *Positivity I*, Section 1.4), and the term $i = 0$ is $(A^d \cdot V) > 0$. The Nakai-Moishezon criterion ends the proof. $\square$

Both facts appear as `nef_pullback_is_nef` and `ample_add_nef_is_ample` in the vanishing theorem on a log resolution of the development volumes. The pullback of an ample divisor by a birational morphism is nef but in general not ample. The notion that survives is bigness.

**Definition (big divisor and volume).** Let $X$ be a projective variety of dimension $n$ and $L$ a line bundle. The *volume* of $L$ is
$$\operatorname{vol}(L) = \limsup_{m \to \infty} \frac{h^0(X, L^{\otimes m})}{m^n / n!}.$$
$L$ is *big* if $\operatorname{vol}(L) > 0$. A $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D$ is big if $mD$ is big for some $m > 0$; then $\operatorname{vol}(mD) = m^n \operatorname{vol}(D)$.

**Lemma (Kodaira's lemma).** Let $D$ be a big Cartier divisor on a projective variety $X$ and $A$ an effective Cartier divisor. Then $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(mD - A)) \neq 0$ for some $m > 0$. In particular, if $A$ is ample, then $mD \sim A + E$ with $E$ effective.

*Proof.* The exact sequence $0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD - A) \to \mathcal{O}_X(mD) \to \mathcal{O}_A(mD) \to 0$ gives $h^0(mD - A) \geq h^0(mD) - h^0(A, mD|_A)$. Since $\dim A = n - 1$, $h^0(A, mD|_A) \leq C m^{n-1}$ for a constant $C$. Since $D$ is big, $h^0(mD) \geq c\, m^n$ for some $c > 0$ and infinitely many $m$. For such $m$ large, the difference is positive. $\square$

**Corollary.** A $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier divisor $D$ is big if and only if $D \equiv A + E$ with $A$ an ample $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $E$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor. If $D$ is nef, then $\operatorname{vol}(D) = (D^n)$, so $D$ is big if and only if $(D^n) > 0$.

The second statement follows from asymptotic Riemann-Roch for nef divisors, which is stated in the [geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/) and is the input `NefRR` of [the Weil bound](/proof/weil-bound/).

**Example.** Let $\pi : Y \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up of a point, with exceptional curve $E$, and let $H = \pi^*\mathcal{O}(1)$. Then $(H^2) = 1$, $(H \cdot E) = 0$ and $(E^2) = -1$. So $H$ is nef and big, but not ample, since it is zero on $E$. For rational $0 < \varepsilon < 1$, $H - \varepsilon E$ is ample, and $H = (H - \varepsilon E) + \varepsilon E$ is the decomposition of Kodaira's lemma.

If $\mu : Y \to X$ is birational and $D$ is nef and big on $X$, then $\mu^*D$ is nef and big, with $(\mu^*D)^n = (D^n)$. This is why the vanishing theorems below ask for "nef and big" and not for "ample": the hypothesis survives the passage to a resolution.

## Metrics, curvature and positivity

Let $X$ be a complex manifold and $L$ a holomorphic line bundle with a [[Hermitian metric|Q6X6W2N29G]] $h$. In a local holomorphic frame $e$ on $U$ write $|e|_h^2 = e^{-\varphi}$; the function $\varphi$ is the *weight* of $h$ in this frame, and $|s|_h^2 = |f|^2 e^{-\varphi}$ for $s = fe$. If $e' = ge$ is another frame, $\varphi' = \varphi - \log|g|^2$. For a Hermitian metric and Chern form as in the Arakelov chapter, $c_1(L, h) = -dd^c \log|e|_h^2 = dd^c \varphi$.

**Definition.** A real $(1,1)$-form $\alpha = i \sum_{j,k} \alpha_{j\bar k}\, dz_j \wedge d\bar z_k$ is *positive* at $x$ if the Hermitian matrix $(\alpha_{j\bar k}(x))$ is positive definite, and *semipositive* if it is positive semidefinite. A metric $h$ on $L$ is *positive* (*semipositive*) if $c_1(L, h)$ is positive (semipositive) at each point. A *Kähler form* is a closed positive $(1,1)$-form.

**Example.** On $\mathbb{P}^n$, the Fubini-Study metric on $\mathcal{O}(1)$ has weight $\varphi = \log(|z_0|^2 + \dots + |z_n|^2) - \log|z_0|^2$ in the frame $z_0$ on $\{z_0 \neq 0\}$. Its Chern form is the Fubini-Study Kähler form $\omega_{FS}$, which is positive, and $\int_{\mathbb{P}^n} \omega_{FS}^n = 1$. The dual metric on $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ has Chern form $-\omega_{FS}$.

**Theorem (Kodaira embedding theorem).** Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold and $L$ a holomorphic line bundle. Then $L$ has a positive metric if and only if $X$ is projective and $L$ is ample (Griffiths and Harris, *Principles of Algebraic Geometry*, Chapter 1, the section on the Kodaira embedding theorem).

One direction is short: if $L^{\otimes m}$ is very ample, the $m$-th root of the pulled back Fubini-Study metric is positive. On a projective manifold, $L$ is nef if and only if for each $\varepsilon > 0$ it has a smooth metric with $c_1(L, h_\varepsilon) \geq -\varepsilon \omega$ for a fixed Kähler form $\omega$ (Demailly, *Analytic Methods in Algebraic Geometry*, the chapter on numerically effective and pseudo-effective line bundles). A nef line bundle need not have a semipositive smooth metric.

The vanishing theorems for $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors need metrics with singularities.

**Definition (plurisubharmonic function).** A function $\varphi : U \to [-\infty, \infty)$ on an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ is *[[plurisubharmonic|FF2P7ER3QT]]* (psh) if it is upper semicontinuous, not identically $-\infty$ on any component, and its restriction to each complex line is subharmonic. A $C^2$ function is psh if and only if its Levi matrix $(\partial^2\varphi / \partial z_j \partial \bar z_k)$ is positive semidefinite at each point. It is *strictly psh* if the Levi matrix is positive definite.

**Definition.** A *singular Hermitian metric* on $L$ is given by weights $\varphi$ in local frames that are locally integrable and satisfy $\varphi' = \varphi - \log|g|^2$ under change of frame. Its *curvature current* is $dd^c \varphi$, a closed real $(1,1)$-current. The metric is *semipositive* if the weights are psh, so that $dd^c\varphi$ is a positive current.

**Example.** Let $D = \sum_j c_j \operatorname{div}(g_j)$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $U$, with $c_j \in \mathbb{Q}_{> 0}$ and $g_j$ holomorphic. Then $\varphi_D = \sum_j c_j \log|g_j|^2$ is psh, and by the [[Poincaré-Lelong formula|B2YBWQQMHA]], $dd^c \varphi_D = [D]$, the current of integration over $D$. If $L \equiv D$ globally with $D$ effective, these local weights give a singular metric on $L$ whose curvature current is $[D]$.

**Definition.** The *Lelong number* of a psh function $\varphi$ at $x$ is
$$\nu(\varphi, x) = \liminf_{z \to x} \frac{\varphi(z)}{\log|z - x|^2}.$$

For $\varphi_D$ as above, $\nu(\varphi_D, x) = \operatorname{mult}_x D$, the multiplicity of $D$ at $x$. Lelong numbers do not depend on the chart, and they measure how singular the metric is at a point (Demailly, *Complex Analytic and Differential Geometry*, Chapter III).

## Chern-Weil forms

Let $E$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank $r$ on a complex manifold $X$, with a Hermitian metric $h$.

**Definition.** The *Chern connection* of $(E, h)$ is the unique connection $D : \mathcal{A}^0(E) \to \mathcal{A}^1(E)$ that is compatible with $h$, that is $d\, h(s, t) = h(Ds, t) + h(s, Dt)$, and whose $(0,1)$-part is $\bar\partial_E$. Its *curvature* is $\Theta_h = D^2$, a $(1,1)$-form with values in $\operatorname{End} E$. If $H$ is the Gram matrix of $h$ in a holomorphic frame, the connection matrix is $\theta = \partial H \cdot H^{-1}$ and $\Theta = \bar\partial \theta$, in the conventions of Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 0.

For a line bundle, $H = |e|^2 = e^{-\varphi}$, so $\Theta = \bar\partial\partial \log|e|^2 = \partial\bar\partial\varphi$ and $\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta = dd^c\varphi = c_1(L, h)$.

**Definition.** The *Chern forms* $c_k(E, h)$ are the forms of degree $(k, k)$ in
$$\det\Big(1 + \frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_h\Big) = 1 + c_1(E, h) + \dots + c_r(E, h).$$
More generally, for a polynomial $P$ on $\mathfrak{gl}_r(\mathbb{C})$ that is invariant under conjugation, $P(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_h)$ is a well defined form, since a change of frame conjugates $\Theta_h$.

**Theorem (Chern-Weil).** Let $P$ be an invariant polynomial.

1. The form $P(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_h)$ is closed.
2. If $h'$ is another metric on $E$, then $P(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_{h'}) - P(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Theta_h) = dd^c \beta$ for a smooth form $\beta$, the *Bott-Chern form* of $(h, h')$.
3. The class of $c_k(E, h)$ in $H^{2k}(X, \mathbb{R})$ is the topological [[Chern class|PAD37GKDPD]] $c_k(E)$.

See Bott and Chern, *Hermitian vector bundles and the equidistribution of the zeroes of their holomorphic sections* (Acta Math. 114, 1965), Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 3, and Demailly, *Complex Analytic and Differential Geometry*, Chapter V.

*Proof for line bundles.* Locally $c_1(L, h) = dd^c\varphi$, which is closed. Any other metric is $h' = h e^{-u}$ with $u$ a smooth function on $X$, so $\varphi' = \varphi + u$ and $c_1(L, h') - c_1(L, h) = dd^c u$. $\square$

**Theorem (Chern numbers as integrals).** Let $X$ be a compact complex manifold of dimension $n$, $(E, h)$ a Hermitian vector bundle, and $P$ a polynomial in $c_1, \dots, c_r$ of weighted degree $n$ (with $c_k$ of weight $k$). Then
$$\int_X P(c_1(E, h), \dots, c_r(E, h)) = \langle P(c_1(E), \dots, c_r(E)), [X] \rangle.$$
If $X$ is the complex manifold of a smooth projective variety, the right side is the intersection number $\int_X P(c(E)) \cap [X]$ of Fulton, *Intersection Theory*, Chapter 3.

This follows from part 3 of the Chern-Weil theorem and Stokes. The development states the projective case as `ChernWeilNumbers`.

**Example.** Let $T = T\mathbb{P}^n$. The Euler sequence gives $c(T) = (1 + \xi)^{n+1}$ with $\xi = c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))$, so $\int_{\mathbb{P}^n} c_n(T) = n + 1$, the Euler characteristic of $\mathbb{P}^n$. With the Fubini-Study metric the integrand is an explicit invariant form, and the integral of $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1), FS)^n = \omega_{FS}^n$ is $1$.

**Proposition (splitting principle and flag push-forward).** Let $f : \mathrm{Fl}(E) \to X$ be the bundle of full flags in $E$. Then $f^* : H^*(X) \to H^*(\mathrm{Fl}(E))$ is injective, and $f^*E$ has a filtration $0 = F_0 \subset F_1 \subset \dots \subset F_r = f^*E$ whose quotients $F_i / F_{i-1}$ are line bundles with first Chern classes $x_1, \dots, x_r$. Then $f^* c(E) = \prod_i (1 + x_i)$, so $c_k(E)$ pulls back to the $k$-th elementary symmetric function of the $x_i$ (Fulton, *Intersection Theory*, Section 3.2). The full flag bundle is an iterated projective bundle, so its cohomology follows from the projective bundle formula of the [geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/). The integral of a polynomial in the $x_i$ over $\mathrm{Fl}(E)$ is computed by a symmetrizing operator (divided differences); this is the statement `FlagPushForward` in `GoodChernWeil`.

On a Shimura variety, the natural metrics on automorphic vector bundles are not smooth at the boundary of a toroidal compactification. Mumford's notion of a good metric controls this.

**Definition.** Let $\bar X$ be a compact complex manifold, $B \subset \bar X$ a divisor with simple normal crossings, and $X = \bar X \setminus B$. Near a point of $B$ take a polydisc chart $\Delta^n$ in which $X$ is $(\Delta^*)^k \times \Delta^{n-k}$. The *Poincaré metric* on this chart is
$$\omega_P = \sum_{j \leq k} \frac{i\, dz_j \wedge d\bar z_j}{|z_j|^2 (\log|z_j|^2)^2} + \sum_{j > k} i\, dz_j \wedge d\bar z_j.$$
A smooth form $\eta$ on $X$ has *Poincaré growth* if, on each such chart, $|\eta(t_1, \dots, t_p)|^2 \leq C \prod_i \|t_i\|^2_{\omega_P}$ for tangent vectors $t_i$. Let $\bar E$ be a holomorphic vector bundle on $\bar X$ and $h$ a Hermitian metric on $E = \bar E|_X$. Then $h$ is *good* along $B$ if, for each chart and each holomorphic frame of $\bar E$ on it, the entries of the Gram matrix $H$ and $(\det H)^{-1}$ are bounded by $C \big(\sum_{j \leq k} |\log|z_j||\big)^{2N}$ for some $C, N$, and the entries of $\partial H \cdot H^{-1}$ and of the curvature have Poincaré growth.

**Theorem (Mumford).** Let $h$ be a good metric on $E = \bar E|_X$. Then each Chern form $c_k(E, h)$ is integrable on $\bar X$, the current it defines is closed, and its class in $H^{2k}(\bar X, \mathbb{R})$ is $c_k(\bar E)$. So $\int_X P(c(E, h)) = \langle P(c(\bar E)), [\bar X] \rangle$ for each $P$ of weighted degree $n$. The extension $\bar E$ is determined by $h$ (Mumford, *Hirzebruch's proportionality theorem in the non-compact case*, Invent. Math. 42, 1977, Section 1).

**Example.** Let $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ be torsion free of finite index, $Y = \Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}$ and $\bar Y$ its compactification by $c$ cusps. Give $\Omega^1_Y$ the metric $\|f(\tau)\, d\tau\|^2 = |f(\tau)|^2 y^2$, $y = \operatorname{Im}\tau$, the dual of the hyperbolic metric. At a cusp of width $1$, $q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$ is a coordinate, $dq/q = 2\pi i\, d\tau$ is a frame of $\Omega^1_{\bar Y}(\log \text{cusps})$, and $\|dq/q\|^2 = 4\pi^2 y^2 = (\log|q|)^2$. So the metric grows like a power of $|\log|q||$, and one checks that it is good. The Chern form is $\frac{1}{2\pi}$ times the hyperbolic area form, since the hyperbolic metric has curvature $-1$. Mumford's theorem gives $\frac{1}{2\pi}\operatorname{area}(Y) = \deg \Omega^1_{\bar Y}(\log \text{cusps}) = 2g - 2 + c$, the Gauss-Bonnet formula for $Y$.

The example is the case of dimension $1$ of *Hirzebruch-Mumford proportionality*: on a toroidal compactification of a Shimura variety, the Chern numbers of the automorphic bundles with their invariant metrics are the Chern numbers of the same bundles on the compact dual, times a volume. The development uses this as `GoodChernWeil`, one of the [published arithmetic results](/step/published-results-arithmetic/), for flag bundles over toroidal compactifications of unitary Shimura varieties.

## Resolution of singularities and principalization

In this section the field $k$ has characteristic $0$.

**Definition.** Let $X$ be a scheme of finite type over $k$ and $Z \subset X$ a closed subscheme with ideal $\mathcal{I}$. The blow-up of $X$ along $Z$ (see *[[blow-ups|4SFS82N88K]]*) is $\pi : \operatorname{Bl}_Z X = \operatorname{Proj} \bigoplus_{m \geq 0} \mathcal{I}^m \to X$. The ideal $\pi^{-1}\mathcal{I} \cdot \mathcal{O}$ is invertible; it defines the *exceptional divisor*. Each morphism $g : T \to X$ such that $g^{-1}\mathcal{I} \cdot \mathcal{O}_T$ is invertible factors uniquely through $\pi$ (Hartshorne, II.7).

**Proposition.** Let $X$ be smooth and $Z \subset X$ a smooth closed subvariety of codimension $c \geq 2$. Then $Y = \operatorname{Bl}_Z X$ is smooth, the exceptional divisor $E$ is the projective bundle $\mathbb{P}(N_{Z/X})$ over $Z$, and $K_Y = \pi^* K_X + (c - 1)E$ (Hartshorne, Exercise II.8.5).

**Definition.** Let $X$ be smooth. A divisor $\sum_i a_i D_i$ has *simple normal crossing* (snc) support if each $D_i$ is smooth and at each point there are local coordinates $z_1, \dots, z_n$ in which $\bigcup D_i$ is $z_1 \cdots z_p = 0$. For a proper birational morphism $\mu : Y \to X$ with $Y$ smooth, the *exceptional locus* $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ is the set where $\mu$ is not a local isomorphism, and the *relative canonical divisor* is $K_{Y/X} = K_Y - \mu^*K_X$. It is the divisor of the Jacobian determinant of $\mu$, so it is effective with support in $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$.

**Definition.** Let $X$ be smooth, $D$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ a nonzero ideal. A *log resolution* of $D$ is a proper birational $\mu : Y \to X$ with $Y$ smooth, $\operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ a divisor, and $\mu^*D + \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ of snc support. A log resolution of $\mathfrak{a}$ is such a $\mu$ with $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F)$ for an effective divisor $F$ with $F + \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ of snc support.

**Theorem ([[Resolution of singularities|MB52NDA6WN]], strong form).** Let $X$ be a reduced scheme of finite type over $k$, with smooth locus $U$. There is a finite sequence of blow-ups $X_m \to \dots \to X_0 = X$ in smooth centres that lie over $X \setminus U$, whose composite $\pi : X_m \to X$ has the following properties.

1. $X_m$ is smooth, $\pi$ is proper, $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $U$, and $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is dense in $X_m$.
2. The reduced preimage $\pi^{-1}(X \setminus U)_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a divisor with snc support.
3. The sequence is functorial: it commutes with smooth morphisms $X' \to X$ and with extensions of the base field, after we drop the blow-ups whose centres become empty.

See Hironaka, *Resolution of singularities of an algebraic variety over a field of characteristic zero I, II* (Ann. of Math. 79, 1964). The functorial form is due to Bierstone and Milman (Invent. Math. 128, 1997) and Włodarczyk (J. Amer. Math. Soc. 18, 2005); Kollár, *Lectures on Resolution of Singularities*, Chapter 3, gives a complete proof.

**Theorem (principalization).** Let $X$ be smooth over $k$, $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ an ideal that is nonzero on each component, and $B$ a divisor with snc support. There is a finite sequence of blow-ups in smooth centres, each with normal crossings with the total transform of $B$ and the exceptional divisors before it, such that the composite $\pi : X' \to X$ is an isomorphism outside the cosupport of $\mathfrak{a}$, and $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{O}_{X'} = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-F)$ with $F + \operatorname{Exc}(\pi) + \pi^{-1}_*B$ of snc support. The sequence is functorial for smooth morphisms (Hironaka, 1964; Kollár, *Lectures on Resolution of Singularities*, Chapter 3).

**Corollary.** On a smooth variety in characteristic $0$, each nonzero ideal and each $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor has a log resolution, which is a composite of blow-ups in smooth centres. If $X$ is projective, the log resolution can be taken projective.

*Proof.* For an ideal, this is principalization. For $D = \sum a_i D_i$, principalize the ideal $\prod_i \mathcal{O}_X(-D_i)$. The exceptional locus of a composite of blow-ups in smooth centres is the union of the exceptional divisors, so it has snc support together with $F$. Blow-ups of projective varieties are projective. $\square$

In Kollár's book, and in the development, resolution is deduced from principalization. One embeds $X$ locally in a smooth $M$ and principalizes the ideal of $X$; the strict transform of $X$ becomes smooth at the step where it is a component of a centre, and functoriality glues the local resolutions. The development also derives its arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula from principalization (`arithHilbertSamuel_of_principalisation`).

**Example.** Let $C = \{y^2 = x^3\} \subset \mathbb{A}^2$, the cusp. Blowing up the origin gives an exceptional curve $E_1$ along which $f = y^2 - x^3$ vanishes to order $2$, and the strict transform is smooth but tangent to $E_1$. Blowing up the point of tangency gives $E_2$, and then $E_1$, $E_2$ and the strict transform meet at one point. Blowing up that point gives $E_3$. On the resulting $\mu : Y \to \mathbb{A}^2$, the divisor $\mu^*C + \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$ has snc support, and
$$(\operatorname{ord}_{E_i} K_{Y/X}, \operatorname{ord}_{E_i} f) = (1, 2), (2, 3), (4, 6) \quad \text{for } i = 1, 2, 3.$$
The orders follow from the Proposition above: each blow-up of a point adds $1$ to $K$ along the new curve, plus the coefficients of $K$ of the curves through the point.

Discrepancies measure singularities of pairs. Let $X$ be normal and $\Delta$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with $K_X + \Delta$ $\mathbb{Q}$-Cartier, where $K_X$ is the [[canonical divisor|W05VYY4HBR]]. A *divisor over $X$* is a prime divisor $E$ on some normal $Y$ with a proper birational $\mu : Y \to X$; it gives a divisorial valuation $\operatorname{ord}_E$ of the function field, and its *centre* is the closure of $\mu(E)$.

**Definition (discrepancy and klt pair).** For $\mu$ as above, write
$$K_Y + \mu^{-1}_*\Delta = \mu^*(K_X + \Delta) + \sum_E a(E; X, \Delta)\, E,$$
with $\mu_* K_Y = K_X$ and the sum over the exceptional prime divisors of $\mu$. The number $a(E; X, \Delta)$ is the *discrepancy* of $E$; it depends only on the valuation $\operatorname{ord}_E$. For a prime divisor $E$ on $X$, put $a(E; X, \Delta) = -\operatorname{coeff}_E \Delta$. The pair $(X, \Delta)$ is *klt* (Kawamata log terminal) if $a(E; X, \Delta) > -1$ for all divisors $E$ over $X$, and *log canonical* (lc) if $a(E; X, \Delta) \geq -1$ for all $E$. It is klt or lc *at $x$* if this holds for all $E$ whose centre contains $x$.

**Proposition.** Let $X$ be smooth, $D$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $\mu : Y \to X$ a log resolution of $D$. Write $\mu^*D = \sum_i b_i E_i$ and $K_{Y/X} = \sum_i k_i E_i$, with the $E_i$ the prime divisors in the support of $\mu^*D + \operatorname{Exc}(\mu)$. Then $(X, D)$ is klt if and only if $k_i - b_i > -1$ for all $i$, and lc if and only if $k_i - b_i \geq -1$ for all $i$ (Kollár and Mori, *Birational Geometry of Algebraic Varieties*, Chapter 2).

The point is that a further blow-up of an snc configuration with coefficients $k_i - b_i > -1$ only creates divisors with discrepancy $> -1$. For $D$ effective on smooth $X$ and $x \in X$, the *log canonical threshold* is $\operatorname{lct}_x(D) = \sup\{c \geq 0 : (X, cD) \text{ is klt at } x\}$. By the Proposition, $\operatorname{lct}_x(D) = \min_i (k_i + 1)/b_i$ over the $E_i$ with $b_i > 0$ whose centre contains $x$.

**Example.** For the cusp, the strict transform of $C$ has $(k, b) = (0, 1)$, and the table above gives $\operatorname{lct}_0(C) = \min(1, 2/2, 3/3, 5/6) = 5/6$. So $(\mathbb{A}^2, cC)$ is klt at $0$ exactly when $c < 5/6$.

The inductive step of Angehrn-Siu in the development works with discrepancies over places in this sense: it shows that a pair $\Delta + (1-\delta)D + \frac{h}{l}\operatorname{div}(f)$ is not klt at given points by producing a divisorial valuation with discrepancy $\leq -1$.

## Kodaira and Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing

**Theorem (Kodaira, Akizuki-Nakano).** Let $X$ be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension $n$ and $L$ a line bundle with a positive metric, for example $X$ smooth projective and $L$ ample. Then $H^q(X, K_X + L) = 0$ for $q > 0$. More generally, $H^q(X, \Omega^p_X \otimes L) = 0$ for $p + q > n$.

See Kodaira (Proc. Nat. Acad. Sci. 39, 1953), Akizuki and Nakano (Proc. Japan Acad. 30, 1954), Griffiths and Harris, Chapter 1, and Lazarsfeld, *Positivity I*, Section 4.2. By the Lefschetz principle the first statement holds for smooth projective varieties over any field of characteristic $0$.

*Proof (outline).* Take the Kähler form $\omega = 2\pi c_1(L, h)$. By Hodge theory and Dolbeault's theorem ([geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/)), each class in $H^q(X, \Omega^p \otimes L)$ has a harmonic representative $u$, with $\Delta'' u = 0$ for $\Delta'' = \bar\partial\bar\partial^* + \bar\partial^*\bar\partial$. The Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity says $\Delta'' = \Delta' + [i\Theta, \Lambda]$, where $\Delta'$ is the Laplacian of the $(1,0)$-part of the Chern connection and $\Lambda$ is the adjoint of $\omega \wedge \cdot$. With $i\Theta = \omega$, the operator $[\omega, \Lambda]$ acts on $(p, q)$-forms as multiplication by $p + q - n$. So $0 = \langle \Delta'' u, u \rangle = \|D' u\|^2 + \|D'^* u\|^2 + (p + q - n)\|u\|^2$, and $u = 0$ when $p + q > n$. $\square$

**Remark.** Kodaira vanishing fails in characteristic $p > 0$ (Raynaud, *Contre-exemple au vanishing theorem en caractéristique $p > 0$*, 1978). Deligne and Illusie (*Relèvements modulo $p^2$ et décomposition du complexe de de Rham*, Invent. Math. 89, 1987) prove it for $X$ smooth projective over a perfect field of characteristic $p$ with $\dim X < p$ that lifts to $W_2(k)$. Spreading out and reduction modulo $p$ then give an algebraic proof in characteristic $0$. The development proves Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing this way (`kawamata_viehweg_spreading`, `charp_vanishing_liftable`).

The hypothesis "ample" is too strong for applications: on a resolution, the pullback of an ample bundle is only nef and big.

**Theorem ([[Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing|TAMCTW7SA0]]).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over a field of characteristic $0$ and $D$ a nef and big divisor. Then $H^i(X, K_X + D) = 0$ for $i > 0$ (Kawamata, Math. Ann. 261, 1982; Viehweg, J. reine angew. Math. 335, 1982; Lazarsfeld, *Positivity I*, Section 4.3).

**Example.** Bigness is needed: for $D = 0$, which is nef, $H^n(X, K_X) \cong H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X)^* \neq 0$ by Serre duality.

**Theorem (Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor form).** Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over a field of characteristic $0$ and $N$ an integral divisor with
$$N \equiv B + \Delta,$$
where $B$ is a nef and big $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $\Delta = \sum_i d_i \Delta_i$ has snc support and $0 \leq d_i < 1$. Then $H^i(X, K_X + N) = 0$ for $i > 0$ (Lazarsfeld, *Positivity II*, Section 9.1; Esnault and Viehweg, *Lectures on Vanishing Theorems*, DMV Seminar 20, 1992).

**Corollary.** Let $Y$ be smooth projective over a field of characteristic $0$, $\Gamma$ an effective divisor with snc support, $e \geq 1$ an integer such that each coefficient of $\Gamma$ is $< e$, $A$ an ample line bundle and $Q$ a line bundle with $eQ = eK_Y + \Gamma + A$ in $\operatorname{Pic} Y$. Then $H^i(Y, Q) = 0$ for $i > 0$.

*Proof.* Put $N = Q - K_Y$. Then $N \equiv \frac{1}{e}A + \frac{1}{e}\Gamma$, where $\frac1e A$ is ample, hence nef and big, and $\frac1e\Gamma$ has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$. $\square$

This is the form `snc_boundary_ample_decomposition` that ends the proof of the vanishing on a log resolution in the development.

*On the proof.* For $B$ ample, Kawamata's covering lemma gives a finite cover $\pi : X' \to X$ with $X'$ smooth on which $\pi^*\Delta$ becomes integral, and $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + N)$ is a direct summand of $\pi_*$ of a sheaf to which Kodaira vanishing applies. For $B$ nef and big, Kodaira's lemma gives $mB \equiv A + E$, so $B \equiv \big((1 - \varepsilon) B + \frac{\varepsilon}{m}A\big) + \frac{\varepsilon}{m}E$ with the first part ample, and one passes to a log resolution of $\Delta + E$. See Lazarsfeld, *Positivity I*, Section 4.3.

**Theorem (relative Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing).** Let $f : Y \to X$ be a proper birational morphism of varieties over a field of characteristic $0$ with $Y$ smooth, and $N$ an integral divisor on $Y$ with $N \equiv_f B + \Delta$, where $B$ is $f$-nef (nonnegative on curves contracted by $f$) and $\Delta$ has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$. Then $R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_Y(K_Y + N) = 0$ for $i > 0$ (Lazarsfeld, *Positivity II*, Section 9.1).

Here bigness relative to $f$ is automatic, since $f$ is birational.

## Multiplier ideals and Nadel vanishing

Let $X$ be a smooth complex variety.

**Definition.** Let $D$ be an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor on $X$ and $\mu : Y \to X$ a log resolution of $D$. The *[[multiplier ideal|BKM0TQSCD9]]* of $D$ is
$$\mathcal{J}(D) = \mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y\big(K_{Y/X} - \lfloor \mu^*D \rfloor\big),$$
where $\lfloor \sum b_i E_i \rfloor = \sum \lfloor b_i \rfloor E_i$. For a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ with log resolution $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-F)$ and $c \geq 0$, $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \mathfrak{a}) = \mu_*\mathcal{O}_Y(K_{Y/X} - \lfloor cF \rfloor)$.

**Theorem.** $\mathcal{J}(D)$ is an ideal sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$ and does not depend on the log resolution (Lazarsfeld, *Positivity II*, Section 9.2).

That it is an ideal follows from $\mathcal{J}(D) \subseteq \mu_*\mathcal{O}_Y(K_{Y/X}) = \mathcal{O}_X$, since $K_{Y/X}$ is effective and exceptional and $X$ is normal. Independence follows by comparing two log resolutions with a third that dominates both. By the Proposition on discrepancies, $(X, D)$ is klt if and only if $\mathcal{J}(D) = \mathcal{O}_X$, so the zero set $Z(\mathcal{J}(D))$ is the *non-klt locus* of $(X, D)$.

**Proposition.** Let $D, D'$ be effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisors.

1. If $D \leq D'$, then $\mathcal{J}(D') \subseteq \mathcal{J}(D)$.
2. If $A$ is an integral divisor, $\mathcal{J}(D + A) = \mathcal{J}(D) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(-A)$. In particular $\mathcal{J}(A) = \mathcal{O}_X(-A)$ for $A$ integral and effective.
3. If $D = \sum a_i D_i$ has snc support, then $\mathcal{J}(D) = \mathcal{O}_X(-\lfloor D \rfloor)$.
4. For $\varepsilon > 0$ small, $\mathcal{J}((1 + \varepsilon) D) = \mathcal{J}(D)$.

*Proof of 3 and 4.* For 3, the identity is a log resolution, and $K_{X/X} = 0$. For 4, fix a log resolution; $\lfloor (1 + \varepsilon) b_i \rfloor = \lfloor b_i \rfloor$ for all of the finitely many $b_i$ once $\varepsilon$ is small. $\square$

**Example (a point).** Let $X = \mathbb{C}^n$ and $\mathfrak{m}$ the ideal of $0$. The blow-up of $0$ is a log resolution with $K_{Y/X} = (n-1)E$ and $\mathfrak{m} \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y = \mathcal{O}_Y(-E)$. Since $\mu_*\mathcal{O}_Y(-jE) = \mathfrak{m}^j$ for $j \geq 0$ and $= \mathcal{O}_X$ for $j \leq 0$,
$$\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \mathfrak{m}) = \mathfrak{m}^{\lfloor c \rfloor - n + 1}.$$
So $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \mathfrak{m})$ is trivial exactly when $c < n$. More generally, for a monomial ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathcal{J}(c \cdot \mathfrak{a})$ is generated by the monomials $x^v$ with $v + (1, \dots, 1)$ in the interior of $c$ times the Newton polyhedron of $\mathfrak{a}$ (Howald, *Multiplier ideals of monomial ideals*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 353, 2001).

**Example (the cusp).** With the log resolution of the cusp $C$ above, $\mathcal{J}(cC) = \mathcal{O}$ near $0$ for $c < 5/6$, and $\mathcal{J}(cC) = \mathfrak{m}$ near $0$ for $5/6 \leq c < 1$: for such $c$ only $E_3$ has $k_3 - \lfloor 6c \rfloor = -1 < 0$, and $\mu_*\mathcal{O}_Y(-E_3) = \mathfrak{m}$ because $E_3$ maps to $0$.

Multiplier ideals have an analytic definition, which is the form that $L^2$ methods use.

**Definition.** Let $\varphi$ be a psh function on an open set of $\mathbb{C}^n$. The *analytic multiplier ideal* $\mathcal{J}(\varphi)$ is the sheaf of germs of holomorphic functions $f$ such that $|f|^2 e^{-\varphi}$ is integrable near the point. It is a coherent ideal sheaf (Nadel, *Multiplier ideal sheaves and Kähler-Einstein metrics of positive scalar curvature*, Ann. of Math. 132, 1990).

**Proposition.** If $D = \sum_j c_j \operatorname{div}(g_j)$ and $\varphi_D = \sum_j c_j \log|g_j|^2$, then $\mathcal{J}(\varphi_D) = \mathcal{J}(D)$.

*Proof.* Let $\mu$ be a log resolution and $y$ local coordinates on $Y$ in which $\mu^*D = \sum_i b_i\, \operatorname{div}(y_i)$ and $K_{Y/X} = \sum_i k_i\, \operatorname{div}(y_i)$. By the change of variables formula, $\int_U |f|^2 e^{-\varphi_D}\, d\lambda = \int_{\mu^{-1}U} |f \circ \mu|^2 |J_\mu|^2 e^{-\varphi_D \circ \mu}\, d\lambda$, where the Jacobian $J_\mu$ is a unit times $\prod y_i^{k_i}$ and $e^{-\varphi_D \circ \mu}$ is a positive smooth function times $\prod |y_i|^{-2b_i}$. Since $\int_{|y| < 1} |y|^{2s}\, d\lambda(y) < \infty$ if and only if $s > -1$, the integral is finite near each point if and only if $\operatorname{ord}_{E_i}(f \circ \mu) + k_i - b_i > -1$ for all $i$, that is, $\operatorname{ord}_{E_i}(f\circ\mu) \geq \lfloor b_i \rfloor - k_i$. This is the condition $f \in \mu_*\mathcal{O}_Y(K_{Y/X} - \lfloor \mu^*D \rfloor)$. $\square$

**Theorem (local vanishing).** Let $\mu : Y \to X$ be a log resolution of an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D$. Then $R^i\mu_* \mathcal{O}_Y(K_{Y/X} - \lfloor \mu^*D \rfloor) = 0$ for $i > 0$ (Lazarsfeld, *Positivity II*, Section 9.4).

*Proof.* Write $K_{Y/X} - \lfloor \mu^*D \rfloor = K_Y + N$ with $N = -\mu^*(K_X + D) + \{\mu^*D\}$, where the fractional part $\{\mu^*D\}$ has snc support and coefficients in $[0, 1)$. The $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $B = -\mu^*(K_X + D)$ is zero on each curve contracted by $\mu$, so it is $\mu$-nef. Relative Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing with $\Delta = \{\mu^*D\}$ ends the proof. $\square$

**Theorem (Nadel vanishing).** Let $X$ be a smooth complex projective variety, $D$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $L$ an integral divisor with $L - D$ nef and big. Then
$$H^i\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(D)\big) = 0 \quad \text{for } i > 0.$$

*Proof.* Let $\mu : Y \to X$ be a projective log resolution of $D$. Then
$$K_Y + \mu^*L - \lfloor \mu^*D \rfloor = K_Y + \mu^*(L - D) + \{\mu^*D\},$$
with $\mu^*(L - D)$ nef and big and $\{\mu^*D\}$ of snc support with coefficients in $[0, 1)$. By the $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor form of Kawamata-Viehweg, $H^i(Y, K_Y + \mu^*L - \lfloor \mu^*D \rfloor) = 0$ for $i > 0$. Since $K_Y = \mu^*K_X + K_{Y/X}$, this sheaf is $\mu^*\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{O}_Y(K_{Y/X} - \lfloor \mu^*D \rfloor)$. By the projection formula and local vanishing, its higher direct images vanish and its direct image is $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(D)$. The Leray spectral sequence ends the proof. $\square$

The development follows this proof in `vanishing_logRes_pushforward_and_local` and `vanishing_logRes_floor`. It states its conclusion on $X$ for $s_*\mathcal{O}_Y(K_Y - \lfloor s^*G/m \rfloor) \otimes N$, which is $\mathcal{O}_X(K_X) \otimes \mathcal{J}(\frac1m G) \otimes N$ when $X$ is smooth.

**Theorem (Nadel vanishing, analytic form).** Let $X$ be a projective manifold with a Kähler form $\omega$ and $L$ a line bundle with a singular metric of weights $\varphi$ such that $dd^c\varphi \geq \varepsilon\omega$ as currents for some $\varepsilon > 0$. Then $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{J}(\varphi)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ (Nadel, 1990; Demailly, *Complex Analytic and Differential Geometry*, Chapter VIII).

The analytic proof solves $\bar\partial$ with the $L^2$ estimates of the next section, after a regularization of $\varphi$.

**Corollary.** In the algebraic Nadel theorem, the restriction map
$$H^0(X, K_X + L) \to H^0\big(X, \mathcal{O}_X(K_X + L) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X / \mathcal{J}(D)\big)$$
is onto. If $x$ is an isolated point of $Z(\mathcal{J}(D))$, then $K_X + L$ has a section that does not vanish at $x$.

*Proof.* The first claim is the long exact sequence of $0 \to \mathcal{J}(D) \to \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{J}(D) \to 0$, twisted by $K_X + L$, with $H^1 = 0$. If $x$ is isolated, $H^0$ of the quotient is the direct sum of its stalk at $x$ and the sections on the rest of the support. Lift the element that is $1$ at $x$ and $0$ elsewhere. Its value at $x$ is not in $\mathcal{J}(D)_x \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x$, so it is nonzero. $\square$

To use the corollary, we need divisors that are singular at a point. The next proposition says how singular.

**Proposition.** Let $X$ be smooth of dimension $n$, $D$ an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor and $x \in X$.

1. If $\operatorname{mult}_x D \geq n + k - 1$ for an integer $k \geq 1$, then $\mathcal{J}(D)_x \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x^k$. In particular, if $\operatorname{mult}_x D \geq n$, then $(X, D)$ is not klt at $x$.
2. If $\operatorname{mult}_x D < 1$, then $\mathcal{J}(D)_x = \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$.

*Proof of 1.* Let $E$ be the exceptional divisor of the blow-up of $x$. Principalization on the blow-up gives a log resolution of $D$ on which $E$ (its strict transform) is one of the $E_i$, with $k_E = n - 1$ and $b_E = \operatorname{mult}_x D$. If $f \in \mathcal{J}(D)_x$, then $\operatorname{ord}_E f \geq \lfloor \operatorname{mult}_x D \rfloor - n + 1 \geq k$. Since $\operatorname{ord}_E f = \operatorname{mult}_x f$, $f \in \mathfrak{m}_x^k$. $\square$

Part 2 is Skoda's integrability theorem: if $\nu(\varphi, x) < 1$, then $e^{-\varphi}$ is integrable near $x$ (Skoda, Bull. Soc. Math. France 100, 1972; Lazarsfeld, *Positivity II*, Chapter 9). The bound $n$ in part 1 cannot be lowered. For $D = \frac{c}{n}(H_1 + \dots + H_n)$, with $H_i$ the coordinate hyperplanes of $\mathbb{C}^n$, $\operatorname{mult}_0 D = c$ and $D$ has snc support, so $(X, D)$ is klt at $0$ exactly when $c < n$.

## L2 estimates

The $L^2$ method produces holomorphic functions and sections with control of their norms. It proves Kodaira and Nadel vanishing, the coherence of analytic multiplier ideals, extension theorems, and the asymptotics of Bergman kernels that the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula uses. The $L^2$ spaces and the [[Hilbert space|HM2HHE3J51]] facts are in the [analysis chapter](/learn/analysis/).

**Definition.** An open set $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ is *pseudoconvex* if it has a continuous psh exhaustion function, that is, a psh $\rho$ with $\{\rho < c\}$ relatively compact in $\Omega$ for each $c$. Convex open sets, and more generally domains of holomorphy, are pseudoconvex.

For a $(0,1)$-form $g = \sum_k g_k\, d\bar z_k$ and a $C^2$ strictly psh function $\varphi$, put
$$|g|^2_{i\partial\bar\partial\varphi}(z) = \sup_{\xi \neq 0} \frac{\big|\sum_k g_k(z)\, \bar\xi_k\big|^2}{\sum_{j,k} \varphi_{j\bar k}(z)\, \xi_j \bar\xi_k},$$
where $\varphi_{j\bar k} = \partial^2 \varphi / \partial z_j \partial \bar z_k$. If $(\varphi_{j\bar k}) \geq c \cdot 1$ for a constant $c > 0$, then $|g|^2_{i\partial\bar\partial\varphi} \leq |g|^2 / c$.

**Theorem (Hörmander L2 estimate).** Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ be pseudoconvex and $\varphi \in C^2(\Omega)$ strictly psh. Let $g$ be a $(0,1)$-form with locally square integrable coefficients and $\bar\partial g = 0$ in the sense of distributions. Then there is a function $u$ with $\bar\partial u = g$ and
$$\int_\Omega |u|^2 e^{-\varphi}\, d\lambda \leq \int_\Omega |g|^2_{i\partial\bar\partial\varphi}\, e^{-\varphi}\, d\lambda.$$

See Hörmander, *L2 estimates and existence theorems for the $\bar\partial$ operator* (Acta Math. 113, 1965), and his book *An Introduction to Complex Analysis in Several Variables*, Chapter IV. Theorem 4.4.2 of the book is a version for any psh $\varphi$, with the weight $e^{-\varphi}(1 + |z|^2)^{-2}$ on the left; Demailly, *Complex Analytic and Differential Geometry*, Chapter VIII, gives the form above.

**Example.** Let $n = 1$, $\Omega = \mathbb{C}$, $\varphi = |z|^2$ and $g = d\bar z$. Then $|g|^2_{i\partial\bar\partial\varphi} = 1$, and the right side is $\int e^{-|z|^2} d\lambda = \pi$. The solution of minimal norm is $u = \bar z$, which is orthogonal to the holomorphic functions in $L^2(e^{-|z|^2})$, and $\int |z|^2 e^{-|z|^2} d\lambda = \pi$. So the constant $1$ is sharp.

*Proof (outline, by Hilbert space methods).* Let $T = \bar\partial$ from functions to $(0,1)$-forms and $S = \bar\partial$ from $(0,1)$-forms to $(0,2)$-forms, as closed densely defined operators between the weighted spaces $L^2(\Omega, e^{-\varphi})$. Let $T^*$ be the Hilbert space adjoint.

1. *A priori estimate.* For smooth compactly supported $\alpha$, an integration by parts (the Morrey-Kohn-Hörmander identity) gives
$$\begin{aligned} \|T^*\alpha\|^2 + \|S\alpha\|^2 &= \int \sum_{j,k} \varphi_{j\bar k}\, \alpha_j \bar\alpha_k\, e^{-\varphi} + \int \sum_{j,k} \Big|\frac{\partial \alpha_j}{\partial \bar z_k}\Big|^2 e^{-\varphi} \\ &\geq \int \sum_{j,k} \varphi_{j\bar k}\, \alpha_j \bar\alpha_k\, e^{-\varphi}. \end{aligned}$$
Pseudoconvexity gives cutoffs and a twist of the weight that extend this to all $\alpha$ in the domains of $T^*$ and $S$.
2. *Duality.* Let $\alpha$ be in the domain of $T^*$. Write $\alpha = \alpha_1 + \alpha_2$ with $\alpha_1 \in \ker S$ and $\alpha_2 \perp \ker S$. Since $g \in \ker S$ and the range of $T$ lies in $\ker S$, $\langle g, \alpha \rangle = \langle g, \alpha_1 \rangle$ and $T^*\alpha_2 = 0$. By Cauchy-Schwarz and step 1, $|\langle g, \alpha \rangle|^2 \leq A\, \|T^*\alpha\|^2$ with $A = \int |g|^2_{i\partial\bar\partial\varphi} e^{-\varphi}$.
3. *Riesz.* The linear form $T^*\alpha \mapsto \langle \alpha, g\rangle$ on the range of $T^*$ is bounded by $A^{1/2}$. The Riesz representation theorem gives $u$ with $\|u\|^2 \leq A$ and $\langle u, T^*\alpha \rangle = \langle g, \alpha \rangle$ for all $\alpha$, that is, $Tu = g$. $\square$

The development proves `HStatement` exactly along these three steps (`APStatement`, `TestIneqStatement`, `rieszOnRange`), on étale Stein spaces.

**Corollary.** Let $\Omega$ and $\varphi$ be as in the theorem, $F_0$ a smooth function and $h \geq 0$ measurable with $|\bar\partial F_0|^2_{i\partial\bar\partial\varphi} \leq h$. Then there is a holomorphic $F$ on $\Omega$ with $\int |F_0 - F|^2 e^{-\varphi}\, d\lambda \leq \int h\, e^{-\varphi}\, d\lambda$.

*Proof.* Solve $\bar\partial u = \bar\partial F_0$ and put $F = F_0 - u$. Then $\bar\partial F = 0$ weakly, so $F$ is equal almost everywhere to a holomorphic function. $\square$

**Example (interpolation at a point).** Let $\Omega$ be bounded and pseudoconvex and $x \in \Omega$. Let $\chi$ be a cutoff with $\chi = 1$ near $x$ and support in a small ball, and put $\varphi = n\log|z - x|^2 + |z|^2$, which is psh with Levi matrix $\geq 1$ off $x$. Apply the corollary to $F_0 = \chi$ (with a regularization of $\varphi$ at $x$). Then $u = \chi - F$ is holomorphic near $x$, and $\int |u|^2 |z - x|^{-2n} < \infty$ forces $u(x) = 0$, since $|z - x|^{-2n}$ is not integrable at $x$. So $F(x) = 1$, with $\int_\Omega |F|^2 \leq C$ for a $C$ that depends only on $\Omega$ and $\chi$. The weight $n\log|z-x|^2$ has Lelong number $n$ at $x$: by the proposition on multiplicities, this is the weight whose multiplier ideal is $\mathfrak{m}_x$.

The same idea on a compact manifold, with the weight $\psi = n\log|s|^2$ for sections $s_1, \dots, s_n$ with finitely many common zeros, produces sections with prescribed values at those zeros. This is the statement `L2ExtensionLogCanonical` of the development; the name records that $e^{-\psi}$ is log canonical but not klt at the zeros.

Extension from a submanifold is harder, since the weight must vanish along a whole hypersurface without losing integrability.

**Theorem (Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension).** Let $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}^n$ be a bounded pseudoconvex open set, $\varphi$ a psh function on $\Omega$, and $H = \Omega \cap \{z_n = 0\}$. For each holomorphic function $f$ on $H$ with $\int_H |f|^2 e^{-\varphi}\, d\lambda_{n-1} < \infty$ there is a holomorphic $F$ on $\Omega$ with $F|_H = f$ and
$$\int_\Omega |F|^2 e^{-\varphi}\, d\lambda_n \leq C \int_H |f|^2 e^{-\varphi}\, d\lambda_{n-1},$$
where $C$ depends only on $\sup_\Omega |z_n|$ (Ohsawa and Takegoshi, *On the extension of $L^2$ holomorphic functions*, Math. Z. 195, 1987).

If $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{C}^{n-1} \times \{|z_n| < r\}$, one can take $C = \pi r^2$, which is optimal (Błocki, Invent. Math. 193, 2013; Guan and Zhou, Ann. of Math. 181, 2015). Manivel (Math. Z. 212, 1993) and Demailly (*On the Ohsawa-Takegoshi-Manivel $L^2$ extension theorem*, 2000) extend sections of line bundles on weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifolds from the zero set of a section of a Hermitian vector bundle, with weights of the form $e^{-\psi}/(1 + \psi^2)$ or similar. The development's `L2ExtensionLogCanonical` has this shape, with weight $e^{-\psi}/(1 + \delta^2\psi^2)$.

**Remark.** Ohsawa-Takegoshi gives the restriction theorem $\mathcal{J}(\varphi|_H) \subseteq \mathcal{J}(\varphi)|_H$, Demailly's approximation of psh functions, and Siu's invariance of plurigenera (Invent. Math. 134, 1998).

**Remark (Bergman kernel asymptotics and Hilbert-Samuel).** Let $(L, h)$ be a positive Hermitian line bundle on a compact manifold $X$ of dimension $n$, with $\omega = c_1(L, h)$. Give $H^0(X, L^{\otimes m})$ the $L^2$ norm $\int_X |s|^2 \omega^n / n!$, and let $B_m(x) = \sum_i |s_i(x)|^2$ for an orthonormal basis. Then $B_m(x) = m^n + O(m^{n-1})$ uniformly on $X$ (Tian, J. Differential Geom. 32, 1990; Bouche, Ann. Inst. Fourier 40, 1990; Zelditch, Int. Math. Res. Not. 1998). The upper bound $B_m \leq C m^n$, which says $\|s\|_{\sup} \leq C' m^{n/2}\|s\|_{L^2}$, follows from the sub-mean value inequality on balls of radius $m^{-1/2}$. The lower bound uses Hörmander's estimate to build peak sections, as in the interpolation example. With this comparison of sup norms and $L^2$ norms, Abbes and Bouche (Ann. Inst. Fourier 45, 1995) give a proof of the arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula of Gillet and Soulé (Invent. Math. 110, 1992), stated in the [Arakelov chapter](/learn/arakelov/). The block formula of the development has the same bound $|f(z)|/h(z)^m \leq C m^{D/2}\|f\|$.

## The Angehrn-Siu theorem

**Remark (Fujita's conjecture).** Fujita conjectured the following. Let $X$ be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension $n$ and $A$ an ample divisor. Then $K_X + mA$ is globally generated for $m \geq n + 1$ and very ample for $m \geq n + 2$.

The freeness part is known for $n \leq 2$ (Reider, Ann. of Math. 127, 1988), $n = 3$ (Ein and Lazarsfeld, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 6, 1993) and $n = 4$ (Kawamata, Math. Ann. 308, 1997). For all $n$ the best general bounds come from the method of Angehrn and Siu.

**Theorem (Angehrn-Siu).** Let $X$ be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension $n$, $L$ an ample divisor and $x \in X$. Suppose that
$$(L^{\dim Z} \cdot Z) > \Big(\tfrac{1}{2} n(n+1)\Big)^{\dim Z}$$
for each irreducible subvariety $Z \subseteq X$ of positive dimension that contains $x$. Then $K_X + L$ has a section that does not vanish at $x$ (Angehrn and Siu, *Effective freeness and point separation for adjoint bundles*, Invent. Math. 122, 1995; Lazarsfeld, *Positivity II*, Section 10.4).

**Corollary.** For $A$ ample, $K_X + mA$ is globally generated for $m \geq \frac12 n(n+1) + 1$.

*Proof.* For $L = mA$ and $Z$ of dimension $d$, $(L^d \cdot Z) = m^d (A^d \cdot Z) \geq m^d > (\frac12 n(n+1))^d$, since $(A^d \cdot Z)$ is a positive integer. $\square$

The proof produces an effective $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor $D \equiv \lambda L$ with $\lambda < 1$ such that $x$ is an isolated point of the non-klt locus $Z(\mathcal{J}(D))$. Then $L - D \equiv (1 - \lambda)L$ is ample, and the corollary of Nadel vanishing gives the section. The divisors come from a count of sections.

**Lemma.** Let $X$ be a projective variety, $L$ an ample divisor, $Z \subseteq X$ a subvariety of dimension $d \geq 1$ and $x$ a smooth point of $Z$. If $(L^d \cdot Z) > c^d$ for a real $c > 0$, then for each large divisible $k$ there is a divisor $D_k \in |kL|$, not containing $Z$, with $\operatorname{mult}_x (D_k|_Z) > ck$.

*Proof.* Choose $c'$ with $c < c' < (L^d \cdot Z)^{1/d}$ and put $m = \lceil c'k \rceil$. By asymptotic Riemann-Roch on $Z$, $h^0(Z, kL|_Z) = (L^d \cdot Z) k^d/d! + O(k^{d-1})$. Vanishing to order $m$ at the smooth point $x$ of $Z$ is $\binom{m + d - 1}{d} = c'^d k^d / d! + O(k^{d-1})$ linear conditions. For large $k$ the first number is larger, so some nonzero section of $kL|_Z$ vanishes to order $m > ck$ at $x$. By Serre vanishing, $H^0(X, kL) \to H^0(Z, kL|_Z)$ is onto for large $k$, so the section lifts to $X$. $\square$

With $|P|$ points instead of one, the count asks for $|P|\binom{m + d - 1}{d}$ conditions. This is the inequality $|P| \binom{M + k - 1}{k} < q l^k / k!$ in the inductive step of Angehrn-Siu of the development.

**Definition.** Let $(X, D)$ be lc at $x$. A *log canonical centre* of $(X, D)$ is the centre on $X$ of a divisor $E$ over $X$ with $a(E; X, D) = -1$.

*Proof of the theorem (outline).* Put $c_d = \frac12 n(n+1)$ for all $d$, so the hypothesis is $(L^d \cdot Z) > c_d^d$.

1. *First divisor.* Apply the lemma to $Z = X$ with $c$ slightly less than $(L^n)^{1/n}$: there is $D_1 \equiv \lambda_1 L$ with $\operatorname{mult}_x D_1 \geq n$ and $\lambda_1$ just above $n / (L^n)^{1/n} < n / c_n$. By the proposition on multiplicities, $(X, D_1)$ is not klt at $x$. Replace $D_1$ by $t D_1$ with $t = \operatorname{lct}_x(D_1) \leq 1$, so $(X, D_1)$ is lc but not klt at $x$. After a small perturbation (the tie-breaking of Angehrn and Siu) there is a unique minimal log canonical centre $Z_1$ through $x$.
2. *Cutting down.* If $\dim Z_1 = 0$, then $x$ is isolated in the non-klt locus. If $d = \dim Z_1 > 0$, apply the lemma to $Z_1$: there is $D' \equiv \lambda' L$ whose restriction to $Z_1$ has multiplicity about $d$ at $x$, with $\lambda'$ just above $d / (L^d \cdot Z_1)^{1/d} < d / c_d$. Then $(1 - \varepsilon) D_1 + t' D'$, with $t' \leq 1$ chosen as a threshold, has a new minimal log canonical centre at $x$ that is properly contained in $Z_1$. Inversion of adjunction (or the analytic restriction theorem) is what passes from the multiplicity on $Z_1$ to the singularity of the pair on $X$.
3. *The count.* The dimensions of the centres strictly decrease, so at most $n$ steps occur, one for each dimension $d$, and the total coefficient is $\lambda < \sum_{d=1}^{n} d / c_d = \frac{n(n+1)/2}{n(n+1)/2} = 1$.

So the final divisor $D$ satisfies $D \equiv \lambda L$ with $\lambda < 1$ and $x$ isolated in $Z(\mathcal{J}(D))$. $\square$

The constant $\frac12 n(n+1) = 1 + 2 + \dots + n$ is the sum of the costs $d$ of the centres of dimensions $d = 1, \dots, n$. Fujita's conjecture would need the cost of each step to be $1$ instead of $d$.

**Remark.** Angehrn and Siu also prove *point separation*: under a stronger lower bound on the degrees $(L^d \cdot Z)$ for subvarieties through two points $x$ and $y$, $K_X + L$ has a section that vanishes at one of them and not at the other. The same method works for a klt pair $(X, \Delta)$ in place of $X$, with $K_X + \Delta + L$ Cartier. The development states this as `PointSeparationPair`. It is an input of [EveryCharacter from the literature](/step/every-character-of-literature/), through `point_separation_pair_of_literature`, and the development proves its inductive step from principalization (`exists_global_step`, `not_klt_at_points`). Kollár, *Singularities of pairs* (Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 62, Part 1, 1997), gives an algebraic account of the method.
