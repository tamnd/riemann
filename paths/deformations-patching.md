This chapter gives the deformation theory of Galois representations and the method of Taylor, Wiles and Kisin that proves automorphy lifting theorems. The method has two inputs, which we also treat: Galois representations attached to automorphic representations, built from the cohomology of Shimura varieties, and the Hecke algebras that carry these representations. The chapter ends with potential automorphy. The proof uses this material in two places. The Galois row [Row0b](/step/row0b-def/), proved in [the Galois row holds](/step/galois-row-holds/), is a theorem of the kind of the section on Shimura varieties, and its proof passes through congruences between Hecke eigensystems. The window bound [VWin](/step/vwin-def/) reaches its case counts through the package `HypothesesH'`, which `published_hypotheses_from_patching_alt` builds in [the window bound](/proof/vwin/), and through ordinary deformation rings, universal ordinary determinant rings and the automorphy of representations of dimensions $3$ and $4$. We fix a prime $\ell$, a finite extension $E$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}$, uniformizer $\lambda$ and residue field $k$. For a number field $F$ and a finite set $S$ of places that contains the places above $\ell$ and $\infty$, $G_{F,S}$ is the Galois group of the maximal extension of $F$ unramified outside $S$, and $G_{F_v}$ is a decomposition group at $v$.

## Deformation functors

**Definition.** Let $\mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ be the category of complete noetherian local $\mathcal{O}$-algebras $A$ for which $\mathcal{O} \to A \to A/\mathfrak{m}_A$ induces $k \cong A/\mathfrak{m}_A$, with local $\mathcal{O}$-algebra maps. Let $\mathrm{Art}_\mathcal{O}$ be its full subcategory of Artinian rings. A map $A' \to A$ in $\mathrm{Art}_\mathcal{O}$ is a *small extension* if it is surjective and its kernel $I$ satisfies $\mathfrak{m}_{A'} I = 0$.

**Definition.** Let $F : \mathrm{Art}_\mathcal{O} \to \mathrm{Sets}$ be a functor such that $F(k)$ is one point. Its *tangent space* is $t_F = F(k[\varepsilon])$. The functor is *pro-representable* if $F \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(R, -)$ on $\mathrm{Art}_\mathcal{O}$ for some $R \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$; then $t_F$ is the $k$-dual of the [[cotangent space|Z1CGB483J6]] $\mathfrak{m}_R/(\mathfrak{m}_R^2 + \lambda R)$ of $R/\lambda R$. A *hull* of $F$ is a pair of $R \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ and a morphism $\mathrm{Hom}(R, -) \to F$ that is bijective on tangent spaces and smooth: for each surjection $A' \to A$, the map $\mathrm{Hom}(R, A') \to \mathrm{Hom}(R, A) \times_{F(A)} F(A')$ is surjective.

**Theorem (Schlessinger).** Let $F$ be as above. For maps $A' \to A$ and $A'' \to A$ in $\mathrm{Art}_\mathcal{O}$, consider the natural map
$$h : F(A' \times_A A'') \to F(A') \times_{F(A)} F(A'').$$
Consider the conditions:

1. (H1) $h$ is surjective whenever $A'' \to A$ is a small extension;
2. (H2) $h$ is bijective when $A = k$ and $A'' = k[\varepsilon]$;
3. (H3) $\dim_k t_F < \infty$;
4. (H4) $h$ is bijective whenever $A' = A''$ and $A' \to A$ is a small extension.

Then $F$ has a hull if and only if (H1), (H2) and (H3) hold, and $F$ is pro-representable if and only if (H1) to (H4) hold. Condition (H2) makes $t_F$ a $k$-vector space. See Schlessinger, *Functors of Artin rings*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 130 (1968), Theorem 2.11.

**Definition.** A profinite group $G$ satisfies *Mazur's condition* $\Phi_\ell$ if, for each open subgroup $H$, the group $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is finite.

**Example.** The group $G_{F,S}$ satisfies $\Phi_\ell$. A nonzero map $H \to \mathbb{F}_\ell$ cuts out a cyclic extension of degree $\ell$ of the fixed field of $H$ that is unramified outside $S$, and by the Hermite-Minkowski theorem there are finitely many such extensions. For $K$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the group $G_K$ satisfies $\Phi_\ell$, since $K^\times/K^{\times \ell}$ is finite and local class field theory applies ([class field theory](/learn/class-field-theory/)). The group $G_\mathbb{Q}$ does not.

**Definition.** Let $G$ be profinite and $\bar\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ continuous. A *lift* of $\bar\rho$ to $A \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ is a continuous homomorphism $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_n(A)$ with $\rho \bmod \mathfrak{m}_A = \bar\rho$. Two lifts are *strictly equivalent* if they are conjugate by an element of $\widehat{\mathrm{GL}}_n(A) = \ker(\mathrm{GL}_n(A) \to \mathrm{GL}_n(k))$. A *deformation* is a strict equivalence class of lifts. Write $D^\square_{\bar\rho}$ and $D_{\bar\rho}$ for the functors of lifts and of deformations on $\mathrm{Art}_\mathcal{O}$. The *adjoint representation* $\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho$ is $M_n(k)$ with $g \cdot X = \bar\rho(g) X \bar\rho(g)^{-1}$, and $\mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho$ is its subspace of trace zero.

**Proposition (tangent spaces).** There are natural bijections $D^\square_{\bar\rho}(k[\varepsilon]) \cong Z^1(G, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ and $t_{D_{\bar\rho}} \cong H^1(G, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$. So
$$\dim_k D^\square_{\bar\rho}(k[\varepsilon]) = \dim_k H^1(G, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) + n^2 - \dim_k H^0(G, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho).$$

*Proof.* A lift to $k[\varepsilon]$ has the form $\rho(g) = (1 + \varepsilon c(g)) \bar\rho(g)$ with $c : G \to M_n(k)$ continuous. The identity $\rho(gh) = \rho(g)\rho(h)$ is the cocycle condition $c(gh) = c(g) + \bar\rho(g) c(h) \bar\rho(g)^{-1}$. Conjugation by $1 + \varepsilon X$ sends $c$ to $c + X - \bar\rho(g) X \bar\rho(g)^{-1}$, so it adds a coboundary, and every coboundary arises. The space of coboundaries is $M_n(k)/H^0(G, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$, which gives the count. $\square$

**Theorem (Mazur).** Let $G$ satisfy $\Phi_\ell$ and let $\bar\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ be continuous.

1. $D^\square_{\bar\rho}$ is pro-represented by a ring $R^\square_{\bar\rho}$, the *framed deformation ring*.
2. $D_{\bar\rho}$ has a hull.
3. If $\mathrm{End}_{k[G]}(\bar\rho) = k$, for example if $\bar\rho$ is absolutely irreducible, then $D_{\bar\rho}$ is pro-represented by a ring $R_{\bar\rho}$, the *universal deformation ring*, with a universal deformation $\rho^{\mathrm{univ}} : G \to \mathrm{GL}_n(R_{\bar\rho})$.

See Mazur, *Deforming Galois representations*, in *Galois Groups over $\mathbb{Q}$*, MSRI Publ. 16 (1989), section 1. In Schlessinger's criterion, $\Phi_\ell$ gives (H3) through the proposition above, and the condition $\mathrm{End} = k$ gives (H4): it makes the centralizer of each lift consist of scalars, so that strict equivalences glue.

The ring $R_{\bar\rho}$ is the object of [[deformation theory|H8AAQJ1SZW]] for Galois groups, and the rest of the chapter builds quotients of it with local conditions.

**Theorem (obstructions).** Let $G$ and $\bar\rho$ be as in part 3 of Mazur's theorem, and suppose that $h^i = \dim_k H^i(G, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ is finite for $i = 1, 2$. Then $R_{\bar\rho} \cong \mathcal{O}[[x_1, \dots, x_{h^1}]]/J$, where $J$ is generated by at most $h^2$ elements. So $\dim R_{\bar\rho} \ge 1 + h^1 - h^2$, and if $h^2 = 0$, then $R_{\bar\rho}$ is a power series ring over $\mathcal{O}$ in $h^1$ variables.

See Mazur (1989), section 1. The source of the relations is this: for a small extension $A' \to A$ with kernel $I$ and a lift $\rho_A$, a set-theoretic lift $\rho'$ gives the $2$-cocycle $(g, h) \mapsto \rho'(gh)\rho'(h)^{-1}\rho'(g)^{-1} - 1$ with values in $M_n(I) = \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho \otimes_k I$, whose class in $H^2(G, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) \otimes_k I$ vanishes if and only if $\rho_A$ lifts to $A'$. The bound on $\dim R_{\bar\rho}$ follows from [[Krull's principal ideal theorem|SJZW2HN1SC]] in the regular ring $\mathcal{O}[[x_1, \dots, x_{h^1}]]$.

**Example (odd representations of $G_\mathbb{Q}$).** Let $\ell$ be odd and let $\bar\rho : G_{\mathbb{Q},S} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(k)$ be absolutely irreducible and odd, that is, $\det \bar\rho(c) = -1$ for complex conjugation $c$. Fix a lift $\psi$ of $\det\bar\rho$ and consider deformations with determinant $\psi$. The proposition on tangent spaces, with $\mathrm{ad}^0$ in place of $\mathrm{ad}$, gives the tangent space $H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},S}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)$. Since $\bar\rho(c)$ is conjugate to $\mathrm{diag}(1, -1)$, complex conjugation acts on $\mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho$ with eigenvalues $1, -1, -1$, so $H^0(\mathbb{R}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)$ has dimension $1$. By Schur's lemma $H^0(G_{\mathbb{Q},S}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho) = 0$. Tate's global Euler characteristic formula ([Galois cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/)) gives $h^0 - h^1 + h^2 = 1 - 3$, so $h^1 - h^2 = 2$ and $\dim R \ge 3$. The problem is *unobstructed* if $h^2 = 0$, and then $R \cong \mathcal{O}[[x_1, x_2]]$. For the residual representations of a fixed newform of weight at least $3$, Weston (*Unobstructed modular deformation problems*, Amer. J. Math. 126, 2004) proved that this holds for almost all $\ell$.

**Remark (polarized deformations).** Let $F$ be a CM field with maximal totally real subfield $F^+$ and complex conjugation $c$. Clozel, Harris and Taylor use the group $\mathcal{G}_n = (\mathrm{GL}_n \times \mathrm{GL}_1) \rtimes \{1, \jmath\}$ with $\jmath (g, \mu) \jmath^{-1} = (\mu\, {}^t g^{-1}, \mu)$. A representation $\bar\rho$ of $G_F$ with $\bar\rho^c \cong \bar\rho^\vee \otimes \bar\mu|_{G_F}$ extends, with suitable choices, to $\bar r : G_{F^+} \to \mathcal{G}_n(k)$ with $\bar r^{-1}(\mathrm{GL}_n \times \mathrm{GL}_1) = G_F$. Deformations of $\bar r$ are the *polarized* deformations of $\bar\rho$. Their tangent space is $H^1(G_{F^+,S}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar r)$, where $\mathrm{ad}\,\bar r = M_n(k)$ with $(g, \mu)$ acting by conjugation by $g$ and $\jmath$ by $X \mapsto -{}^t X$; for $\ell$ odd it is the part of $H^1(G_{F,S}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ fixed by the action of $c$. See Clozel, Harris and Taylor (2008), section 2. The polarized cocycles of the development volumes are tangent vectors of this kind.

## Local deformation problems and global deformation rings

Let $v$ be a finite place of $F$, $\bar\rho_v = \bar\rho|_{G_{F_v}}$, and $R^\square_v = R^\square_{\bar\rho_v}$.

**Definition.** A *local deformation problem* for $\bar\rho_v$ is a quotient $R^\square_v \to R_v$ such that, for each $A \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$, the set of lifts $\rho : G_{F_v} \to \mathrm{GL}_n(A)$ whose map $R^\square_v \to A$ factors through $R_v$ is stable under conjugation by $\widehat{\mathrm{GL}}_n(A)$.

The tangent space of $R_v$ is then a subspace of $Z^1(G_{F_v}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ that contains the coboundaries. Write $L_v \subset H^1(G_{F_v}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$ for its image.

**Example (unramified lifts).** Let $v \nmid \ell$ and let $\bar\rho_v$ be unramified. An unramified lift is fixed by the image of a Frobenius element, which can be any lift of $\bar\rho_v(\mathrm{Frob}_v)$ to $\mathrm{GL}_n(A)$. So the unramified quotient is $\mathcal{O}[[x_{ij} : 1 \le i, j \le n]]$, formally smooth of relative dimension $n^2$, and $L_v = H^1(G_{F_v}/I_v, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$, of dimension $\dim_k H^0(G_{F_v}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$.

**Theorem (Kisin).** Let $K$ be finite over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ and $\bar\rho : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ continuous, and enlarge $E$ so that it contains the images of all embeddings $K \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. Fix a Hodge type $\mathbf{v}$ (a multiset of $n$ Hodge-Tate weights for each embedding $\tau : K \to E$) and an inertial type $\tau_0$ (a representation of $I_K$ with open kernel that extends to $W_K$). There is a unique quotient $R^{\square, \tau_0, \mathbf{v}}$ of $R^\square_{\bar\rho}$ that is reduced and $\ell$-torsion free, such that for each finite extension $E'/E$, a map $x : R^\square_{\bar\rho} \to E'$ factors through it if and only if $\rho_x$ is potentially semistable of Hodge type $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathrm{WD}(\rho_x)|_{I_K} \cong \tau_0$. The same holds with potentially crystalline in place of potentially semistable, with a quotient $R^{\square, \tau_0, \mathbf{v}}_{\mathrm{cr}}$. If the weights are regular (pairwise distinct for each $\tau$), then $\mathrm{Spec}\, R^{\square, \tau_0, \mathbf{v}}[1/\ell]$ is equidimensional of dimension $n^2 + [K : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]\, n(n-1)/2$, and $R^{\square, \tau_0, \mathbf{v}}_{\mathrm{cr}}[1/\ell]$ is regular.

See Kisin, *Potentially semi-stable deformation rings*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 21 (2008), and [p-adic Hodge theory](/learn/padic-hodge/) for the classes of representations. For $\tau_0$ trivial the second ring is the *crystalline lifting ring* $R^{\square, \mathrm{cr}, \mathbf{v}}$.

**Remark.** Since $R^{\square, \mathrm{cr}, \mathbf{v}}[1/\ell]$ is regular, its irreducible components are its connected components. They are not known in general. This is the main difficulty that the last three sections meet: patching proves automorphy on whole irreducible components of a local deformation ring, so one needs an automorphic point on each component that occurs.

**Definition (ordinary lifts).** Let $K$ be finite over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, let $\mathcal{O}_K^\times(\ell)$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$, and let $\Lambda_K = \mathcal{O}[[\mathcal{O}_K^\times(\ell)^n]]$, the *weight space* ring. Through local class field theory, a point of $\Lambda_K$ over $A$ is an $n$-tuple of characters $\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_n$ of the inertia group $I_K$ with values in $A^\times$ that factor through $\mathcal{O}_K^\times(\ell)$. A lift $\rho$ of $\bar\rho$ over such a point is *ordinary* if $A^n$ has a full flag $0 = \mathrm{F}_0 \subset \mathrm{F}_1 \subset \dots \subset \mathrm{F}_n = A^n$ of $G_K$-stable direct summands such that $I_K$ acts on $\mathrm{F}_i/\mathrm{F}_{i-1}$ by $\kappa_i$ times a fixed character with values in the Teichmüller lifts.

Geraghty constructs the *ordinary lifting ring* $R^\triangle_K$, a quotient of $R^\square_{\bar\rho} \hat\otimes_\mathcal{O} \Lambda_K$ whose points over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ are the ordinary lifts, and shows that its irreducible components all have dimension $1 + n^2 + [K : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]\, n(n+1)/2$ (Geraghty, *Modularity lifting theorems for ordinary Galois representations*, Math. Ann. 373, 2019, section 3). Over a regular crystalline point of weight space, the dimension drops by $n[K : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]$ to $1 + n^2 + [K : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]\, n(n-1)/2$, the dimension of Kisin's crystalline ring. Hida families are the global form of this ring ([Galois cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/), section on Hida theory), and the universal ordinary determinant rings of the development volumes are its versions for determinants.

**Definition.** Let $\bar\rho : G_{F,S} \to \mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ be absolutely irreducible. A *global deformation problem* $\mathcal{S}$ is a choice of a local deformation problem $R_v$ for each $v \in S$. A deformation $\rho$ of $\bar\rho$ is *of type* $\mathcal{S}$ if, for each $v \in S$, the restriction $\rho|_{G_{F_v}}$ gives a point of $R_v$. For $T \subseteq S$, a *$T$-framed* deformation is a tuple $(\rho, (\beta_v)_{v \in T})$ with $\beta_v \in \widehat{\mathrm{GL}}_n(A)$, up to the simultaneous action of $\widehat{\mathrm{GL}}_n(A)$ on $\rho$ by conjugation and on each $\beta_v$ by left multiplication.

By Mazur's theorem and the stability in the definition, these functors are pro-represented by rings $R_\mathcal{S}$ and $R^{\square_T}_\mathcal{S}$. The map $(\rho, (\beta_v)) \mapsto (\beta_v^{-1} \rho|_{G_{F_v}} \beta_v)_{v \in T}$ makes $R^{\square_T}_\mathcal{S}$ an algebra over the *local ring* $R^{\mathrm{loc}}_T = \hat\bigotimes_{v \in T} R_v$, the completed tensor product over $\mathcal{O}$.

**Proposition (the tangent space is a Selmer group).** With $L_v$ as above,
$$\begin{aligned} t_{R_\mathcal{S}} &\cong H^1_\mathcal{L}(G_{F,S}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) \\ &= \ker\Big(H^1(G_{F,S}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho) \to \prod_{v \in S} H^1(G_{F_v}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)/L_v\Big). \end{aligned}$$

*Proof.* By the proposition on tangent spaces, a deformation of $\bar\rho$ to $k[\varepsilon]$ is a class $[c] \in H^1(G_{F,S}, \mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho)$. Its restriction to $G_{F_v}$ is a point of $R_v$ if and only if the restricted cocycle lies in the tangent space of $R_v$, which contains the coboundaries; so the condition is $\mathrm{res}_v [c] \in L_v$. $\square$

This is a [[Selmer group|36YNSTESMQ]] in the sense of [Galois cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/), and its size is controlled by the dual Selmer group of $\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho(1)$ through the Greenberg-Wiles formula. With framings, the same argument shows that $R^{\square_S}_\mathcal{S}$ is a quotient of a power series ring $R^{\mathrm{loc}}_S[[x_1, \dots, x_g]]$, where the relative tangent space has dimension $g$, and Poitou-Tate duality bounds the number of relations by the dimension of a dual Selmer group (Clozel, Harris and Taylor, 2008, section 2). This presentation is the starting point of patching.

**Proposition (points in characteristic zero).** Let $x : R_\mathcal{S} \to E'$ be a point, $\mathfrak{p}_x$ its kernel and $\rho_x$ the corresponding representation. Suppose that $R_v$ is the unramified quotient at each $v \in S$ with $v \nmid \ell$ and the crystalline quotient $R^{\square, \mathrm{cr}, \mathbf{v}}$ at each $v \mid \ell$. Then
$$\mathrm{Hom}_{E'}(\mathfrak{p}_x/\mathfrak{p}_x^2 \otimes E', E') \cong H^1_f(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\rho_x),$$
the Bloch-Kato Selmer group of the adjoint representation.

This follows from the proposition above applied over $E'[\varepsilon]$ and the description of the tangent spaces of the local rings; see Kisin (2008) at $v \mid \ell$, and Allen, *Deformations of polarized automorphic Galois representations and adjoint Selmer groups*, Duke Math. J. 165 (2016), for the polarized case. So a statement $R = \mathbb{T}$ gives information on adjoint Selmer groups (see the section on Hecke algebras).

## Determinants and residually reducible representations

When $\bar\rho$ is reducible, $D_{\bar\rho}$ is not pro-representable and its deformations are hard to control. The right object is then the characteristic polynomial of a representation, not the representation itself.

**Definition (Taylor).** Let $A$ be a commutative ring in which $d!$ is invertible and $G$ a group. A *pseudocharacter* of dimension $d$ is a function $T : G \to A$ with $T(1) = d$, $T(gh) = T(hg)$, and
$$\sum_{\sigma \in S_{d+1}} \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\, T_\sigma(g_1, \dots, g_{d+1}) = 0 \quad \text{for all } g_1, \dots, g_{d+1} \in G,$$
where $T_\sigma(g_1, \dots, g_{d+1}) = \prod_{(i_1 \cdots i_r)} T(g_{i_1} \cdots g_{i_r})$, the product over the cycles of $\sigma$.

The trace of a representation $G \to \mathrm{GL}_d(A)$ is a pseudocharacter: the identity is the Cayley-Hamilton theorem in polarized form.

**Definition (Chenevier).** Let $A$ be a commutative ring and $G$ a group. A *determinant* of dimension $d$ of $G$ with values in $A$ is a collection of maps $D_B : B[G] \to B$, one for each commutative $A$-algebra $B$, natural in $B$, such that $D_B(1) = 1$, $D_B(xy) = D_B(x) D_B(y)$ and $D_B(bx) = b^d D_B(x)$ for $b \in B$ and $x, y \in B[G]$. Its *characteristic polynomial* at $g \in G$ is $D_{A[t]}(t - g)$.

For a representation $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_d(A)$, the maps $\det \circ \rho$ form a determinant. Chenevier proves:

1. if $d!$ is invertible in $A$, then $D \mapsto$ (the coefficient of $-t^{d-1}$ in the characteristic polynomial) is a bijection from determinants of dimension $d$ to pseudocharacters of dimension $d$;
2. if $A = \bar k$ is an algebraically closed field, then $\rho \mapsto \det \circ \rho$ is a bijection from isomorphism classes of semisimple representations $G \to \mathrm{GL}_d(\bar k)$ to determinants of dimension $d$;
3. for $G$ profinite with $\Phi_\ell$ and a continuous determinant $\bar D$ over $k$, the functor of continuous determinants lifting $\bar D$ is pro-represented by a ring $R_{\bar D} \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$, the *universal determinant ring*.

See Chenevier, *The p-adic analytic space of pseudocharacters of a profinite group, and pseudorepresentations over arbitrary rings*, in *Automorphic Forms and Galois Representations*, vol. 1, LMS Lecture Note Ser. 414 (2014). Part 2 is the Brauer-Nesbitt theorem in any characteristic, and it is why determinants, not pseudocharacters, are used when $\ell \le d$.

**Theorem (Carayol; Nyssen and Rouquier).** Let $A \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ and $G$ profinite.

1. Let $\rho_1, \rho_2 : G \to \mathrm{GL}_d(A)$ be continuous with $\rho_1 \bmod \mathfrak{m}_A$ absolutely irreducible and $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_1 = \mathrm{tr}\,\rho_2$. Then $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ are conjugate by an element of $\mathrm{GL}_d(A)$ (Carayol, *Formes modulaires et représentations galoisiennes à valeurs dans un anneau local complet*, Contemp. Math. 165, 1994).
2. Let $T : G \to A$ be a continuous pseudocharacter of dimension $d$ whose reduction is the trace of an absolutely irreducible $\bar\rho$. Then $T = \mathrm{tr}\,\rho$ for a continuous $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_d(A)$ lifting $\bar\rho$ (Nyssen, *Pseudo-représentations*, Math. Ann. 306, 1996; Rouquier, *Caractérisation des caractères et pseudo-caractères*, J. Algebra 180, 1996). Chenevier (2014) proves the same for determinants without the condition on $d!$.

**Corollary.** If $\bar\rho$ is absolutely irreducible, then $R_{\bar\rho} \cong R_{\det \circ \bar\rho}$.

*Proof.* By the theorem, the map $\rho \mapsto \det \circ \rho$ from deformations of $\bar\rho$ to determinants lifting $\det \circ \bar\rho$ is a bijection over each $A \in \mathrm{Art}_\mathcal{O}$. The two rings pro-represent isomorphic functors. $\square$

So the universal determinant ring extends the universal deformation ring to residually reducible $\bar\rho$, where the residual determinant still makes sense. This is the role of the universal ordinary determinant rings in the Hida settings of the development volumes.

**Theorem (Taylor; limits of pseudocharacters).** Let $T_i : G \to \overline{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell$ be continuous pseudocharacters of dimension $d$ that converge pointwise to $T : G \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, with values in a fixed finite extension. Then $T$ is a continuous pseudocharacter, and there is a unique semisimple continuous $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_d(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ with $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho = T$.

The first claim is immediate, since the defining identities are closed conditions. The second is Taylor, *Galois representations associated to Siegel modular forms of low weight*, Duke Math. J. 63 (1991), section 1. This is the tool of all constructions by congruences: if the Hecke eigenvalues of an automorphic representation are $\ell$-adic limits of eigenvalues that have Galois representations, then it has one too.

**Definition.** Let $\bar D = \bar D_1 \bar D_2$ be a product of determinants of absolutely irreducible, non-isomorphic representations $\bar\rho_1, \bar\rho_2$ over $k$, and let $D$ be a determinant over $A \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ lifting $\bar D$. The *reducibility ideal* of $D$ is the smallest ideal $I \subset A$ such that $D \otimes A/I = D_1 D_2$ for determinants $D_i$ over $A/I$ lifting $\bar D_i$.

Bellaïche and Chenevier show that it exists and describe the deformations near the reducible point by a generalized matrix algebra $\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & A \end{pmatrix}$, and the reducibility ideal is the image of $B \otimes C$ (Bellaïche and Chenevier, *Families of Galois representations and Selmer groups*, Astérisque 324, 2009, chapter 1).

**Theorem (Ribet's lemma).** Let $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}_2(E)$ be continuous and irreducible, with $\bar\rho^{\mathrm{ss}} \cong \bar\psi_1 \oplus \bar\psi_2$ for characters $\bar\psi_1, \bar\psi_2$. Then there is a $G$-stable $\mathcal{O}$-lattice in $E^2$ whose reduction is a non-split extension $\begin{pmatrix} \bar\psi_1 & * \\ 0 & \bar\psi_2 \end{pmatrix}$.

See Ribet, *A modular construction of unramified p-extensions of $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p)$*, Invent. Math. 34 (1976). It turns a congruence between a cusp form and an Eisenstein series into a nonzero class in $H^1(G, \bar\psi_1 \bar\psi_2^{-1})$.

## Galois representations from Shimura varieties

The Galois representations that automorphy lifting starts from are found in the étale cohomology of Shimura varieties ([geometry](/learn/geometry/), [étale cohomology](/learn/etale-cohomology/)). The simplest case is the modular curve.

**Theorem (Eichler-Shimura congruence relation).** Let $N \ge 1$, let $p \nmid N$ be prime, and let $J_0(N)$ be the Jacobian of $X_0(N)$, which has good reduction at $p$. In $\mathrm{End}(J_0(N)_{\mathbb{F}_p})$,
$$T_p = \mathrm{Frob}_p + \mathrm{Ver}_p,$$
where $T_p$ is the reduction of the [[Hecke operator|S4SV3QZ8B1]], $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is the Frobenius endomorphism and $\mathrm{Ver}_p = p\, \mathrm{Frob}_p^{-1}$ is its dual isogeny.

See Shimura, *Introduction to the Arithmetic Theory of Automorphic Functions*, chapter 7, and Diamond and Shurman, *A First Course in Modular Forms*, chapter 8.

**Corollary.** Let $f$ be a newform of weight $2$ and level $\Gamma_0(N)$ with rational coefficients $a_p$, and $A_f$ the corresponding elliptic curve quotient of $J_0(N)$. For $p \nmid N\ell$, the arithmetic Frobenius at $p$ acting on the Tate module $V_\ell(A_f)$ has characteristic polynomial $X^2 - a_p X + p$.

*Proof.* The reduction map is injective on $\ell$-power torsion and identifies $V_\ell(A_f)$ with $V_\ell$ of the reduction, compatibly with Frobenius. On it, $\mathrm{Frob}_p + p\,\mathrm{Frob}_p^{-1} = a_p$, so $\mathrm{Frob}_p^2 - a_p \mathrm{Frob}_p + p = 0$. The Weil pairing gives determinant $p$, so this is the characteristic polynomial. $\square$

**Theorem (Deligne).** Let $f$ be a newform of weight $k \ge 2$, level $N$ and character $\chi$, with coefficient field $K_f$, and let $\lambda \mid \ell$ be a place of $K_f$. There is a continuous irreducible $\rho_{f,\lambda} : G_\mathbb{Q} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(K_{f,\lambda})$, unramified outside $N\ell$, with $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_{f,\lambda}(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = a_p$ and $\det \rho_{f,\lambda}(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = \chi(p) p^{k-1}$ for $p \nmid N\ell$.

See Deligne, *Formes modulaires et représentations $\ell$-adiques*, Séminaire Bourbaki 355 (1969). The representation lives in $H^{k-1}$ of the *Kuga-Sato variety*, a desingularization of the $(k-2)$-fold fibre power of the universal elliptic curve, and the congruence relation on it gives the characteristic polynomial. For weight $1$, Deligne and Serre (*Formes modulaires de poids 1*, Ann. Sci. ENS 7, 1974) obtain $\rho_f$ by congruences with forms of higher weight.

For $\mathrm{GL}_n$ with $n \ge 3$, there are no Shimura varieties for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ itself. One uses the cohomology of a [[Shimura variety|QT4CF7ZKK5]] attached to a unitary group and transfers automorphic representations between unitary groups and $\mathrm{GL}_n$ by base change ([automorphic forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/), [trace formula](/learn/trace-formula/)).

**Definition.** Let $F$ be a CM field, $F^+$ its maximal totally real subfield, and $n \ge 2$. Let $B$ be a central division algebra over $F$ of dimension $n^2$ with an involution of the second kind, and $G$ the corresponding group of unitary similitudes over $\mathbb{Q}$. We say $G$ is of *Harris-Taylor type* if the unitary group at one real place of $F^+$ has signature $(1, n-1)$ and at the other real places signature $(0, n)$.

For such $G$ the Shimura varieties $\mathrm{Sh}_K$ are proper and smooth of dimension $n - 1$ over $F$ and are moduli spaces of abelian varieties with additional structure (PEL type). An irreducible algebraic representation $\xi$ of $G$ gives a lisse $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$-sheaf $\mathcal{L}_\xi$, and the cohomology decomposes as
$$\varinjlim_K H^{n-1}(\mathrm{Sh}_{K, \bar F}, \mathcal{L}_\xi) = \bigoplus_{\pi} \pi^\infty \otimes R_\ell(\pi),$$
a sum over automorphic representations $\pi$ of $G$, where $R_\ell(\pi)$ is a finite-dimensional representation of $G_F$. Kottwitz computed $R_\ell(\pi)$ at the places of good reduction by counting points over finite fields and comparing with the trace formula (Kottwitz, *On the $\lambda$-adic representations associated to some simple Shimura varieties*, Invent. Math. 108, 1992).

**Theorem (Harris and Taylor; Taylor and Yoshida).** Let $F$ be a CM field and $\Pi$ a cuspidal automorphic representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ with $\Pi^\vee \cong \Pi^c$, regular algebraic at infinity, and square integrable at some finite place. Then there is a continuous semisimple $r_{\ell,\iota}(\Pi) : G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ such that, for each finite place $v \nmid \ell$,
$$\iota\, \mathrm{WD}(r_{\ell,\iota}(\Pi)|_{G_{F_v}})^{\mathrm{F\text{-}ss}} \cong \mathrm{rec}_{F_v}(\Pi_v \otimes |{\det}|_v^{(1-n)/2}).$$

See Harris and Taylor, *The Geometry and Cohomology of Some Simple Shimura Varieties*, Annals of Math. Studies 151 (2001), which proves this up to semisimplification on the left and proves the [[local Langlands correspondence|9E45NBX1G0]] for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ in the same work, and Taylor and Yoshida, *Compatibility of local and global Langlands correspondences*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 20 (2007), for the monodromy operator. Harris and Taylor find $r_{\ell,\iota}(\Pi)$, up to a twist, inside $R_\ell(\pi)$ for a $\pi$ on $G$ whose base change is $\Pi$.

**Remark (removing the hypotheses).** Shin (*Galois representations arising from some compact Shimura varieties*, Ann. of Math. 173, 2011) removes the condition of square integrability, with an extra regularity condition at infinity when $n$ is even. Chenevier and Harris (*Construction of automorphic Galois representations II*, Camb. J. Math. 1, 2013) remove that condition by $\ell$-adic congruences, which give local-global compatibility at $v \nmid \ell$ up to semisimplification. Caraiani (Duke Math. J. 161, 2012, and Algebra Number Theory 8, 2014) proves full local-global compatibility at $v \nmid \ell$ and at $v \mid \ell$, and Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor (*Local-global compatibility for $l = p$*, I and II) prove the potentially semistable property and the Hodge-Tate weights at $v \mid \ell$. The full statement is in [Galois representations](/learn/galois-representations/), and it is the content of [Row0b](/step/row0b-def/).

The proof of the row follows this shape. It obtains Galois representations for automorphic functions on unitary groups with conditions at auxiliary places from the geometry of towers of moduli spaces (`SeedFinOdd`), and then reaches all Hecke eigensystems by congruences (`CongFinOdd`), as in Chenevier and Harris. See [the Galois row](/proof/galois-row/).

## Hecke algebras and R = T

Let $F$ be an imaginary CM field with $F/F^+$ unramified at all finite places, and $G$ a unitary group over $F^+$ that becomes $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over $F$, is quasi-split at each finite place, and has $G(F^+_v)$ compact at each real place $v$; see Clozel, Harris and Taylor (2008), section 3, for when such $G$ exists. Such a [[unitary group|0R9R0Y1HDK]] is *definite*. Its automorphic forms are functions on a finite set, which makes it the simplest setting for patching.

**Definition.** Let $U = \prod_v U_v \subset G(\mathbb{A}^\infty_{F^+})$ be compact open, and let $W$ be a finite $\mathcal{O}$-module with a continuous action of $U_\ell = \prod_{v \mid \ell} U_v$. The space of *algebraic modular forms* is
$$S(U, W) = \{ f : G(F^+) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}^\infty_{F^+}) \to W \;:\; f(gu) = u_\ell^{-1} f(g) \text{ for } u \in U \}.$$
We call $U$ *sufficiently small* if $G(F^+) \cap t U t^{-1}$ has order prime to $\ell$ for each $t$.

The double coset space $G(F^+) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}^\infty_{F^+}) / U$ is finite, so $S(U, W)$ is a finite $\mathcal{O}$-module. If $U$ is sufficiently small, $W \mapsto S(U, W)$ is exact, so $S(U, W)$ is free when $W$ is and $S(U, W) \otimes_\mathcal{O} A = S(U, W \otimes_\mathcal{O} A)$. When $W$ is a lattice in an algebraic representation of $\mathrm{Res}_{F^+/\mathbb{Q}} G$ restricted to $U_\ell$, the space $S(U, W) \otimes_{\mathcal{O}, \iota} \mathbb{C}$ is $\bigoplus_\pi (\pi^\infty)^U$ over the automorphic representations $\pi$ of $G$ with $\pi_\infty$ determined by $W$, with multiplicities (Clozel, Harris and Taylor, 2008, section 3).

**Definition.** Let $S$ be a finite set of places of $F^+$ that split in $F$, containing the places above $\ell$ and those where $U_v$ is not hyperspecial. For $v \notin S$ split as $w w^c$ in $F$, identify $G(F^+_v) \cong \mathrm{GL}_n(F_w)$ so that $U_v = \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_{F_w})$, and let $T_w^{(j)}$ be the [[Hecke operator|S4SV3QZ8B1]] of the double coset of $\mathrm{diag}(\varpi_w 1_j, 1_{n-j})$, for $1 \le j \le n$. The *Hecke algebra* $\mathbb{T}^S(U, W)$ is the $\mathcal{O}$-subalgebra of $\mathrm{End}_\mathcal{O}(S(U, W))$ generated by the $T_w^{(j)}$ and $(T_w^{(n)})^{-1}$.

It is commutative and finite over $\mathcal{O}$, so it is the product of its localizations $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ at its finitely many maximal ideals. This is a global version of the local [[Hecke algebra|5J2P2AGY4N]] of [automorphic forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/).

**Theorem.** Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a maximal ideal of $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{T}^S(U, W)$.

1. There is a continuous semisimple $\bar r_\mathfrak{m} : G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m})$, unramified outside the places above $S$, with $\bar r_\mathfrak{m}^c \cong \bar r_\mathfrak{m}^\vee \otimes \bar\varepsilon_\ell^{1-n}$ and, for each split $w$ above $v \notin S$,
$$\det\big(X - \bar r_\mathfrak{m}(\mathrm{Frob}_w)\big) = \sum_{j=0}^{n} (-1)^j (\mathbf{N}w)^{j(j-1)/2}\, T_w^{(j)} X^{n-j} \bmod \mathfrak{m}.$$
2. If $\bar r_\mathfrak{m}$ is absolutely irreducible ($\mathfrak{m}$ is *non-Eisenstein*), it lifts to a continuous $r_\mathfrak{m} : G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m})$ with the same characteristic polynomials over $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$, and $r_\mathfrak{m}$ extends to a homomorphism $G_{F^+} \to \mathcal{G}_n(\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m})$.

See Clozel, Harris and Taylor (2008), section 3. *Sketch.* For $\pi$ contributing to $S(U, W) \otimes \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, base change to $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ (Labesse, *Changement de base CM et séries discrètes*, in *On the Stabilization of the Trace Formula*, 2011) and the theorems of the previous section give $r_\pi$. Since $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ is reduced after inverting $\ell$ and finite over $\mathcal{O}$, it embeds in $\prod_\pi \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ over the $\pi$ with eigensystem in $\mathfrak{m}$. The product of the $\det \circ r_\pi$ takes values in $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ on Frobenius elements by the formula, hence on all of $G_F$ by the [[Chebotarev|X8VKW5Y1CZ]] density theorem and continuity. This is a determinant over $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$, and the theorem of Carayol, Nyssen and Rouquier gives $r_\mathfrak{m}$.

**Definition.** Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be non-Eisenstein and let $\mathcal{S}$ be a polarized deformation problem for $\bar r_\mathfrak{m}$ such that $r_\pi$ is of type $\mathcal{S}$ for each $\pi$ that contributes to $S(U, W)_\mathfrak{m}$. Then $r_\mathfrak{m}$ gives a map $R_\mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$. An *$R = \mathbb{T}$ theorem* says that it is an isomorphism, or that its kernel is nilpotent ($R_\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{red}} = \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$).

**Proposition.** The map $R_\mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ is surjective.

*Proof.* The image is closed, since $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ is finite over $\mathcal{O}$ and $R_\mathcal{S}$ is complete. It contains the coefficients of the characteristic polynomials of $r_\mathfrak{m}(\mathrm{Frob}_w)$, which are $\pm (\mathbf{N}w)^{j(j-1)/2} T_w^{(j)}$. Since $\mathbf{N}w$ is a unit in $\mathcal{O}$, it contains the generators $T_w^{(j)}$, and with $T_w^{(n)}$ its inverse. $\square$

**Corollary (automorphy from $R = \mathbb{T}$).** Suppose $R_\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{red}} = \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$. Let $\rho : G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}')$ be a lift of $\bar r_\mathfrak{m}$ of type $\mathcal{S}$, over the ring of integers of a finite extension of $E$. Then $\rho \cong r_\pi$ for an automorphic $\pi$ of $G$, so $\rho$ is automorphic.

*Proof.* The point $R_\mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{O}'$ of $\rho$ kills the nilradical, since $\mathcal{O}'$ is reduced, so it factors through $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$. A map $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m} \to \mathcal{O}' \subset \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ is a Hecke eigensystem in $S(U, W)_\mathfrak{m} \otimes \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, so it belongs to some $\pi$. Then $\rho$ and $r_\pi$ have the same characteristic polynomials at almost all Frobenius elements. By Chebotarev and the Brauer-Nesbitt theorem their semisimplifications agree, and $\rho$ is irreducible since $\bar r_\mathfrak{m}$ is. $\square$

This is how a [[modularity lifting|VQ8XNV47K9]] theorem is proved: one proves $R = \mathbb{T}$. The first method was a numerical criterion.

**Theorem (Wiles' numerical criterion, with Lenstra).** Let $\mathbb{T} \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ be finite and flat over $\mathcal{O}$, with a surjection $\pi_\mathbb{T} : \mathbb{T} \to \mathcal{O}$. Define the *congruence ideal* $\eta_\mathbb{T} = \pi_\mathbb{T}(\mathrm{Ann}_\mathbb{T}(\ker \pi_\mathbb{T})) \subseteq \mathcal{O}$, and assume $\eta_\mathbb{T} \ne 0$. Let $\varphi : R \to \mathbb{T}$ be a surjection in $\mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$, put $\pi_R = \pi_\mathbb{T} \circ \varphi$ and $\Phi_R = \ker \pi_R / (\ker \pi_R)^2$. Then $\#\Phi_R \ge \#\mathcal{O}/\eta_\mathbb{T}$. If equality holds with both sides finite, then $\varphi$ is an isomorphism and $R$ and $\mathbb{T}$ are complete intersections.

See Wiles, *Modular elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem*, Ann. of Math. 141 (1995), and Lenstra, *Complete intersections and Gorenstein rings*, in *Elliptic Curves, Modular Forms and Fermat's Last Theorem* (1995); Darmon, Diamond and Taylor give a full account in the chapter on commutative algebra. For $R$ a deformation ring and $\pi_R$ the point of a newform $f$, the Pontryagin dual of $\Phi_R$ is a Selmer group of $\mathrm{ad}^0 \rho_f \otimes E/\mathcal{O}$, and $\eta_\mathbb{T}$ measures congruences between $f$ and other forms.

**Corollary (adjoint Selmer groups vanish).** Suppose that $R_\mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ is an isomorphism and that the local conditions of $\mathcal{S}$ are as in the proposition on points in characteristic zero. Then $H^1_f(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\rho_x) = 0$ for each point $x$ of $R_\mathcal{S}[1/\ell]$.

*Proof.* The Hecke operators act on $S(U, W) \otimes \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell = \bigoplus_\pi (\pi^\infty)^U$ by scalars on each summand, so $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}[1/\ell]$ is reduced, and it is finite over $E$. So it is a finite product of finite field extensions of $E$. Then the tangent space of $R_\mathcal{S}[1/\ell] \cong \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}[1/\ell]$ at $x$ is $0$, and the proposition on points in characteristic zero identifies it with $H^1_f(F, \mathrm{ad}\,\rho_x)$. $\square$

The development volumes use this in its ordinary form: at an automorphic point of a Hida family, each ordinary cocycle of the adjoint is a coboundary, because a tangent vector that does not move the traces is $0$.

## Taylor-Wiles-Kisin patching

Taylor and Wiles add auxiliary primes that kill the dual Selmer group, so that the deformation rings have a fixed number of generators, and pass to a limit in which the Hecke module becomes free over a power series ring. We first give the original case.

**Definition.** Let $\bar\rho : G_{\mathbb{Q},S} \to \mathrm{GL}_2(k)$ be as in the example of the first section, and $N \ge 1$. A *Taylor-Wiles prime* of level $N$ is a prime $q \notin S$ with $q \equiv 1 \pmod{\ell^N}$ such that $\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$ has distinct eigenvalues in $k$. For a finite set $Q$ of them, the problem $\mathcal{S}_Q$ adds the condition at each $q \in Q$ that all lifts are allowed. Let $\Delta_Q$ be the maximal quotient of $\prod_{q \in Q} (\mathbb{Z}/q)^\times$ of exponent $\ell^N$, and $\mathfrak{a}_Q$ the augmentation ideal of $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$.

At $q \in Q$, each lift is a sum $\chi_{1,q} \oplus \chi_{2,q}$ of two characters, by Hensel's lemma applied to the distinct eigenvalues of Frobenius. The tame inertia character $\chi_{1,q} \circ \mathrm{Art}_{\mathbb{Q}_q}$ restricted to $\mathbb{Z}_q^\times$ makes $R_{\mathcal{S}_Q}$ an $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$-algebra, and $R_{\mathcal{S}_Q}/\mathfrak{a}_Q = R_\mathcal{S}$, since a deformation of type $\mathcal{S}_Q$ with $\chi_{1,q}$ unramified is unramified at $q$.

**Proposition (Taylor and Wiles).** Let $\ell$ be odd and suppose that $\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell^*})}}$ is absolutely irreducible, where $\ell^* = (-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell$. Suppose that the local conditions $L_v$ of $\mathcal{S}$ satisfy $\dim L_\ell = 1 + h^0(G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)$ and $\dim L_v = h^0(G_{\mathbb{Q}_v}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho)$ for $v \ne \ell, \infty$. Let $r = \dim_k H^1_{\mathcal{L}^*}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\bar\rho(1))$. Then for each $N$ there is a set $Q_N$ of $r$ Taylor-Wiles primes of level $N$ such that $R_{\mathcal{S}_{Q_N}}$ is a quotient of $\mathcal{O}[[x_1, \dots, x_r]]$.

*Proof.* First the count. Put $Q$ a set of Taylor-Wiles primes. At $q \in Q$, $L_q = H^1(\mathbb{Q}_q, \mathrm{ad}^0)$, and by the local Euler characteristic formula and local duality, $\#L_q / \#H^0(\mathbb{Q}_q, \mathrm{ad}^0) = \#H^0(\mathbb{Q}_q, \mathrm{ad}^0(1)) = \#k$, because $\bar\varepsilon_\ell$ is trivial on $G_{\mathbb{Q}_q}$ and $\bar\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$ has distinct eigenvalues. At $\infty$ the factor is $1/\#H^0(\mathbb{R}, \mathrm{ad}^0) = (\#k)^{-1}$, at $\ell$ it is $\#k$, at the other $v \in S$ it is $1$, and both global $H^0$ vanish under the hypothesis. The Greenberg-Wiles formula ([Galois cohomology](/learn/galois-cohomology/)) gives
$$\dim H^1_{\mathcal{L}_Q}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0) = \dim H^1_{\mathcal{L}_Q^*}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0(1)) + \#Q.$$
Since $L_q^\perp = 0$, the dual Selmer group for $Q$ is the subgroup of classes in $H^1_{\mathcal{L}^*}$ that vanish at each $q \in Q$. Second the choice: for a nonzero class $\psi$ in the dual Selmer group, the hypothesis on $\bar\rho$ and the Chebotarev density theorem give a Taylor-Wiles prime $q$ of level $N$ with $\mathrm{res}_q \psi \ne 0$ (Taylor and Wiles; Darmon, Diamond and Taylor, chapter on Hecke algebras). Adding $q$ lowers the dimension of the dual Selmer group by one. After $r$ steps it is $0$, and $\dim H^1_{\mathcal{L}_Q} = r$, which is the number of generators of $\mathfrak{m}/(\mathfrak{m}^2, \lambda)$. $\square$

On the Hecke side, the modules $M_Q$ of modular forms with auxiliary level at $Q$ are modules over $R_{\mathcal{S}_Q}$, finite free over $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$ through the diamond operators, with $M_Q / \mathfrak{a}_Q M_Q \cong M$, the module at the original level (Darmon, Diamond and Taylor, chapter on Hecke algebras; Diamond, 1997).

**Theorem (patching; Taylor and Wiles, in the form of Diamond and Fujiwara).** Let $R \to \mathbb{T}$ be a surjection in $\mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ and $M$ a faithful $\mathbb{T}$-module, finite free over $\mathcal{O}$. Let $r \ge 0$. Suppose that for each $N \ge 1$ there are a group $\Delta_N \cong (\mathbb{Z}/\ell^N)^r$, an $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_N]$-algebra $R_N$ that is a quotient of $\mathcal{O}[[x_1, \dots, x_r]]$ with $R_N / \mathfrak{a}_N R_N \cong R$, and an $R_N$-module $M_N$, finite free over $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_N]$, with $M_N / \mathfrak{a}_N M_N \cong M$ compatibly with $R_N \to R$. Then $R \to \mathbb{T}$ is an isomorphism, $\mathbb{T}$ is a complete intersection, and $M$ is free over $\mathbb{T}$.

See Diamond, *The Taylor-Wiles construction and multiplicity one*, Invent. Math. 128 (1997), and Fujiwara, *Deformation rings and Hecke algebras in the totally real case* (preprint, 1996). *Sketch.* Put $S_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[y_1, \dots, y_r]]$ and $R_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[x_1, \dots, x_r]]$, and map $S_\infty \to \mathcal{O}[\Delta_N]$ by sending $1 + y_i$ to the generators. The data at level $N$, reduced modulo $\lambda^N$ and the $N$-th powers of the maximal ideals, take finitely many values up to isomorphism, so a compatible subsequence exists, and its limit is an $R_\infty$-module $M_\infty$, finite free over $S_\infty$, with $M_\infty / \mathfrak{a} M_\infty \cong M$ for $\mathfrak{a} = (y_1, \dots, y_r)$, and with $S_\infty$ acting through a map $S_\infty \to R_\infty$. A regular sequence $\lambda, y_1, \dots, y_r$ for $M_\infty$ over $S_\infty$ maps into $\mathfrak{m}_{R_\infty}$, so $\mathrm{depth}_{R_\infty} M_\infty \ge r + 1 = \dim R_\infty$. By the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula ([commutative algebra](/learn/commutative-algebra/)), $M_\infty$ is free over the regular ring $R_\infty$. So $M = M_\infty/\mathfrak{a}M_\infty$ is free over $R_\infty/\mathfrak{a}R_\infty$, which therefore acts faithfully on $M$. The maps $R_\infty/\mathfrak{a}R_\infty \to R \to \mathbb{T} \to \mathrm{End}_\mathcal{O}(M)$ have an injective composite and the first two are surjective, so all are isomorphisms. $\square$

**Example (the numbers must match).** The proof works because $\dim R_\infty = \dim S_\infty$: the number $r$ of Taylor-Wiles primes is both the number of generators of $R_N$ and the number of variables from the diamond operators. In the proposition, the factors at $\ell$ and at $\infty$ cancel. For the polarized problems of unitary groups the same cancellation holds with $[F^+ : \mathbb{Q}]\, n(n-1)/2$ in place of $1$: the Hodge-Tate condition at the places above $\ell$ adds this number (Kisin's theorem), and the oddness of the polarization at the real places takes it away. For $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over a field that is not totally real or CM, the numbers do not match. Calegari and Geraghty treat this case with complexes of Hecke modules (*Modularity lifting beyond the Taylor-Wiles method*, Invent. Math. 211, 2018).

Kisin changed the method in two ways: he framed the deformations at the places of $S$, so that the local rings enter as $R^{\mathrm{loc}}$, and he allowed $R^{\mathrm{loc}}$ to be any ring of the previous sections, not a power series ring. Then $R_\infty = R^{\mathrm{loc}}[[x_1, \dots, x_g]]$ is not regular, $M_\infty$ need not be free over it, and one uses the following lemma in place of Auslander-Buchsbaum.

**Lemma.** Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring and $M$ a finite $A$-module with $\mathrm{depth}_A M \ge \dim A$. Then each associated prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $M$ is a minimal prime of $A$ with $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$. So the support of $M$ is a union of irreducible components of $\mathrm{Spec}\, A$ of maximal dimension.

*Proof.* For $\mathfrak{p} \in \mathrm{Ass}(M)$ we have $\mathrm{depth}\, M \le \dim A/\mathfrak{p}$ (Matsumura, *Commutative Ring Theory*, Theorem 17.2). So $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} \ge \dim A$, which forces $\dim A/\mathfrak{p} = \dim A$ and $\mathfrak{p}$ minimal. The support of $M$ is the union of the closed sets $V(\mathfrak{p})$ over the minimal elements of $\mathrm{Ass}(M)$. $\square$

The [[depth|M2RADBFW99]] here is that of [commutative algebra](/learn/commutative-algebra/), and the [[Krull dimension|EX7N9D1YCD]] is the dimension of $A$.

**Theorem (Kisin's form of patching).** Let $R^{\mathrm{loc}} \in \mathrm{CNL}_\mathcal{O}$ be $\ell$-torsion free, $R_\infty = R^{\mathrm{loc}}[[x_1, \dots, x_g]]$, and $S_\infty = \mathcal{O}[[y_1, \dots, y_h]]$ with $\dim R_\infty = \dim S_\infty = 1 + h$. Let $M_\infty$ be a nonzero $R_\infty$-module, finite free over $S_\infty$ through a map $S_\infty \to R_\infty$. Let $\mathfrak{a} = (y_1, \dots, y_h)$ and suppose that $R_\infty / \mathfrak{a} R_\infty \to R_\mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ are surjective and $M_\infty / \mathfrak{a} M_\infty \cong M$, a faithful $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$-module, compatibly. Then $\mathrm{Supp}_{R_\infty} M_\infty$ is a union of irreducible components of $\mathrm{Spec}\, R_\infty$. If $\mathrm{Spec}\, R_\infty$ is irreducible, then $R_\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{red}} = \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$.

*Proof.* As in the previous proof, $\mathrm{depth}_{R_\infty} M_\infty \ge 1 + h = \dim R_\infty$, and the lemma gives the first claim. If $\mathrm{Spec}\, R_\infty$ is irreducible, the support of $M_\infty$ is all of it, so the support of $M_\infty / \mathfrak{a} M_\infty$ over $R_\infty/\mathfrak{a}R_\infty$ is all of $\mathrm{Spec}\, R_\infty/\mathfrak{a} R_\infty$. This module is $M$, on which $R_\infty/\mathfrak{a}R_\infty$ acts through $R_\mathcal{S}$ and then through $\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$. So $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m} = \mathrm{Supp}\, M$ contains all of $\mathrm{Spec}\, R_\mathcal{S}$, and the kernel of $R_\mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{T}_\mathfrak{m}$ lies in every prime, so it is nilpotent. $\square$

See Kisin, *Moduli of finite flat group schemes and modularity*, Ann. of Math. 170 (2009). In general $R^{\mathrm{loc}}$ has several components, and the theorem gives automorphy of lifts on those components of $\mathrm{Spec}\, R_\infty$ that meet the support of $M_\infty$. To reach a given component one needs one automorphic point on it.

**Remark (Ihara avoidance).** At a place $v \nmid \ell$ the local ring can have several components. Taylor (Publ. Math. IHÉS 108, 2008) patches two problems that agree modulo $\lambda$, one with irreducible local rings, and compares their supports modulo $\lambda$. This removes the condition of minimal ramification of Clozel, Harris and Taylor.

**Definition (adequate subgroups).** Let $k$ have characteristic $\ell$ and $H \subset \mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ a finite subgroup acting absolutely irreducibly on $V = k^n$. Then $H$ is *adequate* if $H^1(H, k) = 0$, $H^1(H, \mathrm{ad}\, V) = 0$, and $\mathrm{End}_k(V)$ is spanned over $k$ by the semisimple elements of $H$.

This is the condition under which Taylor-Wiles places exist in the unitary setting: with $\bar\rho(G_{F(\zeta_\ell)})$ adequate and $\zeta_\ell$ not in the field cut out by $\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho$, the argument of the proposition goes through. See Thorne, *On the automorphy of l-adic Galois representations with small residual image*, J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 11 (2012), and its appendix by Guralnick, Herzig, Taylor and Thorne, which shows that an absolutely irreducible $H$ is adequate when $\ell \ge 2(n+1)$.

## Automorphy lifting for unitary groups

**Definition.** Let $F$ be a number field. A continuous $\rho : G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ is *automorphic* if there is a cuspidal (or isobaric) automorphic representation $\Pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ with $\rho \cong r_{\ell,\iota}(\Pi)$, that is, with matching Frobenius characteristic polynomials at almost all places.

Patching over a definite unitary group, with base change between the unitary group and $\mathrm{GL}_n$ over $F$, gives automorphy lifting for polarized representations of $G_F$ with $F$ CM. The main theorem of Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor, with its five hypotheses, is stated in [Galois representations](/learn/galois-representations/). Its hypothesis at $\ell$ uses the following notion.

**Definition.** Let $K$ be finite over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ and let $\rho_1, \rho_2 : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell)$ be crystalline with the same Hodge-Tate weights and with isomorphic reductions. We say that $\rho_1$ *connects to* $\rho_2$, written $\rho_1 \sim \rho_2$, if they give points of the same irreducible component of $\mathrm{Spec}\, R^{\square, \mathrm{cr}, \mathbf{v}} \otimes \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. A potentially crystalline $\rho$ is *potentially diagonalizable* if there are a finite extension $K'/K$ and crystalline characters $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_n$ of $G_{K'}$ with $\rho|_{G_{K'}} \sim \chi_1 \oplus \dots \oplus \chi_n$.

See Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor, *Potential automorphy and change of weight*, Ann. of Math. 179 (2014), section 1. Ordinary crystalline representations are potentially diagonalizable, and so are crystalline representations with Hodge-Tate weights in a range $[a, a + \ell - 2]$ when $K/\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is unramified (Fontaine-Laffaille), by the same reference. The notion answers the question of the remark after Kisin's theorem: if $\rho$ and the residually automorphic $r_{\ell,\iota}(\pi)$ are both potentially diagonalizable, then after a base change they lie on components that patching can link, since the components of the diagonal sums are known.

The proof of that theorem has three steps:

1. A solvable base change $F'/F$, linearly disjoint from the field cut out by $\bar\rho$, simplifies the ramification of $\bar\rho$ and keeps its image. Automorphy descends along such extensions (Arthur and Clozel; Labesse).
2. Ihara avoidance and potential diagonalizability put $\rho|_{G_{F'}}$ and a residually equal automorphic representation on components of $R^{\mathrm{loc}}$ that the support of $M_\infty$ contains.
3. The patching theorem of the previous section gives $R^{\mathrm{red}} = \mathbb{T}$ on those components, and the corollary on automorphy from $R = \mathbb{T}$ ends the proof.

**Theorem (ordinary automorphy lifting; Geraghty).** Let $F$ be an imaginary CM field and $\ell > 2$. Let $\rho : G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ be continuous, unramified at almost all places, with $\rho^c \cong \rho^\vee \otimes \varepsilon_\ell^{1-n}$, ordinary at each place above $\ell$, with $\bar\rho(G_{F(\zeta_\ell)})$ adequate and $\zeta_\ell$ not in the field cut out by $\mathrm{ad}\,\bar\rho$. If $\bar\rho \cong \bar r_{\ell,\iota}(\Pi)$ for a regular algebraic polarized cuspidal $\Pi$ that is ordinary at the places above $\ell$, then $\rho$ is automorphic.

See Geraghty (2019), with the adequacy condition of Thorne (2012), for the exact hypotheses. The proof patches Hida's ordinary Hecke algebras over the weight space $\Lambda = \hat\bigotimes_{v \mid \ell} \Lambda_{F_v}$ and proves an $R^{\mathrm{ord}} = \mathbb{T}^{\mathrm{ord}}$ theorem over $\Lambda$. The components of the ordinary lifting rings are known, which is why Hida families are easier to patch. This is the setting of the universal ordinary determinant rings of [the window bound](/proof/vwin/), for $2 \le n \le 4$.

**Remark (residually reducible representations).** If $\bar\rho$ is reducible, the patching argument fails, since $R_{\bar\rho}$ does not exist and the Hecke modules are not controlled. Skinner and Wiles (*Residually reducible representations and modular forms*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 89, 1999) prove automorphy lifting for ordinary two-dimensional $\rho$ with reducible $\bar\rho$, using the reducibility ideal and Ribet-type extensions. Thorne (*Automorphy lifting for residually reducible l-adic Galois representations*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 28, 2015) proves a version for unitary groups, with $\bar\rho = \bar\rho_1 \oplus \bar\rho_2$ and the universal determinant ring of the third section. The case V count of [the window bound](/proof/vwin/) works near a reducible point of this kind, with three-dimensional representations.

**Remark.** Khare and Wintenberger (*Serre's modularity conjecture I*, Invent. Math. 178, 2009) combine lifting and potential automorphy theorems with an induction on $\ell$ and the conductor to prove Serre's modularity conjecture.

## Potential automorphy

When $\bar\rho$ is not known to be automorphic, the method still proves automorphy over a finite extension of $F$. Taylor found the argument for $\mathrm{GL}_2$ (*Remarks on a conjecture of Fontaine and Mazur*, J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 1, 2002).

**Theorem (Moret-Bailly).** Let $F$ be a number field, $S$ a finite set of places of $F$, and $F^{\mathrm{avoid}}/F$ a finite extension. Let $X$ be a smooth geometrically irreducible variety over $F$, and for each $v \in S$ let $\Omega_v \subset X(F_v)$ be a nonempty open subset for the $v$-adic topology. Then there is a finite Galois extension $F'/F$, linearly disjoint from $F^{\mathrm{avoid}}$ over $F$, in which each $v \in S$ splits completely, and a point $P \in X(F')$ that lies in $\Omega_v$ for each embedding $F' \to F_v$ over a place $v \in S$.

See Moret-Bailly, *Groupes de Picard et problèmes de Skolem II*, Ann. Sci. ENS 22 (1989), in the form used by Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor.

**Example (the Dwork family).** For $N \ge 2$ and $t \in F$, let $Y_t \subset \mathbb{P}^{N-1}$ be the hypersurface
$$X_1^N + X_2^N + \dots + X_N^N = N t\, X_1 X_2 \cdots X_N.$$
Let $H$ be the group of $(\zeta_i) \in \mu_N^N$ with $\prod_i \zeta_i = 1$, modulo the diagonal $\mu_N$, acting by scaling the coordinates. Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor (*A family of Calabi-Yau varieties and potential automorphy*, Ann. of Math. 171, 2010) use the $H$-invariant part of $H^{N-2}(Y_t)$, of rank $N - 1$, as a family of Galois representations over the $t$-line with large monodromy. For given $\bar\rho$ and an induced residual representation $\bar\rho'$ at a second prime $\ell'$, the moduli space of $t$ with the piece of $H^{N-2}(Y_t)$ congruent to $\bar\rho$ modulo $\ell$ and to $\bar\rho'$ modulo $\ell'$ is a smooth geometrically irreducible variety, and Moret-Bailly gives a point $t$ over some $F'$.

Then the argument has two lifting steps. The $\ell'$-adic representation of $Y_t$ is residually $\bar\rho'$, which is automorphic since it is induced from a character, so it is automorphic by a lifting theorem at $\ell'$. Its compatible partner at $\ell$ is then automorphic, so $\bar\rho|_{G_{F'}}$ is automorphic, and a lifting theorem at $\ell$ shows that $\rho|_{G_{F'}}$ is automorphic.

**Theorem (potential automorphy).** Let $F$ be a CM field, $F^{\mathrm{avoid}}/F$ finite, $\ell \ge 2(n+1)$, and $r : G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ continuous. Suppose that $r$ is unramified at almost all places, that $r^c \cong r^\vee \otimes \varepsilon_\ell^{1-n} \mu|_{G_F}$ for a character $\mu$ of $G_{F^+}$ with $\mu(c_v)$ independent of the real place $v$, that $r$ is potentially diagonalizable with regular Hodge-Tate weights at each place above $\ell$, and that $\bar r|_{G_{F(\zeta_\ell)}}$ is irreducible. Then there is a finite CM extension $F'/F$, Galois over $F$ and linearly disjoint from $F^{\mathrm{avoid}}$ over $F$, such that $r|_{G_{F'}}$ is automorphic.

See Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor (2014), section 4, where there is also a version for totally real $F$ and one for compatible systems.

**Corollary (meromorphic continuation).** Let $(r_\lambda)$ be a weakly compatible system of representations of $G_F$ such that, for a set of $\lambda$ of Dirichlet density one, $r_\lambda$ satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem. Then $L(s, r)$ has meromorphic continuation to $\mathbb{C}$ and satisfies the expected functional equation.

*Sketch.* Take $F'/F$ from the theorem, with group $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(F'/F)$. By Solomon's induction theorem, $1_\Gamma = \sum_j a_j \mathrm{Ind}_{H_j}^\Gamma 1$ with $a_j \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_j$ solvable. Put $F_j = F'^{H_j}$. Then $r = \sum_j a_j \mathrm{Ind}_{F_j}^F (r|_{G_{F_j}})$ in the Grothendieck group, so $L(s, r) = \prod_j L(s, r|_{G_{F_j}})^{a_j}$. Since $F'/F_j$ is solvable, $r|_{G_{F_j}}$ is automorphic by solvable descent, and each factor is the $L$-function of an automorphic representation, which has meromorphic continuation and a functional equation ([L-functions](/learn/l-functions/)). $\square$

The argument does not give holomorphy, since the $a_j$ can be negative. It gives the Sato-Tate conjecture for non-CM elliptic curves over totally real fields and for newforms of weight $k \ge 2$ without CM: the symmetric power $L$-functions have no zeros or poles on $\mathrm{Re}(s) = 1$, and this gives the equidistribution of the Frobenius angles. See Clozel, Harris and Taylor (2008), Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor (2010), Taylor (2008), and Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor, *A family of Calabi-Yau varieties and potential automorphy II*, Publ. RIMS 47 (2011).

**Remark (CM fields without polarization).** Allen, Calegari, Caraiani, Gee, Helm, Le Hung, Newton, Scholze, Taylor and Thorne (*Potential automorphy over CM fields*, Ann. of Math. 197, 2023) prove potential automorphy for representations of $G_F$, $F$ CM, that need not be polarized. They patch the complexes of Calegari and Geraghty, using the torsion Galois representations of Scholze (*On torsion in the cohomology of locally symmetric varieties*, Ann. of Math. 182, 2015).

In the proof, the potential modularity towers of the development volumes play this role for the automorphy in dimensions $3$ and $4$ that the window bound uses. See [the window bound](/proof/vwin/) and the hypotheses of [HypsAlt](/step/hyps-alt-def/).
