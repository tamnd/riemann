This chapter gives the analytic theory of automorphic forms that the development volumes use beyond [the chapter on automorphic forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/): reduction theory for arithmetic groups, the finiteness theorem of Harish-Chandra, the constant terms of Eisenstein series, the trace formula with its simple form and pseudo-coefficients, the twisted trace formula of base change, the relative trace formula with the Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma, and Rankin-Selberg integrals. ## Arithmetic subgroups and Siegel sets

Let $G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_N$ be a linear algebraic group over $\mathbb{Q}$, and put $G(\mathbb{Z}) = G(\mathbb{Q}) \cap \mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb{Z})$.

**Definition.** Two subgroups $\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2$ of a group are *commensurable* if $\Gamma_1 \cap \Gamma_2$ has finite index in $\Gamma_1$ and in $\Gamma_2$. An *arithmetic subgroup* of $G(\mathbb{Q})$ is a subgroup commensurable with $G(\mathbb{Z})$. For $G$ over a number field $F$, an arithmetic subgroup of $G(F)$ is an arithmetic subgroup of $(\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}} G)(\mathbb{Q}) = G(F)$.

The notion does not depend on the embedding $G \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_N$ (Borel, *Introduction aux groupes arithmétiques*). An arithmetic subgroup is discrete in $G(\mathbb{R})$, since $\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb{Z})$ is discrete in $\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb{R})$.

**Definition.** Let $K_f \subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})$ be a compact open subgroup. Then $\Gamma = G(\mathbb{Q}) \cap K_f$ is an arithmetic subgroup, a *congruence subgroup*. For $N \ge 1$, the *principal congruence subgroup* $\Gamma(N)$ is the kernel of $G(\mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$.

**Example.** In $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, the groups $\Gamma(N)$ and $\Gamma_0(N)$ are congruence subgroups. $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ also has arithmetic subgroups that are not congruence subgroups. For $n \ge 3$ each arithmetic subgroup of $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$ is a congruence subgroup (Bass, Milnor and Serre, *Solution of the congruence subgroup problem for $\mathrm{SL}_n$ ($n \ge 3$) and $\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}$ ($n \ge 2$)*, Publ. Math. IHES 33, 1967).

**Definition.** An arithmetic group $\Gamma$ is *neat* if, for each $\gamma \in \Gamma$, the subgroup of $\mathbb{C}^\times$ generated by the eigenvalues of $\gamma$ has no torsion. A neat group has no torsion.

**Theorem (Borel).** Each arithmetic group has a neat subgroup of finite index. For $N \ge 3$, $\Gamma(N)$ is neat.

A reference is Borel, *Introduction aux groupes arithmétiques*. The open compact subgroups with no torsion of [GeoOne](/step/geo-one-def/) are of this kind.

Now let $G = \mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{R})$, $K = \mathrm{SO}(n)$, $A$ the diagonal matrices in $G$ with positive entries, and $N$ the upper triangular unipotent matrices. By the [[Iwasawa decomposition|ZYVEVYBA41]], $(n, a, k) \mapsto nak$ is a diffeomorphism $N \times A \times K \to G$. The *simple roots* are $\alpha_i(a) = a_i / a_{i+1}$ for $1 \le i \le n - 1$.

**Definition.** For $t > 0$ and a compact $\omega \subseteq N$, the *Siegel set* is $\mathfrak{S}_{t, \omega} = \omega A_t K$, where $A_t = \{a \in A : \alpha_i(a) \ge t \text{ for each } i\}$.

The adelic Siegel sets of [the chapter on classical groups](/learn/groups/#quotients-of-finite-volume) have the same form, with $\omega$ in $N_0(\mathbb{A}_F) M_0(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$.

**Theorem (reduction theory for $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$).** Let $\mathcal{F} = \{z \in \mathbb{H} : |\operatorname{Re} z| \le \frac12, |z| \ge 1\}$. Then $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \mathcal{F} = \mathbb{H}$. In particular each $z \in \mathbb{H}$ has a translate in the Siegel domain $\{|\operatorname{Re} z| \le \frac12, \operatorname{Im} z \ge \frac{\sqrt 3}{2}\}$, which is the image in $\mathbb{H} = G/K$ of a Siegel set with $t = \frac{\sqrt 3}{2}$.

*Proof.* For $\gamma = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$, $\operatorname{Im}(\gamma z) = \operatorname{Im}(z) / |cz + d|^2$. The lattice $\mathbb{Z} z + \mathbb{Z}$ has only finitely many points $cz + d$ with $|cz + d| \le 1$, so $\operatorname{Im}(\gamma z)$ takes a largest value at some $\gamma$. Replace $z$ by $\gamma z$, and then by a translate $z + m$ with $|\operatorname{Re} z| \le \frac12$. If $|z| < 1$, then $\operatorname{Im}(-1/z) = \operatorname{Im}(z)/|z|^2 > \operatorname{Im}(z)$, against the choice. So $|z| \ge 1$, and $\operatorname{Im}(z)^2 \ge 1 - \frac14$. $\square$

**Example.** For the measure $dx\, dy / y^2$,
$$\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}) = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \int_{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}^\infty \frac{dy}{y^2}\, dx = \int_{-1/2}^{1/2} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} = \frac{\pi}{3}.$$
This number returns below as the inverse of the residue of the Eisenstein series at $s = 1$.

**Theorem (reduction theory; Siegel; Borel and Harish-Chandra).** Let $G$ be reductive over $\mathbb{Q}$, $\Gamma$ an arithmetic subgroup, $P_0$ a minimal parabolic subgroup over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $\mathfrak{S}$ a Siegel set of $G(\mathbb{R})$ for $P_0$.

1. The set $\Gamma \backslash G(\mathbb{Q}) / P_0(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite.
2. For $t$ small and $\omega$ large there is a finite set $C \subseteq G(\mathbb{Q})$ with $G(\mathbb{R}) = \Gamma C \mathfrak{S}$.
3. (*Siegel property*) For each $g \in G(\mathbb{Q})$, the set of $\gamma \in \Gamma$ with $\gamma g \mathfrak{S} \cap \mathfrak{S} \ne \varnothing$ is finite.

References are Borel and Harish-Chandra, *Arithmetic subgroups of algebraic groups*, Ann. of Math. 75 (1962), and Borel, *Introduction aux groupes arithmétiques*. The elements of $C$ are the *cusps*; for $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ there is one cusp, $\infty$. The set $C \mathfrak{S}$ is a *fundamental set*: it is not a [[fundamental domain|KNJA2NEZ9F]], but its translates cover and each meets only finitely many of the others. The theorem of Borel and Harish-Chandra on finite volume ([classical groups](/learn/groups/#quotients-of-finite-volume)) is a corollary.

**Theorem (Mahler's compactness criterion).** A subset $Y$ of $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{R}) / \mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$, the space of lattices of covolume $1$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, is relatively compact if and only if there is $\varepsilon > 0$ such that no lattice in $Y$ has a nonzero vector of length less than $\varepsilon$.

**Theorem (Godement's criterion; Borel and Harish-Chandra, 1962; Mostow and Tamagawa, 1962).** Let $G$ be semisimple over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\Gamma$ arithmetic. Then $\Gamma \backslash G(\mathbb{R})$ is compact if and only if $G(\mathbb{Q})$ has no unipotent element other than $1$, that is, if and only if $G$ has no proper parabolic subgroup over $\mathbb{Q}$.

The adelic and the classical points of view are related by the finiteness of class numbers.

**Theorem (Borel, 1963).** Let $G$ be reductive over a number field $F$ and $K_f \subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$ compact open. Then $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f}) / K_f$ is finite. If $x_1, \dots, x_h$ are representatives and $\Gamma_i = G(F) \cap x_i K_f x_i^{-1}$, then
$$G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F) / K_f \cong \bigsqcup_{i=1}^h \Gamma_i \backslash G(F \otimes \mathbb{R}),$$
by $\Gamma_i g_\infty \mapsto G(F) g_\infty x_i K_f$.

The reference is Borel, *Some finiteness properties of adele groups over number fields*, Publ. Math. IHES 16 (1963). Each $\Gamma_i$ is a congruence subgroup. So an automorphic form of level $K_f$ is an $h$-tuple of functions on the $\Gamma_i \backslash G(F \otimes \mathbb{R})$.

**Example (Siegel sets for unitary groups).** Let $E/F$ be a CM extension, $J$ a nondegenerate Hermitian matrix of size $n$, and $G = \mathrm{U}_J$. Let $X, Y$ be a *Witt frame* of rank $r$: $n \times r$ matrices with $X^* J X = Y^* J Y = 0$, $X^* J Y = 1$, such that $J$ is anisotropic on the orthogonal complement of the columns of $X$ and $Y$. Let $P_0$ be the stabilizer of the flag of the column spans of $X$, a minimal parabolic, and $a(t)$, for $t \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^r$, the archimedean element that acts by $t$ on $X$, by $t^{-1}$ on $Y$ and trivially on the complement. For $c > 0$ let the cone be the $t$ with $c\, t_{i+1} \le t_i$ and $c \le t_r$. Reduction theory says that each $g \in G(\mathbb{A}_F)$ is $\gamma\, p\, a(t)\, k$ with $\gamma \in G(F)$, $p$ in a compact subset of $P_0(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$, $t$ in the cone and $k$ in a maximal compact subgroup. The development volumes prove this form, and [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/) reaches it through `siegelSetUnitary`.

## Finiteness of automorphic forms

Let $G$ be reductive over a number field $F$, with $K = K_\infty K_f$ and $\mathcal{Z}$ as in [automorphic forms on adelic groups](/learn/automorphic-forms/#automorphic-forms-on-adelic-groups).

**Definition.** Fix a compact open $K_f \subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$, a finite set $\Theta$ of classes of irreducible representations of $K_\infty$, and an ideal $\mathcal{J} \subseteq \mathcal{Z}$ of finite codimension. Let $\mathcal{A}(G; K_f, \Theta, \mathcal{J})$ be the space of automorphic forms $\varphi$ that are right $K_f$-invariant, whose $K_\infty$-translates span a sum of representations in $\Theta$, and with $\mathcal{J}\varphi = 0$.

**Theorem (Harish-Chandra finiteness).** The space $\mathcal{A}(G; K_f, \Theta, \mathcal{J})$ is finite dimensional.

This is Theorem 1 of Harish-Chandra, *Automorphic Forms on Semisimple Lie Groups*, Lecture Notes in Math. 62 (1968); for adelic groups, see Moeglin and Waldspurger, *Spectral Decomposition and Eisenstein Series*, Chapter I. We sketch the proof, because the development volumes follow it.

**Lemma (Harish-Chandra).** Let $U$ be a neighbourhood of $1$ in $G_\infty$. There is a smooth function $\alpha$ with compact support in $U$, invariant under conjugation by $K_\infty$, such that $\varphi * \alpha = \varphi$ for each $\varphi \in \mathcal{A}(G; K_f, \Theta, \mathcal{J})$, where $\varphi * \alpha(g) = \int_{G_\infty} \varphi(g h^{-1}) \alpha(h)\, dh$.

The proof is in Harish-Chandra's notes cited above. It uses that a $\mathcal{Z}$-finite and $K_\infty$-finite function satisfies an elliptic differential equation, hence is real analytic.

**Theorem (Gelfand and Piatetski-Shapiro).** Let $f$ be smooth with compact support on $G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$. Then the operator $R(f)$ on the closure $L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ of the cusp forms in $L^2(G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1)$ is compact. It is even of trace class.

The reference is Gelfand, Graev and Piatetski-Shapiro, *Representation Theory and Automorphic Functions*, Chapter 1, for $\mathrm{SL}_2$; the general case is in Moeglin and Waldspurger, Chapter I. The proof bounds the kernel of $R(f)$ on cusp forms on a Siegel set, by the [rapid decay of cusp forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#constant-terms-and-cusp-forms). The statement `cuspTraceClass` of [ThetaLocalInputs](/step/theta-local-inputs-def/) and of [UnitaryGroupThirdPlaceHypotheses](/step/unitary-group-third-place-hypotheses-def/) is this trace class property.

*Proof of finiteness for cusp forms.* The cusp forms in $\mathcal{A}(G; K_f, \Theta, \mathcal{J})$ lie in $L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ by rapid decay. With $\alpha$ from the lemma and $f = \alpha^\vee \otimes \mathbf{1}_{K_f}$, each of them is an eigenvector of $R(f)$ with eigenvalue $\operatorname{vol}(K_f)$. A compact operator has finite dimensional eigenspaces for nonzero eigenvalues. $\square$

For the other forms we use constant terms. For a parabolic $P = M N$ over $F$, recall $\varphi_P(g) = \int_{N(F) \backslash N(\mathbb{A}_F)} \varphi(ng)\, dn$.

**Definition.** A *form on $P$* is a smooth function of moderate growth on $N(\mathbb{A}_F) M(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)$ that is right $K_f$-invariant, of $K_\infty$-types in $\Theta$, and killed by $\mathcal{J}$. It is *cuspidal relative to $P$* if $\varphi_Q = 0$ for each parabolic $Q \subsetneq P$.

**Lemma.** Let $\varphi$ be an automorphic form on $G$ with $\varphi_P = 0$ for each maximal standard parabolic $P$. Then $\varphi$ is a cusp form.

*Proof.* Let $Q$ be a proper parabolic over $F$. It is $G(F)$-conjugate to a standard parabolic, $Q = \gamma Q_1 \gamma^{-1}$, and $\varphi_Q(g) = \varphi_{Q_1}(\gamma^{-1} g)$ by the change of variables $n \mapsto \gamma n \gamma^{-1}$ and the left invariance of $\varphi$. Let $P \supseteq Q_1$ be maximal standard. Then $N_P \subseteq N_{Q_1}$ is normal, and integrating first over $N_P(F) \backslash N_P(\mathbb{A}_F)$ gives $\varphi_{Q_1} = (\varphi_P)_{Q_1} = 0$. $\square$

*Sketch of the general case.* Harish-Chandra proves that the forms on $P$ that are cuspidal relative to $P$ span a finite dimensional space; for $P = G$ this is the case of cusp forms. Now argue by induction over the standard parabolics, from the minimal one upwards. For a form $\varphi$ on $P$, the constant terms $\varphi_Q$ along the maximal $Q \subsetneq P$ are forms on $Q$; they keep smoothness, moderate growth, the $K_\infty$-types and the ideal. By induction they lie in a finite dimensional space. The kernel of $\varphi \mapsto (\varphi_Q)_Q$ consists of forms cuspidal relative to $P$, by the argument of the lemma. So the forms on $P$ span a finite dimensional space. The case $P = G$ is the theorem. $\square$

**Corollary.** Fix $K_f$, $\Theta$ and $\mathcal{J}$. Only finitely many cuspidal automorphic representations $\pi$ have $\pi^{K_f} \ne 0$, a $K_\infty$-type in $\Theta$ and an infinitesimal character killed by $\mathcal{J}$, and each occurs in the cusp forms with finite multiplicity.

The comparison of trace formulas below needs this finite list of representations. The development volumes prove the theorem for unitary groups over CM fields, with the eigenvalues of the Gelfand operators $\operatorname{tr}(E^{k+1})$ fixed in place of $\mathcal{J}$, and with maximal parabolics given by isotropic frames.

## Eisenstein series and the residual spectrum

All Eisenstein series, Poincaré series and Rankin-Selberg integrals rest on one computation.

**Lemma (unfolding).** Let $G$ be a unimodular locally compact group, $\Gamma \subseteq G$ a discrete subgroup and $\Lambda \subseteq \Gamma$ a subgroup. Give $\Gamma \backslash G$ and $\Lambda \backslash G$ the quotient measures of one Haar measure on $G$. Let $f \ge 0$ be measurable on $\Lambda \backslash G$, or let $\sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda \backslash \Gamma} |f(\gamma g)|$ be integrable on $\Gamma \backslash G$. Then
$$\int_{\Gamma \backslash G} \sum_{\gamma \in \Lambda \backslash \Gamma} f(\gamma g)\, dg = \int_{\Lambda \backslash G} f(g)\, dg.$$

*Proof.* Let $D$ be a measurable fundamental domain for $\Gamma$ and $R$ a set of representatives of $\Lambda \backslash \Gamma$. Each $g \in G$ is $\gamma d$ with $\gamma \in \Gamma$, $d \in D$, unique outside a null set, and $\gamma = \lambda r$ with $\lambda \in \Lambda$, $r \in R$ unique. So $\bigsqcup_{r \in R} r D$ is a fundamental domain for $\Lambda$, and $\int_{\Lambda \backslash G} f = \sum_{r} \int_{rD} f = \int_D \sum_r f(rg)\, dg$, by Tonelli or Fubini. $\square$

In the adelic setting $G = G(\mathbb{A}_F)$, $\Gamma = G(F)$ and $\Lambda = P(F)$ or $H(F)$ for a subgroup $H$. Unfolding is the first step of the Siegel-Weil formula in [the Siegel-Weil chapter](/proof/siegel-weil/) and of the period computation of the row test.

**Definition.** For $z = x + iy \in \mathbb{H}$ and $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$, the *non-holomorphic [[Eisenstein series|3NZBZXYWAJ]]* is
$$E(z, s) = \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_\infty \backslash \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})} \operatorname{Im}(\gamma z)^s = \frac12 \sum_{\gcd(c, d) = 1} \frac{y^s}{|cz + d|^{2s}},$$
with $\Gamma_\infty = \{\pm \begin{pmatrix} 1 & n \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\}$. It is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$-invariant, and $\Delta E = s(1-s) E$ for $\Delta = -y^2(\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2)$.

Let $\xi(s) = \pi^{-s/2} \Gamma(s/2) \zeta(s)$ be the completed [[Riemann zeta function|TXB2F07CC6]], so $\xi(s) = \xi(1-s)$ ([L-functions](/learn/l-functions/#the-functional-equation-and-the-trivial-zeros)), and put $E^*(z, s) = \xi(2s) E(z, s)$.

**Theorem (Fourier expansion).** For $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$,
$$E^*(z, s) = \xi(2s) y^s + \xi(2s - 1) y^{1-s} + 4 \sqrt{y} \sum_{n \ge 1} n^{s - 1/2} \sigma_{1 - 2s}(n) K_{s - 1/2}(2\pi n y) \cos(2\pi n x),$$
where $\sigma_w(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} d^w$ and $K_\nu$ is the $K$-Bessel function.

**Corollary.** $E^*(z, s)$ continues to a meromorphic function of $s \in \mathbb{C}$, with simple poles at $s = 0$ and $s = 1$ only, and $E^*(z, s) = E^*(z, 1 - s)$. The *constant term* of $E$ is $y^s + \varphi(s) y^{1-s}$ with $\varphi(s) = \xi(2s-1)/\xi(2s)$. The residue of $E(z, s)$ at $s = 1$ is the constant $3/\pi = 1 / \operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H})$.

*Proof.* The sum over $n$ converges for all $s$, locally uniformly, because $K_\nu(u)$ decays like $e^{-u}$; it is invariant under $s \mapsto 1 - s$, because $K_\nu = K_{-\nu}$ and $n^{s-1/2}\sigma_{1-2s}(n) = n^{1/2 - s}\sigma_{2s-1}(n)$. The two first terms are exchanged by $s \mapsto 1 - s$, by $\xi(w) = \xi(1-w)$. The poles of $\xi$ are at $0$ and $1$, with residues $-1$ and $1$. At $s = 1$ only $\xi(2s - 1)$ has a pole, with residue $\frac12$ in $s$, and $\xi(2) = \pi/6$. So the residue of $E = E^*/\xi(2s)$ is $\frac12 \cdot \frac{6}{\pi} = \frac{3}{\pi}$. $\square$

The proof of the expansion applies Poisson summation in $d$ for each $c$; see Iwaniec, *Spectral Methods of Automorphic Forms*, the chapter on Eisenstein series, or Bump, *Automorphic Forms and Representations*, Chapter 1.

**Remark.** The residue $3/\pi$ is square integrable, while $E(z, s)$ is not: near the cusp, $y^a$ is square integrable for $dx\, dy/y^2$ if and only if $\operatorname{Re}(a) < \frac12$. At $s = 1$ only the exponent $1 - s = 0$ survives in the residue. This is the simplest case of the *Langlands square integrability criterion*: an automorphic form is square integrable if and only if, along each parabolic, the exponents of its constant term lie in a certain negative cone (Moeglin and Waldspurger, Chapter I). For $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ the residual spectrum is the constants.

Now the adelic form. Let $G = \mathrm{GL}_n$ or $\mathrm{SL}_n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, $B = TN$ the upper triangular Borel subgroup, $K = \mathrm{SO}(n) \prod_p G(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ (with $\mathrm{O}(n)$ for $\mathrm{GL}_n$), and $H_B : G(\mathbb{A}) \to \mathfrak{a} = \operatorname{Hom}(X^*(T), \mathbb{R})$ the map with $e^{\langle \chi, H_B(tnk) \rangle} = |\chi(t)|$. For $\lambda \in \mathfrak{a}^*_{\mathbb{C}}$ let $\phi_\lambda(nak) = e^{\langle \lambda + \rho, H_B(a) \rangle}$, with $\rho$ the half sum of the positive roots.

**Definition.** The *minimal parabolic Eisenstein series* is $E(g, \lambda) = \sum_{\gamma \in B(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash G(\mathbb{Q})} \phi_\lambda(\gamma g)$. For $w$ in the [[Weyl group|YT1ZJ2S4EE]] $W$, the *intertwining operator* is
$$M(w, \lambda)\phi(g) = \int_{(N \cap w N w^{-1})(\mathbb{A}) \backslash N(\mathbb{A})} \phi(w^{-1} n g)\, dn.$$

**Theorem (Langlands; Gindikin and Karpelevich).** The series $E(g, \lambda)$ converges absolutely for $\operatorname{Re} \langle \lambda - \rho, \alpha^\vee \rangle > 0$ for each simple root $\alpha$, and continues meromorphically to all $\lambda$. Its constant term along $B$ is
$$E_B(g, \lambda) = \sum_{w \in W} M(w, \lambda) \phi_\lambda(g), \qquad M(w, \lambda) \phi_\lambda = \prod_{\alpha > 0,\ w\alpha < 0} \frac{\xi(\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle)}{\xi(\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle + 1)}\, \phi_{w\lambda},$$
for the Haar measures that give $N(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash N(\mathbb{A})$ and each $G(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ volume $1$.

The references are Langlands, *Euler Products* (Yale, 1971), for the product formula, and Langlands, *On the Functional Equations Satisfied by Eisenstein Series*, Lecture Notes in Math. 544 (1976), for the continuation. For $\mathrm{SL}_2$, $\lambda + \rho = s\alpha$ gives $\langle \lambda, \alpha^\vee \rangle = 2s - 1$, and the formula is the constant term $y^s + \varphi(s) y^{1-s}$ above.

**Theorem (constant term of cuspidal Eisenstein series).** Let $P = MN$ be a standard parabolic, $\sigma$ a cuspidal automorphic representation of $M$, and $E(g, \phi, \lambda)$ the Eisenstein series of a section $\phi$ as in [Eisenstein series](/learn/automorphic-forms/#eisenstein-series). Let $Q$ be a standard parabolic. Then $E_Q = 0$ unless $Q$ contains a conjugate of $P$; and if $Q = P$, then
$$E_P(g, \phi, \lambda) = \sum_{w \in W(M)} M(w, \lambda) \phi(g),$$
where $W(M)$ is the set of Weyl elements $w$ of minimal length in their coset with $w M w^{-1}$ a standard Levi subgroup, and $M(w, \lambda)$ maps sections for $(\sigma, \lambda)$ to sections for $(w\sigma, w\lambda)$.

The reference is Moeglin and Waldspurger, Chapter II. The poles of $E$ are the poles of the $M(w, \lambda)$, and the constant term of a residue keeps only the terms that have the pole. When these exponents are in the negative cone, the residue is square integrable. This is how [Langlands' description of the residual spectrum](/learn/automorphic-forms/#the-discrete-spectrum) arises.

**Example (residual spectrum of $\mathrm{GL}_n$).** Let $n = ab$ and $\sigma$ unitary cuspidal on $\mathrm{GL}_a$. The iterated residue of the Eisenstein series of $\sigma|\det|^{s_1} \otimes \dots \otimes \sigma|\det|^{s_b}$ at $s_i = \frac{b + 1}{2} - i$ has constant term along the parabolic of type $(a, \dots, a)$ equal to the single term with exponents $(\frac{1-b}{2}, \frac{3-b}{2}, \dots, \frac{b-1}{2})$, in increasing order. So the residual representation $\mathrm{Speh}(\sigma, b)$ embeds into the representation induced with ascending exponents. This is the embedding `AscEmb` that the development volumes assemble from local Speh representations, after Moeglin and Waldspurger, *Le spectre résiduel de GL(n)*, Ann. Sci. ENS 22 (1989).

## The trace formula for a compact quotient

**Definition.** Let $H$ be a separable Hilbert space and $T$ a bounded operator. Then $T$ is *Hilbert-Schmidt* if $\sum_i \|T e_i\|^2 < \infty$ for an orthonormal basis $(e_i)$, and of *trace class* if $T = T_1 T_2$ with $T_1, T_2$ Hilbert-Schmidt. Then $\operatorname{tr} T = \sum_i \langle T e_i, e_i \rangle$ converges absolutely and does not depend on the basis. On $L^2(X)$ for a $\sigma$-finite measure space $X$, the Hilbert-Schmidt operators are the integral operators with kernel in $L^2(X \times X)$.

Let $G$ be a unimodular locally compact group, $\Gamma$ a discrete subgroup with $\Gamma \backslash G$ compact, and $R$ the right regular representation on $L^2(\Gamma \backslash G)$. For $f$ continuous with compact support, $R(f)\phi(x) = \int_G f(y) \phi(xy)\, dy$.

**Lemma.** $R(f)$ is the integral operator with kernel $K_f(x, y) = \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma} f(x^{-1} \gamma y)$.

*Proof.* Substitute $y \mapsto x^{-1} y$: $R(f)\phi(x) = \int_G f(x^{-1} y) \phi(y)\, dy$. Unfold over $\Gamma$: this is $\int_{\Gamma \backslash G} \sum_{\gamma} f(x^{-1} \gamma y) \phi(y)\, dy$. The sum is finite for $x, y$ in a compact set, since $\Gamma$ is discrete. $\square$

**Theorem (Gelfand, Graev and Piatetski-Shapiro).** Let $G$ be a Lie group, or $G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$ for $G$ reductive over $F$, with $\Gamma \backslash G$ compact. Then $L^2(\Gamma \backslash G) = \widehat{\bigoplus}_\pi m(\pi) \pi$, a Hilbert direct sum of irreducible unitary representations with finite multiplicities $m(\pi)$. For $f$ smooth with compact support, $R(f)$ is of trace class, and $\operatorname{tr} R(f) = \int_{\Gamma \backslash G} K_f(x, x)\, dx$.

The reference is Gelfand, Graev and Piatetski-Shapiro, Chapter 1, or Arthur, *An introduction to the trace formula*, Section 1. The trace class property comes from the factorization of Dixmier and Malliavin: each smooth $f$ with compact support is a finite sum of convolutions $f_1 * f_2$ (*Factorisations de fonctions et de vecteurs indéfiniment différentiables*, Bull. Sci. Math. 102, 1978), and each $R(f_i)$ has a continuous kernel on a compact space, so it is Hilbert-Schmidt. The development volumes use this factorization as the input "Dixmier-Malliavin".

**Definition.** For $\gamma \in G$, let $G_\gamma$ be its centralizer. If $G_\gamma$ is unimodular, the *orbital integral* of $f$ at $\gamma$ is
$$O_\gamma(f) = \int_{G_\gamma \backslash G} f(x^{-1} \gamma x)\, dx.$$
For $G$ reductive over a local field, $\gamma$ is *regular semisimple* if $G_\gamma^0$ is a maximal torus, and *elliptic* if also $G_\gamma^0 / Z_G^0$ is compact.

**Theorem (Selberg trace formula, compact quotient).** In the setting of the theorem above,
$$\sum_\pi m(\pi) \operatorname{tr} \pi(f) = \sum_{\{\gamma\}} \operatorname{vol}(\Gamma_\gamma \backslash G_\gamma)\, O_\gamma(f),$$
where $\{\gamma\}$ runs over the $\Gamma$-conjugacy classes in $\Gamma$, and $\Gamma_\gamma = \Gamma \cap G_\gamma$. Only finitely many terms on the right are nonzero.

*Proof.* The left side is $\operatorname{tr} R(f)$. For the right side, group the sum $K_f(x, x) = \sum_\gamma f(x^{-1}\gamma x)$ by conjugacy classes. The class of $\gamma$ is $\{\delta^{-1}\gamma\delta : \delta \in \Gamma_\gamma \backslash \Gamma\}$. Then
$$\begin{aligned} \int_{\Gamma \backslash G} K_f(x, x)\, dx &= \sum_{\{\gamma\}} \int_{\Gamma \backslash G} \sum_{\delta \in \Gamma_\gamma \backslash \Gamma} f(x^{-1}\delta^{-1}\gamma\delta x)\, dx = \sum_{\{\gamma\}} \int_{\Gamma_\gamma \backslash G} f(x^{-1}\gamma x)\, dx \\ &= \sum_{\{\gamma\}} \int_{G_\gamma \backslash G} \int_{\Gamma_\gamma \backslash G_\gamma} f(x^{-1}u^{-1}\gamma u x)\, du\, dx = \sum_{\{\gamma\}} \operatorname{vol}(\Gamma_\gamma \backslash G_\gamma)\, O_\gamma(f), \end{aligned}$$
by unfolding and then integration in stages, since $u$ commutes with $\gamma$. Since $\Gamma \backslash G$ is compact and $f$ has compact support, only finitely many classes meet the support of $x \mapsto f(x^{-1} \cdot x)$ for $x$ in a compact fundamental set, so all sums are finite and the exchanges are allowed. That $\Gamma_\gamma$ is cocompact in $G_\gamma$, so that $G_\gamma$ is unimodular, is in Arthur's Section 1. $\square$

**Example (Poisson summation).** Let $G = \mathbb{R}$ and $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}$. Then $L^2(\mathbb{Z} \backslash \mathbb{R}) = \widehat{\bigoplus}_n \mathbb{C} e^{2\pi i n x}$, $\operatorname{tr} \pi_n(f) = \int f(y) e^{2\pi i n y}\, dy = \hat f(-n)$, and the trace formula is the [[Poisson summation formula|GARGJYSGFG]] $\sum_n \hat f(n) = \sum_m f(m)$.

**Example (Selberg).** Let $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ be discrete, cocompact and without torsion, and let $\lambda_j = \frac14 + r_j^2$ be the eigenvalues of $\Delta$ on $\Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H}$ with multiplicity. Let $h$ be even and holomorphic on $|\operatorname{Im} r| \le \frac12 + \varepsilon$ with $h(r) = O((1 + |r|)^{-2-\varepsilon})$, and $g(u) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int h(r) e^{-iru}\, dr$. Then
$$\sum_j h(r_j) = \frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma \backslash \mathbb{H})}{4\pi} \int_{-\infty}^\infty r\, h(r) \tanh(\pi r)\, dr + \sum_{\{P\}} \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{\log N(P)}{N(P)^{k/2} - N(P)^{-k/2}}\, g(k \log N(P)),$$
where $\{P\}$ runs over the primitive hyperbolic classes and $N(P)$ is the norm. The first term is the identity class, the second the hyperbolic classes. (Selberg, *Harmonic analysis and discontinuous groups in weakly symmetric Riemannian spaces with applications to Dirichlet series*, J. Indian Math. Soc. 20, 1956.)

**Example (definite groups).** Let $F$ be totally real and $G$ reductive over $F$ with $G(F \otimes \mathbb{R})$ compact, for example $\mathrm{U}(V)$ for a definite Hermitian space $V$ over a CM field. Let $\xi$ be an irreducible representation of $G(F \otimes \mathbb{R})$ on $V_\xi$ and $K_f$ compact open. The *automorphic forms of weight $\xi$ and level $K_f$* are the functions $\phi : G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})/K_f \to V_\xi$ with $\phi(\gamma g) = \xi(\gamma)\phi(g)$ for $\gamma \in G(F)$. With $x_i$ and $\Gamma_i$ as in Borel's theorem, the groups $\Gamma_i$ are finite, and
$$\dim \mathcal{A}(\xi, K_f) = \sum_{i=1}^h \dim V_\xi^{\Gamma_i} = \sum_{i=1}^h \frac{1}{|\Gamma_i|} \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_i} \operatorname{tr} \xi(\gamma).$$

*Proof.* A form is fixed by its values $\phi(x_i)$. If $\gamma x_i k = x_i$, then $\gamma = x_i k^{-1} x_i^{-1} \in \Gamma_i$ and $\phi(x_i) = \xi(\gamma)\phi(x_i)$. So $\phi(x_i) \in V_\xi^{\Gamma_i}$, and each choice of these vectors gives a form. The dimension of the invariants of a finite group is the average of the trace. $\square$

The same computation with a Hecke operator in place of the identity is a trace formula with finitely many terms. This is the case of the definite unitary groups of [GeoOne](/step/geo-one-def/), and the development volumes write the trace of a Hecke operator on forms of a weight as the dimension times a sum over rational classes.

## Arthur's trace formula, the simple trace formula and pseudo-coefficients

Let $G$ be reductive over $F$, and $G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$ the common kernel of the $|\chi|$, $\chi$ a rational character. If $G$ has a proper parabolic over $F$, then $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$ is not compact, the continuous spectrum of [the spectral decomposition](/learn/automorphic-forms/#the-discrete-spectrum) is not zero, and $K_f(x, x)$ is not integrable. Arthur cuts the diagonal off near the cusps.

**Definition (Arthur's truncation).** Fix a minimal parabolic $P_0$ and a point $T$ of $\mathfrak{a}_0$ far inside the positive chamber. For a standard parabolic $P$, let $\hat\tau_P$ be the characteristic function of the $H \in \mathfrak{a}_0$ with $\varpi(H) > 0$ for the fundamental weights $\varpi$ of $P$, and $H_P$ the map of the Iwasawa decomposition. For a function $\phi$ on $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$,
$$\Lambda^T \phi(x) = \sum_{P \supseteq P_0} (-1)^{\dim(A_P/A_G)} \sum_{\delta \in P(F) \backslash G(F)} \phi_P(\delta x)\, \hat\tau_P(H_P(\delta x) - T).$$

**Example.** For $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}$ and $T > 1$, $\Lambda^T \phi(z) = \phi(z) - \phi_N(y)$ on the part $y > T$ of $\mathcal{F}$, and $\Lambda^T\phi = \phi$ on the rest. Here $\phi_N(y) = \int_0^1 \phi(x + iy)\, dx$. For $E(z, s)$, the truncated function $\Lambda^T E(z, s)$ is square integrable.

**Theorem (Arthur, 1978).** Let $f$ be smooth with compact support on $G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$ and $K_P(x, y) = \sum_{\gamma \in M_P(F)} \int_{N_P(\mathbb{A}_F)} f(x^{-1}\gamma n y)\, dn$. For $T$ regular enough, the truncated kernel
$$k^T(x, f) = \sum_{P \supseteq P_0} (-1)^{\dim(A_P/A_G)} \sum_{\delta \in P(F) \backslash G(F)} K_P(\delta x, \delta x)\, \hat\tau_P(H_P(\delta x) - T)$$
is integrable on $G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$. The integral $J^T(f)$ is a polynomial in $T$. It has a geometric expansion $J^T = \sum_{\mathfrak{o}} J^T_{\mathfrak{o}}$ over the classes $\mathfrak{o}$ of semisimple parts of elements of $G(F)$, and a spectral expansion $J^T = \sum_\chi J^T_\chi$ over cuspidal data $\chi = (M, \sigma)$ up to conjugacy.

The reference is Arthur, *A trace formula for reductive groups I: terms associated to classes in $G(\mathbb{Q})$*, Duke Math. J. 45 (1978), with the spectral side in part II, Compositio Math. 40 (1980). If $\mathfrak{o}$ is the class of a regular elliptic $\gamma \in G(F)$, then $J^T_{\mathfrak{o}}(f) = \operatorname{vol}(G_\gamma(F) \backslash G_\gamma(\mathbb{A}_F)^1)\, O_\gamma(f)$, as in the compact case. The other geometric terms are weighted orbital integrals, and the other spectral terms come from Eisenstein series. For the use in the proof, these terms only have to vanish.

**Definition.** Let $G$ be reductive over a local field $F_v$ with compact center, or fix a central character. A smooth function $f_v$ with compact support is *cuspidal* if $O_\gamma(f_v) = 0$ for each regular semisimple $\gamma$ that is not elliptic. It is *supported on the regular elliptic set* if $f_v(x) = 0$ unless $x$ is regular elliptic.

**Theorem (simple trace formula).** Let $f = \bigotimes_v f_v$ on $G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1$. Suppose that at one finite place $v_1$, $f_{v_1}$ is a matrix coefficient of a supercuspidal representation, and that at a second place $v_2$, $f_{v_2}$ is supported on the regular elliptic set. Then
$$\sum_{\pi} m_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\pi) \operatorname{tr} \pi(f) = \sum_{\{\gamma\}} \operatorname{vol}(G_\gamma(F) \backslash G_\gamma(\mathbb{A}_F)^1)\, O_\gamma(f),$$
where $\pi$ runs over the cuspidal automorphic representations and $\{\gamma\}$ over the regular elliptic classes of $G(F)$.

This form goes back to Deligne and Kazhdan; see Deligne, Kazhdan and Vignéras, *Représentations des algèbres centrales simples $p$-adiques*, in *Représentations des groupes réductifs sur un corps local* (Hermann, 1984), and, for functions cuspidal at two places, Arthur, *The invariant trace formula II. Global theory*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 1 (1988). The function at $v_1$ kills the continuous and residual spectrum, and the one at $v_2$ kills all geometric terms but the regular elliptic ones. The development volumes use the variant with archimedean pseudo-coefficients at the infinite places and an elliptic kernel.

**Definition.** Let $G$ be a real or $p$-adic reductive group with compact center and $\pi$ a [[discrete series|9AB7HYGPEK]] representation ([representations](/learn/representations/#discrete-series)). A *pseudo-coefficient* of $\pi$ is a smooth, compactly supported, bi-$K$-finite function $f_\pi$ with $\operatorname{tr} \pi(f_\pi) = 1$ and $\operatorname{tr} \pi'(f_\pi) = 0$ for each irreducible tempered $\pi' \not\cong \pi$.

**Example.** Let $\pi$ be supercuspidal, $v$ a unit vector and $d(\pi)$ the formal degree. Then $f(g) = d(\pi) \overline{\langle \pi(g) v, v \rangle}$ has compact support, and by Schur orthogonality $\pi(f)$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathbb{C}v$, so $\operatorname{tr} \pi(f) = 1$. It is a pseudo-coefficient.

**Theorem (existence).** Each discrete series of a real reductive group has a pseudo-coefficient (Clozel and Delorme, *Le théorème de Paley-Wiener invariant pour les groupes de Lie réductifs*, Invent. Math. 77, 1984). The same holds for $p$-adic groups (Kazhdan, *Cuspidal geometry of $p$-adic groups*, J. Analyse Math. 47, 1986).

**Theorem (orbital integrals of pseudo-coefficients).** A pseudo-coefficient $f_\pi$ is cuspidal. At a regular elliptic $\gamma$, $O_\gamma(f_\pi)$ is a constant, which depends only on $G$ and the measures, times $\overline{\Theta_\pi(\gamma)}$, where $\Theta_\pi$ is the Harish-Chandra character of $\pi$.

See Arthur, *The $L^2$-Lefschetz numbers of Hecke operators*, Invent. Math. 97 (1989), and Clozel and Delorme, cited above.

**Example (stable sums for $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$).** Let $\Pi_\lambda$ be the set of the $\binom{p+q}{p}$ discrete series of $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$ with regular integral infinitesimal character $\lambda$, its *L-packet*. On the compact torus $T$, Harish-Chandra's formula is $\Theta_{\pi}(t) = (-1)^{q(G)} \sum_{w \in W_K} \varepsilon(w) e^{w\lambda'}(t) / \Delta(t)$ for the parameter $\lambda'$ of $\pi$, with $q(G) = \frac12 \dim G/K = pq$ and $\Delta$ the Weyl denominator. The parameters in $\Pi_\lambda$ run over $W / W_K$, so $\sum_{\pi \in \Pi_\lambda} \Theta_\pi = (-1)^{q(G)} \Theta_F$ on regular elements of $T$, where $F$ is the finite dimensional representation with infinitesimal character $\lambda$. So the sum of the pseudo-coefficients over $\Pi_\lambda$ has orbital integrals that depend only on $\lambda$ and not on $(p, q)$. Such a function is *stable*.

The theorem `LocalPC` of the development volumes is the existence of pseudo-coefficients for $\mathrm{U}(p, q)$, with odd $p + q$ and a parameter far from the walls, with these orbital integrals.

**Lemma (linear independence of characters).** Let $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_k$ be pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible admissible representations of $G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$, and $a_i \in \mathbb{C}$ with $\sum_i a_i \operatorname{tr} \sigma_i(f) = 0$ for each $f$ in the Hecke algebra. Then $a_i = 0$ for all $i$.

*Proof.* Choose $K_f$ compact open with $\sigma_i^{K_f} \ne 0$ for each $i$. The spaces $\sigma_i^{K_f}$ are finite dimensional, simple and pairwise non-isomorphic modules over the [[Hecke algebra|5J2P2AGY4N]] $\mathcal{H}(G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f}), K_f)$, and $\sigma \mapsto \sigma^{K_f}$ is injective on irreducible representations with $K_f$-fixed vectors. By the density theorem of Jacobson, $\mathcal{H} \to \prod_i \operatorname{End}(\sigma_i^{K_f})$ is onto. Take $f$ that acts by the identity on $\sigma_j^{K_f}$ and by $0$ on the others: the sum is $a_j \dim \sigma_j^{K_f}$. $\square$

**Proposition (comparison of two unitary groups).** Let $V, V'$ be Hermitian spaces of the same odd dimension over a CM extension $E/F$, with isometries $V_v \cong V'_v$ at each finite place, so that $G = \mathrm{U}(V)$ and $G' = \mathrm{U}(V')$ have isomorphic groups of finite adelic points. Fix a regular infinitesimal character $\lambda$ far from the walls at each infinite place. Then, for each irreducible $\sigma_f$ of $G(\mathbb{A}_{F,f})$, the sum of the cuspidal multiplicities $m(\sigma_f \otimes \pi_\infty)$ over $\pi_\infty$ in the discrete series packets $\Pi_\lambda$ of $G$ is equal to the same sum for $G'$.

*Sketch.* Take $f = f_\infty \otimes f_f$ with $f_\infty$ the sum of the pseudo-coefficients over $\Pi_\lambda$ and $f'_f$ the transport of $f_f$. An auxiliary place makes the simple trace formula apply. The geometric sides are sums over elliptic classes, matched by their characteristic polynomials; the archimedean orbital integrals depend only on $\lambda$ by stability, and the finite ones are preserved by the local isometries. So the traces are equal. Harish-Chandra finiteness gives a finite list of $\sigma_f$ on both spectral sides, and the linear independence of characters gives the equality term by term. $\square$

This is the shape of the equality of cuspidal multiplicities below [HypsAlt is nonempty](/step/nonempty-hyps/): its nine inputs are `LocalPC`, rapid decay, Harish-Chandra finiteness, Dixmier-Malliavin, the statement Green, a spectral trace statement, a separation statement (the lemma above), the elliptic trace and the elliptic expansion.

## The twisted trace formula and base change

Let $G$ be reductive over $F$ and $\theta$ an automorphism of $G$ over $F$ of finite order.

**Definition.** Elements $\delta, \delta'$ of $G$ are *$\theta$-conjugate* if $\delta' = x^{-1}\delta\theta(x)$ for some $x$. The *twisted centralizer* is $G_{\delta\theta} = \{x : x^{-1}\delta\theta(x) = \delta\}$. Over a local field, the *twisted orbital integral* is
$$TO_\delta(\phi) = \int_{G_{\delta\theta}(F_v) \backslash G(F_v)} \phi(x^{-1}\delta\theta(x))\, dx.$$

Let $I_\theta \psi = \psi \circ \theta^{-1}$ on $L^2(G(F) \backslash G(\mathbb{A}_F)^1)$. By the computation of the last section, $R(\phi) \circ I_\theta$ is the integral operator with kernel $K_{\phi,\theta}(x, y) = \sum_{\delta \in G(F)} \phi(x^{-1}\delta\theta(y))$. On the diagonal, the classes are now $\theta$-conjugacy classes, and the terms are twisted orbital integrals. On the spectral side only the $\theta$-stable $\pi$, with $\pi \circ \theta \cong \pi$, contribute, by the *twisted trace* $\operatorname{tr}(\pi(\phi) A_\pi)$ for an intertwining operator $A_\pi : \pi \to \pi \circ \theta$. The truncation and the expansions of Arthur extend to this setting; this is the *twisted trace formula*.

Now let $E/F$ be a cyclic extension of degree $\ell$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) = \langle\sigma\rangle$, $G = \mathrm{Res}_{E/F}\mathrm{GL}_n$ and $\theta = \sigma$, so $G(F) = \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$.

**Definition.** The *norm* of $\delta \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ is $N\delta = \delta\sigma(\delta) \cdots \sigma^{\ell-1}(\delta)$.

**Lemma.** If $N\delta$ is regular semisimple, its $\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$-conjugacy class meets $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ in exactly one $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$-conjugacy class, which depends only on the $\sigma$-conjugacy class of $\delta$.

*Proof.* We have $\sigma(N\delta) = \delta^{-1} (N\delta) \delta$, so the characteristic polynomial of $N\delta$ has coefficients in $F$. A regular semisimple class over $E$ is given by its characteristic polynomial, and the companion matrix of that polynomial lies in $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$. Two elements of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ that are conjugate over $E$ are conjugate over $F$, by the rational canonical form. Finally $N(x^{-1}\delta\sigma(x)) = x^{-1}(N\delta)x$. $\square$

This gives the *norm map* $\delta \mapsto \mathcal{N}\delta$ from regular twisted classes to classes of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$. Then $G_{\delta\sigma}$ is an inner form of the centralizer of $\mathcal{N}\delta$, and we choose compatible measures.

**Definition.** Let $v$ be a place of $F$. Functions $\phi_v$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(E \otimes F_v)$ and $f_v$ on $\mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$ are *associated* (or *match*) if, for each regular semisimple $\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$, $O_\gamma(f_v) = TO_\delta(\phi_v)$ when $\gamma = \mathcal{N}\delta$, and $O_\gamma(f_v) = 0$ when $\gamma$ is not a norm.

**Theorem (transfer; Arthur and Clozel).** For each $\phi_v$ there is an associated $f_v$, and associated functions satisfy the character identity $\operatorname{tr}(\Pi(\phi_v) A_\Pi) = \operatorname{tr} \pi(f_v)$ when $\Pi$ is the local base change of $\pi$, for a suitable normalization of $A_\Pi$.

The reference is Arthur and Clozel, *Simple Algebras, Base Change, and the Advanced Theory of the Trace Formula*, Annals of Math. Studies 120 (1989), the chapter on local results.

**Theorem (fundamental lemma for base change).** Let $E_v/F_v$ be unramified and $v$ finite. The characteristic functions of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_{E_v})$ and $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_{F_v})$ are associated, for the measures that give these groups volume $1$. More generally, $\phi_v$ in the spherical Hecke algebra is associated with its image under the base change homomorphism $b$ of spherical Hecke algebras, dual to $t \mapsto t^{\ell}$ on Satake parameters.

The case of the unit is Kottwitz, *Base change for unit elements of Hecke algebras*, Compositio Math. 60 (1986). The full Hecke algebra is Clozel, *The fundamental lemma for stable base change*, Duke Math. J. 61 (1990), and Labesse, *Fonctions élémentaires et lemme fondamental pour le changement de base stable*, Duke Math. J. 61 (1990). In the development volumes, the local matching of units `LocUnit` is this theorem, and the global matching of test functions is built from it.

*The global comparison.* Take $\phi = \bigotimes_v \phi_v$ and $f = \bigotimes_v f_v$ associated at each place, units at almost all places. On the geometric sides, the elliptic terms match class by class, by the definition of association and the equality of the global volumes. With simple conditions at two places, the other terms vanish, and the equality of the spectral sides $I_\theta(\phi) = I(f)$ gives, through the linear independence of characters, the base change of [Arthur and Clozel](/learn/automorphic-forms/#base-change).

**Remark (unitary groups).** Take instead $G = \mathrm{Res}_{E/F}\mathrm{GL}_n$ for a CM extension $E/F$ and $\theta(g) = J\, {}^t\bar g^{-1} J^{-1}$, with $N(\delta) = \delta\theta(\delta)$. The $\theta$-stable representations are the conjugate self-dual ones, and the regular twisted classes correspond to stable classes of unitary groups. The comparison of the twisted trace formula with the stable trace formula of a unitary group is *stable base change*, in Labesse, *Cohomologie, stabilisation et changement de base*, Astérisque 257 (1999), and Labesse, *Changement de base CM et séries discrètes* (2011). This is the base change of [BaseChangeIsobaricN](/step/base-change-isobaric-n-def/).

**Remark (the elliptic part).** In the development volumes, $E$ is a CM field with at least two infinite places, $\theta(g) = {}^t\bar g^{-1}$, and the test functions are *very cuspidal* at each infinite place, a twisted version of cuspidality in the sense of Labesse. An element $d \in \mathrm{GL}_n(E)$ is regular elliptic if the characteristic polynomial of $d\theta(d)$ is separable with all roots of absolute value $1$ at each embedding. For a truncation parameter large compared with the support, the truncated twisted trace on $\mathrm{GL}_a \times \mathrm{GL}_b$ is the sum of the twisted orbital integrals over pairs of regular elliptic classes: the terms of regular non-elliptic classes vanish, by a flag at some infinite place along which the very cuspidal function integrates to $0$. The archimedean matching uses Clozel, *Changement de base pour les représentations tempérées des groupes réductifs réels*, Ann. Sci. ENS 15 (1982).

## The relative trace formula and the Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma

The [relative trace formula](/learn/periods/#orbit-integrals-and-the-relative-trace-formula) integrates the kernel $K_f(x, y)$ over $[H_1] \times [H_2]$, with $[H] = H(F) \backslash H(\mathbb{A}_F)$. Its spectral side is a sum over $\pi$ of products of an $H_1$-period and an $H_2$-period, its geometric side a sum over double cosets of relative orbital integrals.

**Example.** For $G \times G$ and $H_1 = H_2 = \Delta G$ the diagonal, the double cosets $\Delta G(F) \backslash (G \times G)(F) / \Delta G(F)$ are the conjugacy classes of $G(F)$, and the relative trace formula of $f_1 \otimes f_2$ is the trace formula of $f_1^\vee * f_2$. So the trace formula is a relative trace formula.

Let $E/F$ be a quadratic extension of number fields with character $\eta = \eta_{E/F}$ of $\mathbb{A}_F^\times / F^\times$. Let $W \subseteq V$ be Hermitian spaces over $E$ of dimensions $n$ and $n + 1$, with $V = W \oplus Eu$.

**Definition (Jacquet and Rallis).** The *unitary side* is $G = \mathrm{U}(W) \times \mathrm{U}(V)$ with $H_1 = H_2 = \mathrm{U}(W)$, embedded diagonally; its period is the Gross-Prasad period of [the periods chapter](/learn/periods/#the-gan-gross-prasad-conjectures). The *general linear side* is $G' = \mathrm{Res}_{E/F}(\mathrm{GL}_n \times \mathrm{GL}_{n+1})$, with $H'_1 = \mathrm{Res}_{E/F}\mathrm{GL}_n$, embedded diagonally, and $H'_2 = \mathrm{GL}_n \times \mathrm{GL}_{n+1}$ over $F$, with a character built from $\eta$. The $H'_1$-period is a Rankin-Selberg integral, and the $H'_2$-period detects the base changes from unitary groups.

The geometric sides are compared on the Lie algebras. Let $\mathfrak{g}_n = \mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}(F)$ with $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ acting by conjugation, and write $Y = \begin{pmatrix} A & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ with $A$ of size $n$. On the unitary side, $\mathrm{U}(W)$ acts by conjugation on the Hermitian matrices of size $n + 1$ for $V$.

**Definition.** The *invariants* of $Y$ are the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of $A$, the numbers $cA^ib$ for $0 \le i \le n - 1$, and $d$. The element $Y$ is *regular semisimple* if $\Delta(Y) = \det(cA^{i+j}b)_{0 \le i, j \le n-1} \ne 0$; then its stabilizer is trivial and its orbit is closed. Elements on the two sides *match* if they have the same invariants. The *transfer factor* is $\omega(Y) = \eta(\det(e, eY, \dots, eY^n))$, with $e = (0, \dots, 0, 1)$; it satisfies $\omega(g^{-1} Y g) = \eta(\det g)\omega(Y)$.

**Proposition (Jacquet and Rallis).** Over a $p$-adic field, matching gives a bijection between the regular semisimple $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$-orbits in $\mathfrak{g}_n$ and the union, over the two isomorphism classes of Hermitian spaces $V$ of dimension $n + 1$, of the regular semisimple $\mathrm{U}(W)$-orbits.

**Definition.** For $f'$ on $\mathfrak{g}_n$ and regular semisimple $Y$, $O(Y, f') = \int_{\mathrm{GL}_n(F)} f'(g^{-1}Yg)\eta(\det g)\, dg$. For $f$ on the Hermitian matrices of $V$, $O(X, f) = \int_{\mathrm{U}(W)} f(h^{-1}Xh)\, dh$. Then $f'$ and the pair $(f_V)_V$ *match* if $\omega(Y) O(Y, f') = O(X, f_V)$ whenever $X$ for $V$ matches $Y$.

**Theorem (Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma).** Let $E/F$ be an unramified quadratic extension of $p$-adic fields, and give $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_F)$ and $\mathrm{U}(W)(\mathcal{O}_F)$ volume $1$. Then $\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}(\mathcal{O}_F)}$ matches the pair whose component for the split space with a self-dual lattice is the characteristic function of its integral Hermitian matrices, and whose other component is $0$.

The conjecture is in Jacquet and Rallis, *On the Gross-Prasad conjecture for unitary groups*, Clay Math. Proc. 13 (2011). Yun proved it for large residue characteristic (*The fundamental lemma of Jacquet and Rallis*, Duke Math. J. 156, 2011, with an appendix by Gordon), and Beuzart-Plessis proved it in general (*A new proof of the Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma*, Duke Math. J. 170, 2021).

**Theorem (smooth transfer; W. Zhang).** Over a $p$-adic field, each smooth compactly supported $f'$ has a matching pair, and each pair has a matching $f'$. The transfer commutes with partial Fourier transforms up to a constant, a Weil constant of the [[Weil representation|H3Q2703KKV]].

The reference is W. Zhang, *Fourier transform and the global Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture for unitary groups*, Ann. of Math. 180 (2014). The development volumes prove the fundamental lemma for unit functions, in its Lie algebra form, by induction on $n$ in this spirit: $\mathrm{SL}_2(F)$ acts on test functions on both sides by a Weil representation for $L = K \otimes_F E$, with $w$ acting by a partial Fourier transform; the elements that preserve the matching of units form a monoid that contains $w$ and enough of the $n(t)$, so it is all of $\mathrm{SL}_2(F)$.

**Remark.** The comparison of the two relative trace formulas, with the fundamental lemma and smooth transfer, proves the global Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture for $\mathrm{U}(n) \times \mathrm{U}(n+1)$: Zhang (2014) under local conditions; Beuzart-Plessis, Liu, Zhang and Zhu, *Isolation of cuspidal spectrum, with application to the Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture*, Ann. of Math. 194 (2021); and Beuzart-Plessis, Chaudouard and Zydor (2022) in the endoscopic case. The proof reaches its periods by theta lifts instead, as [the periods chapter](/learn/periods/#orbit-integrals-and-the-relative-trace-formula) explains, and uses the Jacquet-Rallis fundamental lemma only below [EveryCharacter](/step/every-character/).

## Rankin-Selberg integrals

A *Rankin-Selberg integral* integrates a product of automorphic forms against an Eisenstein series and unfolds it to an Euler product.

**Theorem (Rankin, 1939; Selberg, 1940).** Let $f = \sum a_n q^n$ and $g = \sum b_n q^n$ be cusp forms of weight $k$ for $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, and $d\mu = dx\, dy/y^2$. For $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$,
$$\int_{\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}} f(z) \overline{g(z)} y^k E(z, s)\, d\mu = \frac{\Gamma(s + k - 1)}{(4\pi)^{s+k-1}} \sum_{n \ge 1} \frac{a_n \overline{b_n}}{n^{s+k-1}}.$$

*Proof.* The function $f\bar g y^k$ is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$-invariant and bounded. Unfold with $\Gamma = \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ and $\Lambda$ the image of $\Gamma_\infty$; a fundamental domain for $\Lambda$ is the strip $0 \le x \le 1$. So the integral is
$$\int_0^\infty \int_0^1 f(z)\overline{g(z)}\, y^{s+k-2}\, dx\, dy = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n \overline{b_n} \int_0^\infty e^{-4\pi n y} y^{s+k-2}\, dy,$$
by orthogonality of $e^{2\pi i n x}$ on $[0, 1]$. The last integral is $\Gamma(s+k-1)(4\pi n)^{-(s+k-1)}$. The exchanges hold for $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ by absolute convergence. $\square$

**Corollary.** The Dirichlet series $D(w) = \sum_n a_n \overline{b_n} n^{-w}$ continues meromorphically to $\mathbb{C}$. In $\operatorname{Re}(w) \ge k$ it is holomorphic except for a simple pole at $w = k$ with residue $\frac{3}{\pi} \frac{(4\pi)^k}{\Gamma(k)} \langle f, g \rangle$, where $\langle f, g\rangle$ is the [Petersson inner product](/learn/automorphic-forms/#modular-forms). If $f$ and $g$ are Hecke eigenforms, then $\zeta(2w - 2k + 2) D(w) = L(w, f \times \bar g) = \prod_p \prod_{i,j} (1 - \alpha_{p,i}\overline{\beta_{p,j}} p^{-w})^{-1}$, where $\alpha_{p,1}, \alpha_{p,2}$ are the roots of $X^2 - a_p X + p^{k-1}$ and $\beta_{p,j}$ those for $g$.

*Proof.* Put $w = s + k - 1$ and use the continuation of $E(z, s)$ and its residue $3/\pi$ at $s = 1$. In $\operatorname{Re}(s) \ge 1$, $E^*$ has its only pole at $s = 1$, and $\xi(2s)$ has no zero, since $\zeta$ has no zero on $\operatorname{Re} \ge 1$. The Euler product is a computation with the Hecke relations, as in Bump, Chapter 1. $\square$

For $f = g$ the coefficients $|a_n|^2$ are nonnegative, and the Wiener-Ikehara Tauberian theorem gives $\sum_{n \le X} |a_n|^2 \sim \frac{R}{k} X^k$ with $R$ the residue above. This is the Ramanujan bound on average.

**Example (Hecke's integral, adelic form).** Let $\varphi$ be a cusp form on $\mathrm{GL}_2$ over $F$ with Whittaker function $W_\varphi$ for a character $\psi$ of $\mathbb{A}_F/F$. By the [Fourier expansion of cusp forms](/learn/automorphic-forms/#fourier-coefficients-and-whittaker-functions), $\varphi(g) = \sum_{\alpha \in F^\times} W_\varphi(\mathrm{diag}(\alpha, 1) g)$. So, for $\operatorname{Re}(s)$ large, unfolding the sum over $F^\times$ gives
$$\int_{F^\times \backslash \mathbb{A}_F^\times} \varphi\begin{pmatrix} t & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} |t|^{s - 1/2}\, d^\times t = \int_{\mathbb{A}_F^\times} W_\varphi\begin{pmatrix} t & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} |t|^{s - 1/2}\, d^\times t.$$
The left side is entire in $s$, by rapid decay. For factorizable $W_\varphi$ the right side is a product of local integrals, equal to $L^S(s, \pi)$ times finitely many local factors.

Now let $\pi, \pi'$ be unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ with central characters $\omega, \omega'$.

**Definition.** For a *Schwartz-Bruhat* function $\Phi$ on $\mathbb{A}_F^n$ and a unitary character $\mu$ of $F^\times \backslash \mathbb{A}_F^\times$, the *mirabolic Eisenstein series* is
$$E(g, \Phi, s) = |\det g|^s \int_{F^\times \backslash \mathbb{A}_F^\times} \sum_{\xi \in F^n \setminus \{0\}} \Phi(t \xi g)\, |t|^{ns} \mu(t)\, d^\times t.$$
It is left invariant under $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$, since $\xi \mapsto \xi\gamma$ permutes $F^n \setminus \{0\}$. It is the Eisenstein series of the parabolic of type $(n - 1, 1)$, written by Tate's method ([Tate's thesis](/learn/class-field-theory/#tates-thesis-and-hecke-l-functions)).

**Theorem (Jacquet and Shalika; Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika).** $E(g, \Phi, s)$ continues meromorphically to $\mathbb{C}$ and satisfies $E(g, \Phi, s) = E({}^tg^{-1}, \hat\Phi, 1 - s)$ (with $\mu$ replaced by $\mu^{-1}$). It is entire unless $\mu|\cdot|^{ins}$ is trivial for some real number; then its poles are at most simple, at $s = 0$ and $s = 1$ up to this shift, with residue at $s = 1$ a constant times $\hat\Phi(0)$.

*Proof.* Split the integral over $|t| \ge 1$ and $|t| \le 1$, and apply the [[Poisson summation formula|GARGJYSGFG]] for $F^n \subseteq \mathbb{A}_F^n$ to the second part, exactly as in Riemann's proof of the functional equation of $\zeta$. The terms $\xi = 0$ give the poles. $\square$

**Theorem (unfolding of the Rankin-Selberg integral).** Let $\varphi \in \pi$, $\varphi' \in \pi'$ be cusp forms, and $\mu = (\omega\omega')^{-1}$. For $\operatorname{Re}(s)$ large,
$$\begin{aligned} I(s, \varphi, \varphi', \Phi) &= \int_{Z(\mathbb{A}_F)\mathrm{GL}_n(F) \backslash \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)} \varphi(g)\varphi'(g) E(g, \Phi, s)\, dg \\ &= \int_{N_n(\mathbb{A}_F) \backslash \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)} W_\varphi(g) W'_{\varphi'}(g) \Phi(e_n g) |\det g|^s\, dg, \end{aligned}$$
with $W_\varphi$ for $\psi_N$ and $W'_{\varphi'}$ for $\psi_N^{-1}$, and $e_n = (0, \dots, 0, 1)$. For factorizable data this is $\prod_v \Psi_v(s, W_v, W'_v, \Phi_v)$, and at a place $v$ where everything is unramified, $\Psi_v = L(s, \pi_v \times \pi'_v)$.

*Proof sketch.* The orbit of $e_n$ under $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ is $F^n \setminus \{0\}$, with stabilizer the mirabolic subgroup $P_n$. Unfold the integral over $F^\times$ with the center, and then the sum over $P_n(F) \backslash \mathrm{GL}_n(F)$. Expand $\varphi$ on $P_n$ by its Whittaker function, as in the theorem of Shalika and Piatetski-Shapiro on [Fourier coefficients](/learn/automorphic-forms/#fourier-coefficients-and-whittaker-functions), and unfold once more. The unramified value is the computation of Jacquet and Shalika with Shintani's formula for the spherical Whittaker function. $\square$

The local integrals $\Psi_v$ and the factors $L(s, \pi_v \times \pi'_v)$ are those of [generic representations and local factors](/learn/representations/#generic-representations-and-local-factors).

**Theorem (Jacquet and Shalika, 1981).** Let $\pi, \pi'$ be unitary cuspidal on $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ and $S$ a finite set of places containing the infinite places and the ramified ones. The partial L-function $L^S(s, \pi \times \tilde\pi')$ continues meromorphically, is holomorphic and nonzero on $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1$ except at $s = 1$, and has a pole at $s = 1$ if and only if $\pi' \cong \pi$; the pole is then simple.

The reference is Jacquet and Shalika, *On Euler products and the classification of automorphic representations I and II*, Amer. J. Math. 103 (1981). The nonvanishing on $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1$ is also in Shahidi, *On certain L-functions*, Amer. J. Math. 103 (1981). The residue at $s = 1$ comes from $\hat\Phi(0)$ and the Petersson inner product $\int \varphi \bar\varphi$; for $\pi' \not\cong \pi$ the inner product of $\pi$ and $\pi'$ is $0$, and there is no pole.

*Proof of strong multiplicity one.* Let $\pi_v \cong \pi'_v$ for all $v \notin S$. Then $L^S(s, \pi \times \tilde\pi') = L^S(s, \pi \times \tilde\pi)$, which has a pole at $s = 1$. By the theorem, $\pi' \cong \pi$, and by multiplicity one they are the same space of cusp forms. $\square$

**Remark.** For $m < n$ and $\pi'$ cuspidal on $\mathrm{GL}_m$, the global integral is $\int_{\mathrm{GL}_m(F) \backslash \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{A}_F)} \mathbb{P}\varphi\begin{pmatrix} h & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \varphi'(h) |\det h|^{s - 1/2}\, dh$, with $\mathbb{P}$ a projection to the mirabolic subgroup, and no Eisenstein series is needed. It unfolds to the local integrals of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika, *Rankin-Selberg convolutions*, Amer. J. Math. 105 (1983).

The development volumes prove the pole statement `RSPole` for $\pi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_m$ and $\pi'$ on $\mathrm{GL}_k$ over a totally complex field, $k \le m$: there are functions $Z$ and $Z_S$, holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0$, with $Z_S$ nonzero on a vertical line, $Z(s) = (s-1) Z_S(s) L^S(s)$, and $Z(1) = 0$ when $\pi \ne \pi'$. The statement `jacquetShalika` of [UnitaryGroupThirdPlaceHypotheses](/step/unitary-group-third-place-hypotheses-def/) and the input `JacquetShalika` of [the literature layer](/proof/literature/) are of this kind, and strong multiplicity one is `CuspidalMultiplicityOne`.
