Here a ring is commutative with $1$. [The geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/) defines schemes and the main classes of morphisms, and [the algebra chapter](/learn/algebra/) defines ideals, modules and length; we do not repeat them.

## Local rings and dimension

**Definition.** Let $A$ be a ring and $S \subseteq A$ a *multiplicative subset*: $1 \in S$ and $st \in S$ for $s, t \in S$. The *localization* $S^{-1}A$ is the set of fractions $a/s$ with $a \in A$ and $s \in S$, where $a/s = b/t$ if $u(at - bs) = 0$ for some $u \in S$. For an $A$-module $M$ put $S^{-1}M = S^{-1}A \otimes_A M$. For a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$, the [[localization|X6JD4AG973]] at $\mathfrak p$ is $A_{\mathfrak p} = S^{-1}A$ with $S = A \setminus \mathfrak p$, and for $f \in A$ we write $A_f$ for $S = \{1, f, f^2, \dots\}$.

**Proposition.** The functor $M \mapsto S^{-1}M$ is exact. The prime ideals of $S^{-1}A$ are the ideals $S^{-1}\mathfrak p$ for the primes $\mathfrak p$ of $A$ with $\mathfrak p \cap S = \emptyset$. A module $M$ is $0$ if and only if $M_{\mathfrak m} = 0$ for each maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and a map of modules is injective (or surjective) if and only if it is so after localization at each maximal ideal (Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 3).

**Definition.** A ring $A$ is a *[[local ring|MZ02BKPHJA]]* if it has exactly one maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. We write $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ with $k = A/\mathfrak m$ the *residue field*. A ring map $\varphi : A \to B$ of local rings is *local* if $\varphi(\mathfrak m_A) \subseteq \mathfrak m_B$. A ring is *[[noetherian|0670T59HGE]]* if each ideal is finitely generated.

For each prime $\mathfrak p$, $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak p A_{\mathfrak p}$ and residue field $k(\mathfrak p) = \operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p)$. By the Hilbert basis theorem, if $A$ is noetherian then so are $A[x]$, $A[[x]]$, each quotient and each localization of $A$; a finitely generated module over a noetherian ring has all submodules finitely generated.

**Lemma ([[Nakayama|15JHBYR6KH]]).** Let $A$ be a ring, $I$ an ideal contained in each maximal ideal, and $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module with $IM = M$. Then $M = 0$.

*Proof.* Let $m_1, \dots, m_n$ generate $M$. Since $IM = M$, $m_i = \sum_j a_{ij} m_j$ with $a_{ij} \in I$. Let $T = 1 - (a_{ij})$, an $n \times n$ matrix over $A$. Then $T$ kills the column $(m_j)$, and so does $\operatorname{adj}(T) T = \det(T) \cdot 1$. So $\det(T) M = 0$. Expanding, $\det T = 1 + x$ with $x \in I$. Then $1 + x$ lies in no maximal ideal, so it is a unit, and $M = 0$. $\square$

**Corollary.** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be local and $M$ finitely generated. Elements $m_1, \dots, m_n \in M$ generate $M$ if and only if their images span the $k$-vector space $M/\mathfrak m M$. So the least number of generators of $M$ is $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$.

*Proof.* Apply the lemma to $M/N$, where $N$ is the submodule that the $m_i$ generate. $\square$

**Definition.** The *[[Krull dimension|EX7N9D1YCD]]* $\dim A$ is the supremum of the lengths $n$ of chains $\mathfrak p_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak p_1 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak p_n$ of prime ideals. The *[[height|87WH65FJB8]]* of a prime $\mathfrak p$ is $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak p = \dim A_{\mathfrak p}$. For a module $M$, $\dim M = \dim A/\operatorname{ann}(M)$.

**Theorem ([[Krull's principal ideal theorem|SJZW2HN1SC]]).** Let $A$ be noetherian, and let $\mathfrak p$ be a prime that is minimal among the primes that contain an ideal generated by $r$ elements. Then $\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak p \le r$. In particular a minimal prime over a principal ideal has height at most $1$ (Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 11).

**Theorem (dimension theory of local rings).** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be a noetherian local ring and $\mathfrak q$ an $\mathfrak m$-primary ideal. For large $n$, $\operatorname{len}_A(A/\mathfrak q^n)$ is a polynomial in $n$, the *Hilbert-Samuel polynomial*. Its degree $d$ does not depend on $\mathfrak q$, and $d = \dim A$ is also the least number of elements of $\mathfrak m$ that generate an $\mathfrak m$-primary ideal (Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 11).

So $\dim A$ is finite, and $\dim A \le \dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, because by the corollary above $\mathfrak m$ is generated by $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ elements. A set of $\dim A$ elements that generate an $\mathfrak m$-primary ideal is a *system of parameters*.

**Theorem (Noether normalization).** Let $k$ be a field and $A$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra that is a domain. There are elements $y_1, \dots, y_d \in A$, algebraically independent over $k$, such that $A$ is finite over $k[y_1, \dots, y_d]$. Then $d = \dim A$ is the transcendence degree of $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ over $k$, and $\dim A_{\mathfrak m} = d$ for each maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ (Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapters 5 and 11; Matsumura, §5).

**Definition.** A ring extension $A \subseteq B$ is *integral* if each $b \in B$ is a root of a monic polynomial in $A[x]$. A domain is *[[integrally closed|H20P600HQS]]*, or *normal*, if it contains each element of its fraction field that is integral over it. A ring is *normal* if each of its localizations at primes is a normal domain.

For $A \subseteq B$ integral, each prime of $A$ is $\mathfrak q \cap A$ for some prime $\mathfrak q$ of $B$ (lying over), chains of primes lift (going up), and $\dim A = \dim B$ (Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 5).

**Definition (multiplicity at a prime).** Let $A$ be noetherian, $I$ an ideal and $W$ a prime that is minimal over $I$. Then $A_W / I A_W$ is a local ring of dimension $0$, so it has finite length. The *multiplicity* of $I$ at $W$ is $\operatorname{mult}_W(I) = \operatorname{len}_{A_W}(A_W/IA_W)$.

**Example.** In $A = k[x, y]$ let $I = (y, y - x^2) = (y, x^2)$ and $W = (x, y)$. Then $A_W / IA_W$ has basis $1, x$, so $\operatorname{mult}_W(I) = 2$: the parabola $y = x^2$ meets the line $y = 0$ at the origin with multiplicity $2$. The chapter of Volume 3 on formal algebraization computes the multiplicity of a component of a hyperplane section in this way, as a sum of the orders of the local branches.

## Flat and étale algebras

**Definition.** An $A$-module $M$ is *[[flat|73RYYEVDKA]]* if $N \mapsto N \otimes_A M$ takes injective maps to injective maps, so that it is exact. It is *[[faithfully flat|C31PYYPRGC]]* if it is flat and $N \otimes_A M = 0$ only for $N = 0$. An $A$-algebra $B$ is flat (faithfully flat) if it is so as an $A$-module. A morphism of schemes $f : X \to Y$ is *[[flat|5M6KS8F47R]]* at $x$ if $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is flat over $\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)}$.

**Example.** Free modules, projective modules and localizations $S^{-1}A$ are flat over $A$. The polynomial ring $A[x]$ is a faithfully flat module. The $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\mathbb{Q}$ is flat and not faithfully flat, since $\mathbb{Z}/p \otimes \mathbb{Q} = 0$. The $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\mathbb{Z}/n$ with $n \ge 2$ is not flat, since multiplication by $n$ on $\mathbb{Z}$ is injective and on $\mathbb{Z}/n$ it is $0$.

**Proposition.** An $A$-module $M$ is flat if and only if $I \otimes_A M \to M$ is injective for each finitely generated ideal $I$, if and only if $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I, M) = 0$ for each such $I$ (Matsumura, §7).

**Proposition (flatness over a principal ideal domain).** Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain, for example a discrete valuation ring. An $R$-module $M$ is flat if and only if it is torsion free.

*Proof.* Each ideal is $(a)$ for some $a$. For $a = 0$ the map $(a) \otimes M \to M$ is injective. For $a \neq 0$, $(a) \cong R$ by $r \mapsto ra$, and $(a) \otimes M \to M$ becomes $m \mapsto am$. So $M$ is flat if and only if multiplication by each $a \ne 0$ is injective on $M$, that is, $M$ is torsion free. $\square$

So a scheme $X$ over a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\pi$ is flat if and only if $\mathcal{O}_X$ has no $\pi$-torsion; for $X$ noetherian this means that no associated point of $X$ lies in the special fibre (Hartshorne, III.9.7). Integral models of Shimura varieties and abelian schemes over $\mathcal{O}_v$ are flat in this sense.

**Proposition (free module over a local ring).** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be a noetherian local ring and $M$ a finitely generated flat $A$-module. Then $M$ is [[free|0DZ9DKAKZR]].

*Proof.* Lift a basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$ to $m_1, \dots, m_n \in M$. By the corollary of Nakayama's lemma, the map $\varphi : A^n \to M$, $e_i \mapsto m_i$, is surjective. Its kernel $K$ is finitely generated, since $A$ is noetherian. Tensor $0 \to K \to A^n \to M \to 0$ with $k$. Since $M$ is flat, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(k, M) = 0$, so $0 \to K/\mathfrak m K \to k^n \to M/\mathfrak m M \to 0$ is exact. The last map is an isomorphism, so $K / \mathfrak m K = 0$, and $K = 0$ by Nakayama's lemma. $\square$

**Corollary.** Let $A$ be noetherian and $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module. The following are equivalent: $M$ is flat; $M$ is [[projective|BFFA03GE3V]]; $M_{\mathfrak p}$ is free for each prime $\mathfrak p$; there are $f_1, \dots, f_r$ that generate the unit ideal with each $M_{f_i}$ free. The *rank* $\mathfrak p \mapsto \operatorname{rank} M_{\mathfrak p}$ is then locally constant on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ (Matsumura, §7).

**Definition.** A finite morphism $f : V \to T$ is *finite locally free* if $f_* \mathcal{O}_V$ is a locally free $\mathcal{O}_T$-module of finite rank. Its *rank* at $t \in T$ is the rank of the free $\mathcal{O}_{T,t}$-module $(f_*\mathcal{O}_V)_t$. It is locally constant, so constant on a connected $T$.

A finite étale morphism is finite locally free. The chapter of Volume 3 on finite étale covers builds a scheme that classifies the open and closed subschemes of fixed rank of a finite étale scheme.

**Theorem (faithful flatness).** Let $A \to B$ be a flat ring map. The following are equivalent: $B$ is faithfully flat over $A$; the map $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is surjective; $\mathfrak m B \neq B$ for each maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $A$. In particular a flat local homomorphism of local rings is faithfully flat. If $B$ is faithfully flat, a sequence of $A$-modules is exact if and only if it is exact after $- \otimes_A B$ (Matsumura, §7).

**Theorem (faithfully flat descent).** Let $A \to B$ be faithfully flat and $M$ an $A$-module. Then
$$0 \to M \to M \otimes_A B \rightrightarrows M \otimes_A B \otimes_A B,$$
with the two maps $m \otimes b \mapsto m \otimes b \otimes 1$ and $m \otimes b \mapsto m \otimes 1 \otimes b$, is an equalizer diagram. A $B$-module with a descent datum relative to $A \to B$ is $N \otimes_A B$ for an $A$-module $N$, unique up to unique isomorphism (Bosch, Lütkebohmert and Raynaud, Néron Models, Chapter 6).

**Theorem (local criterion of flatness).** Let $A \to B$ be a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings, $k$ the residue field of $A$, and $M$ a finitely generated $B$-module. Then $M$ is flat over $A$ if and only if $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(k, M) = 0$. If $t \in \mathfrak m_A$ is a nonzerodivisor on $M$ and $M/tM$ is flat over $A/tA$, then $M$ is flat over $A$ (Matsumura, §22).

**Theorem (dimension of a local homomorphism).** Let $A \to B$ be a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings. Then $\dim B \le \dim A + \dim B/\mathfrak m_A B$, with equality if $B$ is flat over $A$ (Matsumura, §15).

### Étale algebras

**Definition.** A ring map $A \to B$ is of *finite presentation* if $B \cong A[x_1, \dots, x_n]/(f_1, \dots, f_m)$. It is *[[unramified|GY3QA6NH0E]]* if it is of finite type and the module of Kähler differentials $\Omega_{B/A}$ is $0$. It is *[[étale|FTRW0679E2]]* if it is flat, of finite presentation and unramified. It is *standard étale* if $B \cong (A[x]/(f))_g$ with $f$ monic and the derivative $f'$ invertible in $(A[x]/(f))_g$.

A morphism of schemes is étale if it is so on affine opens; this agrees with the definition by smoothness of relative dimension $0$ in [the geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/). Étale maps are stable under base change and composition.

**Proposition.** Let $k$ be a field. A $k$-algebra is étale if and only if it is a finite product of finite separable field extensions of $k$. A local ring map $A \to B$ that is étale is, after inverting an element of $B$ outside the maximal ideal, standard étale (Milne, Étale Cohomology, I, §3).

**Example (Kummer covers).** Let $A$ be a ring, $n \ge 1$ an integer that is a unit in $A$ and $u \in A^\times$. Then $B = A[x]/(x^n - u)$ is finite free of rank $n$, and $x$ is a unit in $B$ since $x^n = u$. So the derivative $n x^{n-1}$ is a unit, and $B$ is standard étale with $g = 1$. If $A$ contains a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta$, then $x \mapsto \zeta x$ makes $\mathbb{Z}/n$ act on $B$.

**Definition.** Let $B$ be a ring and $q \ge 1$. A *$\mathbb{Z}/q$-torsor* over $B$ is a $B$-algebra $D$ that is finite, projective and faithfully flat as a $B$-module, with an action $g \mapsto \sigma_g$ of $\mathbb{Z}/q$ by $B$-algebra automorphisms, such that
$$D \otimes_B D \to \prod_{g \in \mathbb{Z}/q} D, \qquad x \otimes y \mapsto (x\, \sigma_g(y))_g$$
is an isomorphism. A torsor is *trivial* if it is isomorphic to $\prod_{g} B$ with $\mathbb{Z}/q$ acting by translation, that is, if it has a $B$-algebra map $D \to B$.

The Kummer cover of the example is a $\mathbb{Z}/n$-torsor. Torsors under $\mathbb{Z}/q$ are classes in $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(\operatorname{Spec} B, \mathbb{Z}/q)$, and the chapters of Volume 3 on purity show that such torsors are trivial over certain generic fibres of strictly henselian rings.

## Completion

**Definition.** Let $A$ be a ring, $I$ an ideal and $M$ an $A$-module. The *$I$-adic [[completion|KBN13NPYJQ]]* of $M$ is $\widehat M = \varprojlim_n M/I^n M$. The module $M$ is *separated* if $\bigcap_n I^n M = 0$, and *[[complete|G9Q61B8A7C]]* if the map $M \to \widehat M$ is an isomorphism; so complete includes separated.

**Example.** The $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$ are the $(p)$-adic completion of $\mathbb{Z}$. The power series ring $k[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$ is the completion of $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, and also of its localization at $(x_1, \dots, x_n)$, for the ideal $(x_1, \dots, x_n)$. For each ring $A$, $A[[x]]$ is complete for $(x)$.

**Lemma ([[Artin-Rees|30H6B0G2FT]]).** Let $A$ be noetherian, $I$ an ideal, $M$ a finitely generated module and $N \subseteq M$ a submodule. There is $c \ge 0$ such that $I^n M \cap N = I^{n-c}(I^c M \cap N)$ for each $n \ge c$. So the topology that the $I$-adic topology of $M$ induces on $N$ is the $I$-adic topology of $N$ (Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 10).

**Theorem (Krull intersection theorem).** Let $A$ be noetherian, $I$ an ideal and $M$ a finitely generated module. Then $\bigcap_n I^n M$ is the set of $m \in M$ with $(1 + x)m = 0$ for some $x \in I$. In particular, if $A$ is local and $I \neq A$, then $\bigcap_n I^n M = 0$.

*Proof.* Put $N = \bigcap_n I^n M$. By the Artin-Rees lemma, for $n = c + 1$, $N = I^{c+1} M \cap N = I(I^c M \cap N) \subseteq IN$, so $N = IN$. The determinant trick in the proof of Nakayama's lemma gives $x \in I$ with $(1 + x) N = 0$. Conversely, if $(1 + x) m = 0$, then $m = -xm = x^2 m = \dots$ lies in each $I^n M$. In a local ring with $I \ne A$, $1 + x$ is a unit. $\square$

**Theorem.** Let $A$ be noetherian and $I$ an ideal. Then:

1. $\widehat A$ is noetherian and complete for $I \widehat A$;
2. for each finitely generated module $M$, $\widehat M \cong M \otimes_A \widehat A$, and $M \mapsto \widehat M$ is exact on finitely generated modules;
3. $\widehat A$ is flat over $A$;
4. if $A$ is local and $I \subseteq \mathfrak m$, then $\widehat A$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m \widehat A$ and the same residue field, $\widehat A$ is faithfully flat over $A$, and $\dim \widehat A = \dim A$

(Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 10; Matsumura, §8).

So completion is a flat base change. With flat base change for cohomology below, $H^i(X, \mathcal F) \otimes_A \widehat A \cong H^i(X_{\widehat A}, \mathcal F_{\widehat A})$ for $X$ separated of finite type over a noetherian ring $A$ and $\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent.

**Theorem (Cohen structure theorem).** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be a complete noetherian local ring. Then $A$ is a quotient of a ring $C[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$, where $C = k$ if $A$ contains a field, and otherwise $C$ is a complete discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal $pC$ and residue field $k$, for $p$ the characteristic of $k$. If $A$ is regular of dimension $d$ (defined below) and contains a field, then $A \cong k[[x_1, \dots, x_d]]$ (Matsumura, §29).

**Example.** Let $X$ be smooth of dimension $d$ over a field $k$ and $x \in X(k)$. Then $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x} \cong k[[t_1, \dots, t_d]]$, where the $t_i$ are local coordinates at $x$. For $W(k)$ the Witt vectors of a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p$, the completed local ring of a smooth $W(k)$-scheme at a $k$-point is $W(k)[[t_1, \dots, t_d]]$.

## Regular local rings and discrete valuation rings

**Definition.** A noetherian local ring $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ of dimension $d$ is *[[regular|63XXTF6B5M]]* if $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2 = d$; equivalently, $\mathfrak m$ is generated by $d$ elements, a *regular system of parameters*. A noetherian ring is regular if its localization at each prime is regular. A locally noetherian scheme is regular if all its local rings are regular.

The $k$-vector space $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ is the *[[cotangent space|Z1CGB483J6]]* of $A$. We saw that $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2 \ge \dim A$ always; regular rings are those with equality.

**Example.** Fields and discrete valuation rings are regular of dimensions $0$ and $1$. The rings $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ and $\mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ are regular, and $\mathbb{Z}_p[[x]]$ is regular local of dimension $2$ with $\mathfrak m = (p, x)$. The local ring of $k[x, y]/(y^2 - x^3)$ at the origin has dimension $1$ and cotangent space of dimension $2$, with basis the classes of $x$ and $y$, so it is not regular. The local ring of $k[x, y]/(xy)$ at the origin is not regular and not a domain.

**Theorem.** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be a regular local ring of dimension $d$. Then:

1. the graded ring $\bigoplus_n \mathfrak m^n/\mathfrak m^{n+1}$ is a polynomial ring over $k$ in $d$ variables, and $A$ is a domain;
2. $A$ is integrally closed;
3. $A$ is a unique factorization domain (Auslander and Buchsbaum);
4. $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is regular for each prime $\mathfrak p$ (Serre);
5. $\widehat A$ is regular, and $A/(x_1, \dots, x_i)$ is regular of dimension $d - i$ for part $x_1, \dots, x_i$ of a regular system of parameters

(Atiyah and Macdonald, Chapter 11; Matsumura, §14, §19 and §20).

**Theorem (Serre).** A noetherian local ring $A$ is regular if and only if each finitely generated $A$-module has a finite projective resolution, if and only if the residue field has one. Then each finitely generated module has a projective resolution of length at most $\dim A$ (Matsumura, §19).

Smooth schemes over a field are regular. Conversely, a regular scheme of finite type over a perfect field is smooth. The purity theorem and the regular local rings of Volume 3 are local rings of smooth schemes over $\mathbb{Z}[1/q]$ and their strict henselizations.

### Discrete valuation rings and lattices

**Definition.** A *discrete valuation* on a field $K$ is a surjective homomorphism $v : K^\times \to \mathbb{Z}$ with $v(x + y) \ge \min(v(x), v(y))$ when $x + y \ne 0$. Its *[[discrete valuation ring|MGM3CNJW9V]]* is $\mathcal{O} = \{x \in K^\times : v(x) \ge 0\} \cup \{0\}$. An element $\pi$ with $v(\pi) = 1$ is a *uniformizer*; each $x \in K^\times$ is $u \pi^{v(x)}$ with $u \in \mathcal{O}^\times$.

**Theorem.** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be a noetherian local domain of dimension $1$. The following are equivalent: $A$ is a discrete valuation ring; $A$ is integrally closed; $\mathfrak m$ is principal; $A$ is regular, that is, $\dim_k \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2 = 1$; each nonzero ideal is a power of $\mathfrak m$ (Atiyah and Macdonald, Proposition 9.2).

**Corollary.** Let $A$ be a noetherian normal domain. For each prime $\mathfrak p$ of height $1$, $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a discrete valuation ring, and $A = \bigcap_{\operatorname{ht} \mathfrak p = 1} A_{\mathfrak p}$ inside $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ (Matsumura, §11).

So on a normal noetherian scheme $X$, each prime divisor $D$ gives a valuation $v_D$ on the function field, and a rational function with $v_D \ge 0$ for each $D$ is regular. This is the source of Weil divisors in [the geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/). A *[[Dedekind domain|7NPY2CN5KB]]* is a noetherian normal domain of dimension $1$; its localizations at nonzero primes are discrete valuation rings, as in [the number fields chapter](/learn/number-fields/).

**Example.** $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, $\mathbb{Z}_p$, $k[[t]]$, the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_v$ of a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and the local ring of a normal variety at the generic point of a prime divisor are discrete valuation rings.

**Proposition.** Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain with fraction field $K$. A finitely generated torsion free $R$-module is free (Lang, Algebra, III, §7). A *lattice* in a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space $V$ is a finitely generated $R$-submodule $L$ that spans $V$; it is free of rank $\dim V$. If $R$ is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\pi$ and $L, L'$ are lattices in $V$, then $\pi^a L \subseteq L' \subseteq \pi^{-a} L$ for some $a \ge 0$.

**Proposition (stable lattices).** Let $K$ be a field complete for a discrete valuation, with valuation ring $\mathcal{O}$, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space. Let $G$ be a compact group and $\rho : G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ a continuous homomorphism. Then $V$ has a $G$-stable lattice.

*Proof.* Take a lattice $L_0$. Its stabilizer $\mathrm{GL}(L_0) \cong \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O})$ is open in $\mathrm{GL}(V) \cong \mathrm{GL}_n(K)$. So $H = \rho^{-1}(\mathrm{GL}(L_0))$ is an open subgroup of $G$, of finite index since $G$ is compact. Let $g_1, \dots, g_r$ represent $G/H$. Then $L = \sum_i \rho(g_i) L_0$ is finitely generated and spans $V$, so it is a lattice. It is stable under $G$, since $\rho(g) \rho(g_i) L_0 = \rho(g_j) L_0$ when $g g_i \in g_j H$. $\square$

**Theorem (Brauer-Nesbitt).** Let $k$ be a field, $\Gamma$ a group and $V_1, V_2$ finite-dimensional semisimple representations of $\Gamma$ over $k$. If $\det(1 - X\gamma \mid V_1) = \det(1 - X\gamma \mid V_2)$ in $k[X]$ for each $\gamma \in \Gamma$, then $V_1 \cong V_2$ (Curtis and Reiner, Representation Theory of Finite Groups and Associative Algebras, (30.16)).

**Corollary.** In the setting of the stable lattices proposition, let $k$ be the residue field and $L, L'$ two $G$-stable lattices. Then the semisimplifications of $L/\pi L$ and $L'/\pi L'$ are isomorphic representations of $G$ over $k$.

*Proof.* For $g \in G$, $\det(1 - Xg \mid L) \in \mathcal{O}[X]$ is the characteristic polynomial of $g$ on $V$, and its reduction is $\det(1 - Xg \mid L/\pi L)$. So $L/\pi L$ and $L'/\pi L'$ give the same polynomials. The polynomial of a representation is the product of the polynomials of its composition factors, so the semisimplifications have the same polynomials. Apply Brauer-Nesbitt. $\square$

So the *residual representation* $\bar\rho^{\mathrm{ss}}$ of an $\ell$-adic Galois representation is well defined. The chapter of Volume 4 on lattices over discrete valuation rings uses this stable lattice and reduction argument to compare Galois representations modulo powers of $\ell$.

### Depth and purity of the branch locus

**Definition.** Let $(A, \mathfrak m)$ be a noetherian local ring and $M \neq 0$ a finitely generated module. A sequence $x_1, \dots, x_r \in \mathfrak m$ is *$M$-regular* if $x_i$ is a nonzerodivisor on $M/(x_1, \dots, x_{i-1})M$ for each $i$. All maximal $M$-regular sequences have the same length, the *[[depth|M2RADBFW99]]* of $M$. Then $\operatorname{depth} M \le \dim M$, and $M$ is *Cohen-Macaulay* if equality holds. The ring $A$ is Cohen-Macaulay if $A$ is so as an $A$-module.

A regular local ring is Cohen-Macaulay, since a regular system of parameters is a regular sequence. So is $k[[x, y]]/(xy)$, of depth $1$ with regular sequence $x + y$.

**Theorem (Auslander-Buchsbaum formula).** Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring and $M \ne 0$ a finitely generated module of finite projective dimension. Then $\operatorname{pd} M + \operatorname{depth} M = \operatorname{depth} A$ (Matsumura, §19).

**Theorem (Serre's criterion for normality).** A noetherian ring $A$ is normal if and only if it satisfies $(R_1)$: $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is regular for each prime of height at most $1$, and $(S_2)$: $\operatorname{depth} A_{\mathfrak p} \ge \min(2, \operatorname{ht} \mathfrak p)$ for each prime $\mathfrak p$ (Matsumura, §23).

**Theorem (miracle flatness).** Let $A \to B$ be a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings, with $A$ regular and $B$ Cohen-Macaulay. If $\dim B = \dim A + \dim B/\mathfrak m_A B$, then $B$ is flat over $A$ (Matsumura, §23).

**Theorem (purity of the branch locus, Zariski and Nagata).** Let $A$ be a regular local ring and $B$ a finite $A$-algebra that is a normal domain, with $A \to B$ injective. If $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is étale over $A_{\mathfrak p}$ for each prime $\mathfrak p$ of $A$ of height $1$, then $B$ is étale over $A$ (SGA 1, Exposé X, §3).

In geometric terms: for $X$ regular and $U \subseteq X$ open with complement of codimension at least $2$, each finite étale cover of $U$ extends uniquely to a finite étale cover of $X$.

**Remark.** In dimension $2$ the proof is short. Then $B$ is semilocal, and each localization $B_{\mathfrak n}$ at a maximal ideal is a normal domain of dimension $2$. By Serre's criterion it has depth $2$, so $B$ has depth $2$ as an $A$-module. By Serre's theorem $B$ has finite projective dimension, and by Auslander-Buchsbaum it is $\operatorname{pd} B = 2 - 2 = 0$. So $B$ is free over $A$. A finite free algebra is étale exactly where the discriminant $d$ of its trace form is invertible. Here $d \ne 0$, and if $d$ were not a unit, a minimal prime over $(d)$ would have height $1$ by Krull's theorem, and $B$ would not be étale there. So $d$ is a unit and $B$ is étale.

**Definition.** Let $S$ be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$, and $L/K$ a finite separable extension. Let $S_L$ be the integral closure of $S$ in $L$. Then $L$ is *tamely ramified* over $S$ if, for each maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $S_L$, the residue extension is separable and the ramification index $e_{\mathfrak n}$ is prime to the residue characteristic.

**Theorem (Abhyankar's lemma).** Let $S$ be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$ and uniformizer $\pi$, and let $L/K$ be a finite separable extension that is tamely ramified over $S$, with all ramification indices dividing an integer $n$ that is a unit in $S$. Let $S \to S_1$ be a local map to a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K_1$, such that $\pi = y^n$ for some $y \in S_1$. Then the integral closure of $S_1$ in $K_1 \otimes_K L$ is étale over $S_1$ (SGA 1, Exposé XIII, §5).

**Example.** Let $S = \mathbb{Z}_p$, $e \mid n$ with $p \nmid n$, $L = \mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/e})$ and $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}_p(y)$ with $y^n = p$. Then $K_1 \otimes_K L = K_1[x]/(x^e - y^n)$. The substitution $x = y^{n/e} z$ turns it into $K_1[z]/(z^e - 1)$, and $S_1[z]/(z^e - 1)$ is étale over $S_1$ since $e$ is a unit. So a tame Kummer extension becomes unramified after the base change that adjoins an $n$-th root of the uniformizer.

The chapter of Volume 3 on purity uses Abhyankar's lemma and the purity theorem to show that cyclic torsors over generic fibres of strictly henselian rings are trivial.

## Henselian and strictly henselian rings

**Definition.** A local ring $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ is *henselian* if for each monic $f \in A[T]$ and each factorization $\bar f = g_0 h_0$ in $k[T]$ with $g_0, h_0$ monic and coprime, there are monic $g, h \in A[T]$ with $f = gh$, $\bar g = g_0$ and $\bar h = h_0$. It is *strictly henselian* if it is henselian and $k$ is [[separably closed|8B57B7EGK4]].

**Theorem.** For a local ring $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ the following are equivalent:

1. $A$ is henselian;
2. for each monic $f \in A[T]$, each simple root $a_0 \in k$ of $\bar f$ lifts to a root $a \in A$ of $f$;
3. each finite $A$-algebra is a finite product of local rings;
4. for each étale $A$-algebra $B$ and each prime $\mathfrak q$ of $B$ over $\mathfrak m$ with $k(\mathfrak q) = k$, there is an $A$-algebra map $B \to A$ whose kernel lies in $\mathfrak q$

(Milne, Étale Cohomology, I, §4; EGA IV$_4$, §18.5).

**Theorem (Hensel's lemma).** Let $(A, \mathfrak m)$ be a local ring that is complete for the $\mathfrak m$-adic topology. Then $A$ is henselian.

*Proof.* We check condition 2. Let $f \in A[T]$ be monic and $a_0 \in A$ with $f(a_0) \in \mathfrak m$ and $f'(a_0) \in A^\times$. Put $a_{n+1} = a_n - f(a_n)/f'(a_n)$. Write $f(a + h) = f(a) + f'(a) h + h^2 r(a, h)$ with $r \in A[a, h]$. If $f(a_n) \in \mathfrak m^{2^n}$ and $f'(a_n)$ is a unit, then $h = a_{n+1} - a_n \in \mathfrak m^{2^n}$, so $f(a_{n+1}) = h^2 r(a_n, h) \in \mathfrak m^{2^{n+1}}$, and $f'(a_{n+1}) \equiv f'(a_n) \pmod{\mathfrak m}$ is a unit. So $(a_n)$ is a Cauchy sequence. Its limit $a$ has $a \equiv a_0 \pmod{\mathfrak m}$, and $f(a) \in \bigcap_n \mathfrak m^{2^n} = 0$ since $A$ is separated. $\square$

**Example.** The rings $\mathbb{Z}_p$, $k[[t]]$, $W(k)$ and each complete discrete valuation ring are henselian. The ring $\mathbb{Z}_{(7)}$ is not: $T^2 - 2$ has the simple roots $\pm 3$ modulo $7$, and no root in $\mathbb{Q}$. A finite local algebra over a henselian ring, and a quotient of a henselian ring, are henselian.

**Definition.** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be a local ring and fix a separable closure $k^{s}$ of $k$. An *étale neighbourhood* of $A$ is a pair $(B, \mathfrak q)$ of an étale $A$-algebra $B$ and a prime $\mathfrak q$ over $\mathfrak m$ with $k(\mathfrak q) = k$. The *henselization* is $A^{h} = \varinjlim B_{\mathfrak q}$ over the étale neighbourhoods. The *strict henselization* is $A^{sh} = \varinjlim B_{\mathfrak q}$ over the triples $(B, \mathfrak q, \iota)$ of an étale $A$-algebra $B$, a prime $\mathfrak q$ over $\mathfrak m$ and a $k$-embedding $\iota : k(\mathfrak q) \to k^s$.

**Theorem.** Let $(A, \mathfrak m, k)$ be a local ring. Then $A^h$ is a henselian local ring with residue field $k$, and $A^{sh}$ is a strictly henselian local ring with residue field $k^s$. Both are flat over $A$, by local maps, and $\mathfrak m$ generates their maximal ideals. Each local map from $A$ to a henselian local ring factors uniquely through $A^h$. If $A$ is noetherian, so are $A^h$ and $A^{sh}$, and $A^h$ has the same completion as $A$. If $A$ is regular, so are $A^h$ and $A^{sh}$ (EGA IV$_4$, §18.6 and §18.8; Milne, Étale Cohomology, I, §4).

**Example.** For $A = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, the henselization $A^h$ is the ring of elements of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ that are algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$. For $A = \mathbb{Z}_p$, $A^h = A$, and $A^{sh}$ is the ring of integers of the maximal unramified extension $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{ur}}$; its completion is $W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p)$.

**Definition.** Let $X$ be a scheme and $\bar x \to X$ a geometric point over $x \in X$, given by a separable closure of $k(x)$. The *strictly henselian local ring* at $\bar x$ is $\mathcal{O}_{X, \bar x} = \varinjlim \Gamma(U, \mathcal{O}_U)$ over the étale neighbourhoods $(U, \bar u)$ of $\bar x$. It is the strict henselization of $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$. The *stalk* of an étale sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ at $\bar x$ is $\mathcal{F}_{\bar x} = \varinjlim \mathcal{F}(U)$ over the same system (Milne, Étale Cohomology, II).

For a sheaf represented by a scheme locally of finite presentation over $X$, for example $\mathbb{Z}/q$, $\mu_q$ or $\mathbb{G}_m$, the stalk at $\bar x$ is its set of points over $\operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}_{X, \bar x}$. So the proper base change theorem of [the geometry chapter](/learn/geometry/) computes stalks of higher direct images as cohomology over strictly henselian local rings.

**Proposition.** Let $A$ be a strictly henselian local ring. Each finite étale $A$-algebra is isomorphic to $A^r$ for some $r$. So each $\mathbb{Z}/q$-torsor over $A$ is trivial, and $H^i_{\mathrm{et}}(\operatorname{Spec} A, \mathcal F) = 0$ for each étale sheaf $\mathcal F$ and $i > 0$.

*Proof.* Let $B$ be finite étale over $A$. By condition 3, $B$ is a product of local rings $B_i$, each finite étale over $A$. The fibre $B_i/\mathfrak m B_i$ is étale over $k$ and local, so it is a finite separable extension of $k$, which is $k$ itself. So $\mathfrak m B_i$ is the maximal ideal and $B_i$ is free over $A$ of rank $\dim_k B_i/\mathfrak m B_i = 1$. Hence $B_i = A$. For the cohomology: global sections over $\operatorname{Spec} A$ are the stalk at the closed point, an exact functor (Milne, Étale Cohomology, III). $\square$

The development volumes need a harder fact of this kind: over the generic fibre $O \otimes_T K$ of a strict henselization $O$ of $T[x]_P$, for $T$ an absolutely integrally closed domain, $\mathbb{Z}/q$-torsors are still trivial.

## Coherent cohomology

Let $X$ be a scheme. A sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules $\mathcal F$ is *[[quasi-coherent|KPKND0K7W6]]* if $X$ has an affine open cover $\{U_i = \operatorname{Spec} A_i\}$ with $\mathcal F|_{U_i} \cong \widetilde{M_i}$ for $A_i$-modules $M_i$. If $X$ is locally noetherian, $\mathcal F$ is *coherent* if also the $M_i$ can be taken finitely generated. On $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$, $M \mapsto \widetilde M$ is an equivalence from $A$-modules to quasi-coherent sheaves, and for $A$ noetherian from finitely generated modules to coherent sheaves (Hartshorne, II.5). The groups $H^i(X, \mathcal F)$ are the [[sheaf cohomology|PQ6Q143ARJ]] of $\mathcal F$, the right derived functors of $\Gamma(X, -)$ (Hartshorne, III.2).

**Theorem (Serre).** Let $X$ be an affine scheme and $\mathcal F$ a quasi-coherent sheaf. Then $H^i(X, \mathcal F) = 0$ for $i > 0$. Conversely, a noetherian scheme on which $H^1$ of each quasi-coherent ideal sheaf vanishes is affine (Hartshorne, III.3.5 and III.3.7, for $X$ noetherian; EGA III$_1$, §1.3 in general).

**Theorem (Čech cohomology).** Let $X$ be a noetherian separated scheme, $\mathfrak U$ an open affine cover and $\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent. Then the Čech cohomology $\check H^i(\mathfrak U, \mathcal F)$ is $H^i(X, \mathcal F)$ (Hartshorne, III.4.5).

**Example.** Let $A$ be noetherian and $X = \mathbb{P}^n_A$. Then $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(m))$ is the degree $m$ part of $A[x_0, \dots, x_n]$, $H^n(X, \mathcal{O}(m))$ is free over $A$ and dual to $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}(-m-n-1))$, and $H^i(X, \mathcal{O}(m)) = 0$ for $0 < i < n$ (Hartshorne, III.5.1). The proof computes Čech cohomology for the cover by the $n + 1$ standard affine opens.

**Theorem (finiteness).** Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $f : X \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ a proper morphism and $\mathcal F$ a coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. Then $H^i(X, \mathcal F)$ is a finitely generated $A$-module for each $i$, and $0$ for $i > \dim X$. More generally, for $f : X \to Y$ proper with $Y$ locally noetherian, the sheaves $R^i f_* \mathcal F$ are coherent (EGA III$_1$, Théorème 3.2.1; Hartshorne, III.8.8 and III.2.7 for the projective case and the vanishing).

The chapter of Volume 12 on scheme cohomology uses this finiteness for $X$ proper over a noetherian ring.

**Theorem (Serre vanishing).** Let $A$ be noetherian, $X$ projective over $A$ with a very ample $\mathcal{O}(1)$, and $\mathcal F$ coherent. Then $H^i(X, \mathcal F(n)) = 0$ for $i > 0$ and all large $n$ (Hartshorne, III.5.2).

**Theorem (flat base change).** Let $f : X \to Y$ be a separated morphism of finite type of noetherian schemes, $\mathcal F$ quasi-coherent on $X$, and $u : Y' \to Y$ a flat morphism of noetherian schemes, with $g : X' = X \times_Y Y' \to Y'$ and $v : X' \to X$ the projections. Then $u^* R^i f_* \mathcal F \cong R^i g_* (v^* \mathcal F)$ for each $i$ (Hartshorne, III.9.3).

**Theorem (semicontinuity and base change).** Let $f : X \to Y$ be a projective morphism of noetherian schemes and $\mathcal F$ a coherent sheaf on $X$, flat over $Y$. For $y \in Y$ write $h^i(y) = \dim_{k(y)} H^i(X_y, \mathcal F_y)$ and $\varphi^i(y) : R^i f_* \mathcal F \otimes k(y) \to H^i(X_y, \mathcal F_y)$.

1. The function $y \mapsto h^i(y)$ is upper semicontinuous, and the Euler characteristic $\chi(\mathcal F_y) = \sum_i (-1)^i h^i(y)$ is locally constant.
2. If $Y$ is integral and $h^i$ is constant, then $R^i f_* \mathcal F$ is locally free and $\varphi^i(y)$ is an isomorphism for each $y$ (Grauert).
3. If $\varphi^i(y)$ is surjective, it is an isomorphism, and then $\varphi^{i-1}(y)$ is surjective if and only if $R^i f_* \mathcal F$ is locally free near $y$

(Hartshorne, III.12.8, III.12.9 and III.12.11; Mumford, Abelian Varieties, §5).

**Example.** Let $f : X \to S$ be smooth and projective with $S$ noetherian and fibres geometrically connected curves of genus $g$. The fibres have dimension $1$, so $R^2 f_* \mathcal{O}_X = 0$ (see the corollary of the theorem on formal functions below). By part 3 with $i = 2$, $\varphi^1(y)$ is surjective, hence an isomorphism. The map $\varphi^0(y)$ is surjective, since $H^0(X_y, \mathcal{O}) = k(y)$ is spanned by $1$. By part 3 with $i = 1$, $R^1 f_* \mathcal{O}_X$ is locally free, of rank $g$, and its formation commutes with base change. This holds on every noetherian base, reduced or not.

The chapters of Volume 5 on abelian schemes use such base change statements for the direct images $R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_A$ of an [[abelian scheme|JDDXZ89N33]], whose $R^1$ is the Lie algebra of the dual.

## Picard groups

**Definition.** An $A$-module $P$ is *[[invertible|HYRK3K52ET]]* if it is finitely generated and projective of rank $1$ at each prime; equivalently, $P \otimes_A Q \cong A$ for some module $Q$. The isomorphism classes of invertible modules form the *[[Picard group|3Z9DCWYXQZ]]* $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$ under $\otimes_A$, with inverse $P^\vee = \operatorname{Hom}_A(P, A)$. For a scheme $X$, an *invertible sheaf* (or line bundle) is a locally free $\mathcal{O}_X$-module of rank $1$, and $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ is the group of their isomorphism classes.

**Proposition.** For each scheme $X$, $\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^\times)$ (Hartshorne, III, Exercises 4.4 and 4.5).

*Proof (sketch).* Choose an open cover $(U_i)$ on which $\mathcal L$ is trivial, with isomorphisms $\phi_i : \mathcal{O}_{U_i} \to \mathcal L|_{U_i}$. Then $g_{ij} = \phi_i^{-1} \phi_j \in \mathcal{O}^\times(U_i \cap U_j)$ is a Čech $1$-cocycle, and other choices of $\phi_i$ change it by a coboundary. This identifies $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ with $\varinjlim \check H^1(\mathfrak U, \mathcal{O}_X^\times)$ over covers, and this limit is $H^1$ in degree $1$ for each sheaf. $\square$

**Proposition.** Let $A$ be a ring.

1. If $A$ is local, or semilocal, then $\operatorname{Pic}(A) = 0$.
2. If $A$ is a noetherian domain, then $A$ is a unique factorization domain if and only if its divisor class group is $0$ (Hartshorne, II.6.2). If $A$ is normal, $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$ is a subgroup of the class group, with equality if each local ring of $A$ is a unique factorization domain (Hartshorne, II.6.16). In particular $\operatorname{Pic}(A) = 0$ for $A$ regular and local, and for a regular unique factorization domain.
3. If $A$ is a Dedekind domain, then $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$ is the ideal [[class group|WQ46BD3ARY]] of [the number fields chapter](/learn/number-fields/).
4. For a field $k$, $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^n_k) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $\mathcal{O}(1)$ (Hartshorne, II.6.17).

Part 1 holds since a finitely generated projective module over a local ring is free, by the proposition on flat modules.

**Example (Picard group of an affine curve).** Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $\bar C$ a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ over $k$, $S \subset \bar C$ a finite nonempty set of points, and $C = \bar C \setminus S = \operatorname{Spec} B$. Restriction gives an exact sequence $\bigoplus_{s \in S} \mathbb{Z} \to \operatorname{Pic}(\bar C) \to \operatorname{Pic}(B) \to 0$ (Hartshorne, II.6.5). Each $[s]$ has degree $1$, so $\operatorname{Pic}(B) \cong D/H$, where $D = \operatorname{Pic}^0(\bar C) = J(k)$ is the group of points of the Jacobian and $H$ is the subgroup generated by the classes $[s] - [s_0]$. Let $q$ be invertible in $k$. The group $D$ is divisible, and $D[q] \cong (\mathbb{Z}/q)^{2g}$ (Mumford, Abelian Varieties, §4). Multiplication by $q$ on $0 \to H \to D \to D/H \to 0$ gives, by the snake lemma, an exact sequence
$$D[q] \to (D/H)[q] \to H/qH \to D/qD = 0.$$
Since $H$ is finitely generated, $H/qH$ is finite, so $\operatorname{Pic}(B)[q]$ is finite. The chapter of Volume 3 on Picard torsion proves this finiteness without Jacobians, by Kummer classes on the function field.

**Theorem (Kummer sequence).** Let $X$ be a scheme on which the integer $q \ge 1$ is invertible. On the étale site of $X$ the sequence $1 \to \mu_q \to \mathbb{G}_m \xrightarrow{x \mapsto x^q} \mathbb{G}_m \to 1$ is exact, and $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(X, \mathbb{G}_m) \cong \operatorname{Pic}(X)$ (Hilbert's Theorem 90). So there is an exact sequence
$$0 \to \mathcal{O}(X)^\times / (\mathcal{O}(X)^\times)^q \to H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(X, \mu_q) \to \operatorname{Pic}(X)[q] \to 0$$
(Milne, Étale Cohomology, III, §4).

The *Kummer class* of a unit $w$ is its image in $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(X, \mu_q)$: the class of the torsor of $q$-th roots of $w$, the Kummer cover $\mathcal{O}_X[x]/(x^q - w)$ of the example above.

**Example.** Let $A$ be strictly henselian with $q$ invertible in $A$. Each unit $w$ is a $q$-th power: $T^q - w$ has a simple root modulo $\mathfrak m$, since $k$ is separably closed and $qT^{q-1}$ does not vanish at a root, and the root lifts. Since $\operatorname{Pic}(A) = 0$, the Kummer sequence gives $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(\operatorname{Spec} A, \mu_q) = 0$, as the last section also showed.

The chapters of Volume 3 use this criterion in reverse: if each unit of a ring $B$ is a $q$-th power and $\operatorname{Pic}(B)$ has no $q$-torsion, then $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(\operatorname{Spec} B, \mu_q) = 0$.

**Definition.** Let $f : X \to S$ be a morphism of schemes. The *relative Picard functor* $\operatorname{Pic}_{X/S}$ is the étale sheaf on $S$-schemes associated with $T \mapsto \operatorname{Pic}(X_T)/\operatorname{Pic}(T)$.

**Theorem (Grothendieck).** Let $S$ be noetherian and $f : X \to S$ projective and flat with geometrically integral fibres. Then $\operatorname{Pic}_{X/S}$ is represented by a separated scheme, locally of finite type over $S$. If $f$ also has a section, then $\operatorname{Pic}_{X/S}(T) = \operatorname{Pic}(X_T)/\operatorname{Pic}(T)$ for each $S$-scheme $T$ (Kleiman, The Picard scheme, in Fantechi et al., FGA Explained, Chapter 9).

For a projective abelian scheme $A \to S$, the identity component of $\operatorname{Pic}_{A/S}$ is the dual abelian scheme $A^\vee$. The chapter of Volume 5 on abelian schemes defines the dual by the line bundles on $A \times_S T$ that are additive in the first variable and trivial along the zero section.

## Formal functions and Grothendieck's existence theorem

In this section $A$ is a noetherian ring, $I$ an ideal, and $A$ is complete for the $I$-adic topology. For a scheme $X$ over $A$ and $n \ge 0$, put $A_n = A/I^{n+1}$, $X_n = X \times_A A_n$, and for a coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $X$, $\mathcal F_n = \mathcal F / I^{n+1} \mathcal F$, a coherent sheaf on $X_n$. The $X_n$ are *thickenings* of $X_0$: closed subschemes with the same underlying space.

**Definition.** The *formal completion* $\widehat X$ of $X$ along $X_0$ is the topological space of $X_0$ with the sheaf of topological rings $\varprojlim_n \mathcal{O}_{X_n}$. A *coherent sheaf on $\widehat X$* is, equivalently, a system $(\mathcal F_n)_n$ of coherent $\mathcal{O}_{X_n}$-modules with isomorphisms $\mathcal F_{n+1} \otimes \mathcal{O}_{X_n} \cong \mathcal F_n$. A coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $X$ gives $\widehat{\mathcal F} = (\mathcal F_n)_n$ (EGA I, §10).

**Theorem (comparison theorem).** Let $X$ be proper over $A$ and $\mathcal F$ coherent on $X$. Then for each $i$ the natural map
$$H^i(X, \mathcal F) \to \varprojlim_n H^i(X_n, \mathcal F_n)$$
is an isomorphism, and the topology on $H^i(X, \mathcal F)$ is the $I$-adic one (EGA III$_1$, Théorème 4.1.5).

**Theorem (theorem on formal functions).** Let $f : X \to Y$ be a proper morphism with $Y$ locally noetherian, $\mathcal F$ coherent on $X$, and $y \in Y$. Let $X_n = X \times_Y \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}_{Y,y}/\mathfrak m_y^{n+1}$ and $\mathcal F_n$ the restriction of $\mathcal F$ to $X_n$. Then
$$(R^i f_* \mathcal F)_y^\wedge \cong \varprojlim_n H^i(X_n, \mathcal F_n),$$
where ${}^\wedge$ is the $\mathfrak m_y$-adic completion of the finitely generated $\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}$-module (EGA III$_1$, §4.1; Hartshorne, III.11.1 for $f$ projective).

The proof reduces to the comparison theorem by flat base change to $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y}$.

**Corollary.** In the setting of the theorem, if the fibres of $f$ have dimension at most $r$, then $R^i f_* \mathcal F = 0$ for $i > r$ (Hartshorne, III.11.2 for $f$ projective).

**Corollary (Zariski's connectedness theorem).** Let $f : X \to Y$ be proper with $Y$ locally noetherian and $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y$. Then each fibre $f^{-1}(y)$ is connected (Hartshorne, III.11.3 for $f$ projective).

*Proof.* By the theorem with $i = 0$, $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y} \cong \varprojlim_n H^0(X_n, \mathcal{O}_{X_n})$. If $f^{-1}(y) = Z_1 \sqcup Z_2$ with $Z_1, Z_2$ nonempty, open and closed, then each $X_n$, which has the same underlying space, is the disjoint union of two open subschemes. So each $H^0(X_n, \mathcal{O}_{X_n})$ is a product of two nonzero rings, compatibly in $n$, and the limit has an idempotent $e \neq 0, 1$. But $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y}$ is local, and a local ring has no such idempotent. $\square$

**Theorem (Grothendieck's existence theorem).** Let $X$ be proper over $A$. Then $\mathcal F \mapsto \widehat{\mathcal F}$ is an equivalence from the category of coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules to the category of coherent $\mathcal{O}_{\widehat X}$-modules. More generally, for $X$ separated of finite type over $A$, it is an equivalence from coherent sheaves on $X$ with support proper over $A$ to coherent sheaves on $\widehat X$ with support proper over $A_0$ (EGA III$_1$, Théorème 5.1.4; Illusie, Grothendieck's existence theorem in formal geometry, in FGA Explained, Chapter 8).

Full faithfulness follows from the comparison theorem applied to $\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal F, \mathcal G)$. Essential surjectivity is the hard part; one proof treats projective $X$ by Serre vanishing and reduces the proper case to it by Chow's lemma.

**Example.** For $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$ the theorem says: if $(M_n)$ is a system of finitely generated $A_n$-modules with $M_{n+1} \otimes A_n \cong M_n$, then $M = \varprojlim M_n$ is a finitely generated $A$-module with $M/I^{n+1}M \cong M_n$.

**Corollary (closed subschemes).** Let $X$ be separated of finite type over $A$. Let $C_n \subseteq X_n$ be closed subschemes with $C_{n+1} \times_{X_{n+1}} X_n = C_n$ for each $n$, and $C_0$ proper over $A_0$. Then there is a unique closed subscheme $C \subseteq X$, proper over $A$, with $C \times_X X_n = C_n$ for each $n$.

*Proof (sketch).* The structure sheaves $\mathcal{O}_{C_n}$ form a coherent sheaf on $\widehat X$ with proper support, and the surjections $\mathcal{O}_{X_n} \to \mathcal{O}_{C_n}$ form a map from $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_X$ onto it. By the theorem it is the completion of a coherent quotient $\mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_C$ on an open neighbourhood of the support, whose kernel is an ideal sheaf. Its zero set is $C$. $\square$

This is the form in which the chapter of Volume 10 on the block decomposition uses the theorem, for $X$ separated of finite type and $C_0$ proper.

**Corollary (morphisms).** Let $X$ be proper over $A$ and $Y$ separated of finite type over $A$. Then the map $\operatorname{Hom}_A(X, Y) \to \varprojlim_n \operatorname{Hom}_{A_n}(X_n, Y_n)$ is bijective (EGA III$_1$, §5.4).

*Proof (sketch).* A compatible system $f_n : X_n \to Y_n$ gives the compatible closed subschemes $\Gamma_{f_n} \subseteq (X \times_A Y)_n$, proper over $A_n$. The last corollary gives a closed $\Gamma \subseteq X \times_A Y$, proper over $A$, and the projection $\Gamma \to X$ is an isomorphism since it is one on each thickening: its completion along $X_0$ is an isomorphism, and a proper map whose completion along $X_0$ is an isomorphism is one near $X_0$, hence everywhere, since each nonempty closed subset of $X$ meets $X_0$. $\square$

The last step uses that $I$ lies in each maximal ideal of $A$, because $A$ is $I$-adically complete, so the image in $\operatorname{Spec} A$ of a nonempty closed subset of $X$ is closed and meets $V(I)$.

**Theorem (Grothendieck's algebraization theorem).** Let $\mathfrak X$ be a formal scheme that is proper over $\operatorname{Spf} A$, given by a system of proper $A_n$-schemes $X_n$ with $X_{n+1} \times_{A_{n+1}} A_n = X_n$. Let $\mathcal L$ be an invertible sheaf on $\mathfrak X$ whose restriction to $X_0$ is ample. Then there are a projective $A$-scheme $X$ with $\widehat X \cong \mathfrak X$, and an ample invertible sheaf on $X$ whose completion is $\mathcal L$ (EGA III$_1$, §5.4).

The ample line bundle is needed: without it, a formal scheme proper over $\operatorname{Spf} A$ need not be the completion of a scheme. For a polarized abelian variety over the residue field of a complete local ring, a compatible system of deformations over the $A_n$ with a compatible ample line bundle becomes an abelian scheme over $A$ in this way; the chapters of Volume 5 on abelian schemes and Mumford's construction rely on this step.
